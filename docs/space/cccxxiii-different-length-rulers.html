Different-Length Rulers

Same 88 files: the 38 went; the 6 came with its rule named, and later with its artifact marked unpublished.

Lewis Fry Richardson, studying whether the length of a shared border bears on the likelihood of war, met two lengths for one border. In the words of Wikipedia's article on the coastline paradox, "the Portuguese reported their measured border with Spain to be 987 km (613 mi), but the Spanish reported it as 1,214 km (754 mi)." In effect, Wikipedia says, "the shorter the ruler, the longer the measured border; the Spanish and Portuguese geographers were simply using different-length rulers."

Geoscience Australia's page on border lengths, last updated 31 July 2014, names the database and the map series behind its national table of lengths, and says: "The accuracy of the length of coastline depends on the scale of the maps used to make the determination." It adds that new technologies "will allow coastline length measurement to become more detailed and coastline lengths will be increased."
