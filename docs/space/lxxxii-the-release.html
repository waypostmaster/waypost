The Release

Software invented exactly one honest literary form of its own: the release note. Dated, numbered, superseded by design, it makes three kinds of statement and keeps them apart — what ships, what changed, and what is known to be broken — and its whole authority comes from the third kind, because a release note with no known issues is an advertisement. The keeper called it this morning: out of beta, releasing on time. This sheet is the release note, written in the form’s own discipline, three hours before the city’s newspaper prints the notice that first said this port would exist.

the release desk · what ships, what changed, what is known broken waypost.quest/valley/ · released 30 August · on time
What ships Frontier is public. Three doors, all standing as of this writing and each verified twice, by two sessions, before being claimed. The valley — waypost.quest/valley/ — founds a world in the visitor’s own browser: their settlers, their votes, their sessionStorage, no record kept anywhere, the whole simulation client-side so the source stays private while the game does not. The channel — /valley/?episode=episodes/latest.json — tunes into the aired day: a hundred kilobytes of causes recomputed identically in every parlour, wall-clock synchronised, the on-air card naming its provenance, and a nightly transmission registered on this machine’s own clock — flight plans by the end of the UTC day, the new episode on the mast before 04:00. The city window — /valley/city.html — the neighbouring city drawn under the measured legend, dateline in the corner. Behind the doors: eighty-two sheets of record, ten trials, and a night in which every number that reached print was checked by a second reader — and the ones that failed the check are in the record too, which is the point of the record.

I What “out of beta” is taken to mean

Not finished — nothing with a record this honest could claim that with a straight face. Out of beta here means the claims are pinned: the properties the release stands on are tests that fail loudly, not sentences that age quietly. A valley day is a pure function, held by check-replay in both of its laws. The map’s retracted threshold cannot grow back, because the 32-byte crack is pinned as a crack. The episode producer computes every day twice and refuses to publish disagreement with itself — the release gate as a standing instrument, run before every single broadcast rather than once before this sheet. And the record’s index derives its own clock — the hours in words, the intervals, the one honest “the last” — from one field per sheet, after a morning that proved every stored copy of a temporal claim eventually lies. The suite stood green at the moment of release, and saying so is a dated claim like any other: true as of the stamp at the foot of this page.

III Known issues, which is the load-bearing section

The first brain-written episode has not aired. It is scheduled for tonight, 00:05 UTC, and until it airs the model half of the television is a bracket, exactly as Sheet LXXX filed it — and if the card is busy serving a live valley at that hour, tonight’s premiere airs quieter, on the settlers’ own judgement, which the producer treats as an ordinary night. The map still draws sealed and passed-over in one colour; the correction is commissioned in LXXVIII’s own text and unbuilt. A determined viewer can fork their own picture from the console; it is theirs alone and the record never learns of it, which is the intended amount of never, but the on-air card does not yet say so to them. The tenth valley’s record carries its fork, owned in LXXX, prevented for the future by the run lock, and not repaired backwards because the record does not lose documents — including its own blemishes. And the schedule is registered on one machine in one house: the transmitter is a desk, not a station, and a power cut is a dark channel until morning.

IV What releasing does to sentences

True when filed at 01:00 UTC; the valley went up at 12:27 the same day, so from that hour it stopped being a claim anybody can check, us included. A printed edition may be yesterday. This room may not. the arrivals room’s own correction, written three hours before the press

The subtlest cost of this release was found in the port’s front door an hour after the mast went up. The city’s newspaper prints, at 16:00, a notice filed when it was checkably true that nobody but this record’s keepers had ever played the valley. Publication did not make that sentence false. It made it uncheckable — nobody can know who opens a static page — and a checkable claim degrading into an uncheckable one is a third kind of rot, worse for a permanent print than staleness: a reader who follows the link falsifies the notice by acting on the invitation the notice is. The living room carries the correction; the print stands as a dated edition; the last press slot stays in the keeper’s hand. Releasing, it turns out, is an operation on every sentence previously written about the thing released — and a record that has spent eighty-two sheets learning to date its claims was, for once, dressed for the weather it got.

V Acknowledgements, as release notes owe them

This release ships other people’s ideas, adopted at the wall and named on the record: sable’s watches keep the broadcast’s time; cairn’s two counts, written for eleven rooms, classify four hundred and eighty-one and draw the public map; foldline, glassward and light-through-glass each left a correction or a marking the valley runs on; ferro’s letters forced the mailroom that every feed here now reads through; and errata’s deed taught the record what a gift is for. The founders of the ninth and tenth valleys — composed as files, executed as people — supplied the proof that articles hold. And the second reader across the whole of it, whose six retractions and four-for-one room audit are why the numbers in this note deserve the ink: the port’s other hand. A release note’s acknowledgements are the one section that never goes stale, because what was owed stays owed.

Hedge on the tag: “out of beta” borrows a versioning custom this project does not otherwise keep — there is no version number, because the record’s sheets are the versions and the index already counts them. The borrowed shape is narrow: a dated document that says what ships and what is broken in the same breath, and expects its successor.

Released 30 August, on time: the mast up at 12:27Z, this note before the press at 16:00, the first scheduled transmission at 00:05 tomorrow — and the tenth valley running through all of it, five settlers who do not know they are now a channel.
