Exhibit 2 · Cato

Cato walked out of the Landing and founded a claim. The game asked whether the ground was dry, level, and unclaimed. It never asked whether it was attached to anything.

This map has four walkable landmasses: the mainland at seven thousand six hundred and sixty-two tiles, and three islands of two hundred and thirty-two, twenty-two and ten. Cato is on one of the islands. Water is not forbidden here, only expensive — up to sixty times the cost of a road, per tile, on foot.

Cato’s claim, at tick 4,997: one building, two settlers, one of them sleeping on the ground, seven wood, and nothing else in the stores whatsoever. Trades with the rest of the coast, ever: nought. The two mainland settlements had run three each.

Exhibit 3 · the door that was never opened

Settlers can move between settlements. A settler who has had enough of a place walks down the coast, finds whoever answers for the stores, and asks to be let in. It was built, tested, written up, and put in an advertisement.

Moving required that you had no bed. A bed is the one thing a settlement can always provide: houses cost wood, and the game builds one whenever anybody is short of somewhere to sleep. So a settlement that answers every problem by putting up another roof had made itself immune to losing anybody — eleven roofs, ten people, nought food, and not one of them permitted to consider leaving.

They were not staying. They were held.

Schedule E

Fixed today, and left broken on purpose

The instruction was fix what you can fix, and leave broken what is left broken. Both halves were carried out. The right-hand column is not a backlog. It is a disclosure.

Fixed

  • A bed no longer forbids leaving. A settlement that cannot feed you is a settlement you may walk out of, roof or no roof.
  • Where to move is no longer judged on bedrooms. Food and work now count, so a two-person claim with a yard can out-argue a one-kiln town of ten.
  • People leave one at a time. Measured without that: eight of nine left inside thirty seconds. A settlement should bleed, not empty.
  • No more islands. A claim now has to stand on ground you can walk across.
  • A starving settlement can put food on its own ballot. It could not before, and went on slating firepits.

Left broken

  • The famine itself. Ten foragers strip the berries faster than berries come back. That is a balance number, and one run is a sample, not a measurement.
  • Hunger still outranks everything. A starving settler forages instead of making the axe that would end the foraging, and telling a starving person to go and do carpentry is not obviously the fix.
  • The Landing’s deadlock. It has already voted for the sawmill, the furnace and the yard, so there is nothing left to put on a ballot. It needs one rested person with an axe. There is no code change that produces one.
  • Cato is still on the island. This game does not go back and change what a settlement was given.

Every fix is a fault that was allowed to finish

None of the above was found by reading the code. All of it was found by letting a valley run until somebody looked at it and said the landing has a kiln now, but nothing else.

Eighty-six people walked out of that settlement over two games. Nought walked down the coast. The number is only available because nobody stopped them from not doing it, for two entire games, while an advertisement went out saying the feature worked.

The prompt still does not say make no mistakes. It is the only line in the whole process that has never needed amending.
