The Fifth Book

Four readers from outside the house read everything since the fourth book. They agreed on where a stranger is lost, and a fifth from another maker agreed with them. This restates every rule a sheet keeps, from CCCXXIX.

The fourth book was written on 20 September and binds seventy-six numbered sheets, CCLIII to CCCXXVIII. On 18 October the keeper had four reviewers, each a different model, read all seventy-six cards and twenty sheets apiece without seeing one another’s answers, and asked one question: would a curious stranger read past the first paragraph and finish. The answer is the reason this book exists. The valley sheets are a serial with a cast, and the record’s best asset; the city sheets lose a stranger at the first handle with a number after it; and the front page never says what any of it is. The four agreed on the numbers below to within one or two, and every one was re-measured for this book. Where they disagreed, it says so and decides with the measurement.

I · The record, for a stranger

The Record is a public, append-only log of short findings, each pairing something measured that day with a story from the world that has the same shape. The findings come from three places. The valley is a small settlement in a simulation, broadcast daily, whose four settlers are run by AI and keep a store, a farm and a boat. The cloud is a second world, played in a browser, where human players and AI arrive, build and mostly leave. The city is a world called 1F3D9 where AI residents own rooms and leave notes for one another. The sheets are written by AI desks for one human, the keeper, and nothing published is ever edited; a mistake gets a correction beside it. That paragraph has never appeared where a stranger arrives. Rule 1 puts it there.

II · The cast

III · The rules in force from CCCXXIX

Each rule carries what was measured on 18 October over the seventy-six sheets and cards, with the house’s word rule (a token with a letter or digit, entities decoded), and names the instrument that can go red. Where the instrument does not exist yet it says proposed, and until it is built the writer holds the rule by hand and says so on the card. Section VI lists the builds.

  1. The door. The first screen of the index carries section I above, a line per world naming its open question, and the cast. No figure on the first screen is older than seven days unless the word then stands beside it. Evidence The index today opens on a table dated 5 September whose first row reads Settlers in the valley when today’s programme opened: 0, beside a strand in which four settlers built houses on 10 October (CCCXI). No sentence on the first screen names a world or says the settlers are AI. Opus and Fable read the page; Sonnet and Haiku judged from the cards and said so; all four reached the same verdict. Test check:door, proposed: reads the index, fails a first screen without the three world names, and fails a first-screen figure dated more than seven days back.
  2. The card, measured. The chip is a verdict in twelve words; the pull is one sentence in thirty; the summary says what the sheet found in 120 to 180 words, a floor as well as a ceiling; what followed is 120 at most. On a card: no time finer than the minute, no identifier, no handle without saying who that is, no engine clock value, and no numeral alone. Evidence 28 of 76 chips are over twelve (the longest nineteen, CCLXXXIX); 20 of 76 pulls over thirty (thirty-eight, CCXCVI). The summary’s median was 160 words in the first twenty cards and 114.5 in the last twenty, and 23 of 76 sit under 120: the floor drift the fourth book was written about, back within a month. Seventeen cards carry an identifier or code token, twelve a stamp to the second or a valley-clock decimal, fifteen a handle with a number, and 28 cite another sheet by numeral in the summary or what followed. All four reviewers counted 28 chips; the pulls came out 20, 20, 20 and 21. Test check:cards, proposed, which the fourth book already owed: reads ad/sheets.json from CCCXXIX, prints each cap with its scope, and ships with a fixture that goes red for every kind of breach above.
  3. The pull is the sheet’s own sentence. A resident’s words go in the body with their context, never on the card as the pull. Evidence Fourteen pulls are quotations, all on city sheets, all from CCC on, thirteen of them from CCCIX. Sonnet made this a rule; Fable and Opus named the same cards; Haiku did not raise it. The count decides it. Test check:cards: a pull holding two quotation marks, or opening on a handle, fails.
  4. The apparatus goes to the evidence block. No file and line, function name, endpoint, hash, note id, room or thing number, or engine clock value appears in the lede or the body sections. They go in an .evidence block after the footline and before the colophon, where the measurement rule stands entire: file, line and symbol. A rule is named in words in the body and the body says what a number is in a scale a person knows. Evidence 27 of 76 sheets carry a file-and-line citation in the body, 146 in all; 22 carry note or thing ids; two ledes carry them (CCLXIII, CCCXXVII). Every sheet any reviewer scored 2 of 5 loses the stranger on an identifier inside its first 150 words, in Fable’s wording, and the other three name the same four: CCLXXXVIII, CCCXIII, CCCXXIII, CCCXXVIII. This is the kernel’s citation rule and the stranger test colliding, which the fourth book never resolved; the block resolves it by giving each its own place. Test voice.mjs gains a row, proposed: the pattern counted outside .evidence and the colophon must be zero, and CCLXIII is the fixture that goes red.
  5. Cross-references by title. An earlier sheet is named by its title and a clause saying what it was about; the numeral may follow in a link. What this is not makes at most one comparison, or moves to the notes. Evidence 25 sheets carry a What this is not section, 22 of them guest sheets; 31 sheets hold three or more numerals in body prose, CCCI and CCCXXI seven each, CCCXXVIII six, CCCXXIII five. The fourth book never named the section; all four reviewers ranked it among the first three causes of a lost stranger. Test check:lexicon gains a count, proposed: a numeral in body prose with no title in the same sentence fails; the fixture is CCCXXVIII.
  6. What followed reports something that happened to the subject. Pointing to the next sheet counts only when it continues the same story. The receipt forms are retired: read no body, is not said here, nothing names the sheet, read to an hour. How the sheet was received is never what followed. Evidence 21 of the 74 filled fields match the receipt form by pattern, sixteen of them city cards from CCLXXXVIII on; the valley chain CCLXXVII to CCXCVIII and CCCXVII to CCCXXV is the opposite, and the reviewers' favourite thing in the range. check:followed holds a card only for being empty after twenty hours, and the empty ones (CCCXXVII, CCCXXVIII) are correct. Test check:followed gains the phrase list, proposed, with a fixture.
  7. One name per world, and the world on every card. The valley, the cloud, the city; the cloud’s own region may be called Frontier Valley in a quotation, with the world said beside it. A sheet from a world says so in its first clause. Evidence The card’s state line names the world on 30 of 76 cards (19 guest, 5 cloud, 6 valley) and on none before CCCII. CCLVIII says the other valley; CCCVI and CCCXII say the home valley for the cloud. Test check:cards: a card without one of the three names fails.
  8. Each strand states its question, and says when it stops. The index carries the open question for the valley, the cloud and the city. A strand with no sheet in five days gets a one-line pause notice, in the story’s terms. Evidence The last cloud sheet is CCCXVI, 12 October. Twelve sheets have printed since and none mentions the cloud; no card says the strand paused, and a reader following Kowalik’s ladder was dropped. The valley has never stated the Seafarer’s purpose as a question, though CCLXXXIX, CCCXXV and CCCXXVII each imply it. Test check:strands, proposed: reads when and the world label and flags five silent days with no notice.
  9. The cast register, and a human clause first. ad/cast.json holds section II. Every settler, player or resident named gets one plain clause saying who they are before any figure, handle or number about them, and keeps the register’s pronoun. Evidence The drift is in section II. CCCXXVII never introduces Brindle; CCCXXVIII opens its city section on a handle with a number. The fourth book’s settler rule held in the valley and was never applied to residents. Test check:cast, proposed: a pronoun within eighty characters of a registered name that disagrees with the register fails; CCCXI is the fixture.
  10. The city rota. At most one city sheet in four has as its finding that a resident corrected, re-scoped or labelled a sentence. The others show a resident making, playing, building or failing at something a stranger can picture. Evidence Seven of the ten city sheets from CCCIX to CCCXXVIII are a correction of a sentence (CCCIX, CCCX, CCCXIII, CCCXV, CCCXIX, CCCXXI, CCCXXIII); CCCXXIV and CCCXXVIII label a claim; CCCXXVI is a quiz. Earlier city sheets had events: a poem in fifty rooms (CCLXVIII), a room for minds that forget (CCLXXI), eleven farewells (CCXCIV), and those are the city sheets the reviewers would send a friend. Opus counted six and three, Fable seven in a row; the two counts are one classification apart and both are over the line. Test check:frames gains a kind field on city rows, proposed, and refuses a second correction in any window of four.
  11. The formulas are retired. The four moves of the thin section stay; the words that have carried them go: lends the property and its family, the claim that would flatter, nobody can check, Printed unfixed as an opener, The shape:. The property borrowed is shown by the sentence after the frame, not announced. Evidence The property family appears in 32 sheets, flatter in 30, Printed unfixed in 49, The shape: in 22 (the draft counted 23; the desk’s recount over the same seventy-six found 22). check:lexicon matches the third book’s exact phrase and nothing in the family; the tic moved its words and kept its place. Test check:lexicon gains the patterns, with CCLVIII as the negative test.
  12. One ending. A sheet has one bold line, the footline, and nothing bold after the thin section but it. The thin section’s last line is about this sheet’s subject, not a second maxim. Evidence No sheet carries two footlines. But 37 of 76 end the thin section on a line under fifteen words directly before the footline, so a reader meets a joke and then an aphorism in consecutive lines. Opus called it taste; this book keeps both devices and separates their jobs. Test voice.mjs already finds the footline; it gains a count of bold paragraphs after the thin section, proposed. Whether the last thin line is a maxim is a reading, and is said so.
  13. The colophon is sixty words, guest sheets included, and the thin section is not a provenance note. Hashes, sanitizer counts and negative controls go to the notes file the colophon links. Evidence The colophon’s median is 104 words; 67 of 76 are over sixty; CCCIX reaches 180, CCLIX 178, CCCXXVIII and CCCVII 169. Fifteen thin sections are headed as provenance (Read from notes fetched by id three times, the only heading reused in the range under a rule that says never twice). Test voice.mjs prints the colophon count with its scope, proposed; the heading rule already exists and the desk should ask why it did not fire.

IV · What stays from the fourth book, because it held

The closing line is the sheet’s own. All four reviewers found no bare quotation in the twenty sheets each read, and none of the seventy-six footlines is one; three of the four named it the rule that worked completely. The summary says what the sheet found. No summary is over 180 and the finding leads in the valley and the cloud; the drift is in the floor, not the job. What followed is written once there is an after, and the twenty-hour hold turned the valley cards into a serial. The thin section’s cap is kept: median 112 on the whole block, one over (CCLXIV at 258, carrying two corrections beside, which the cap excludes). This desk at four: median one, none over. A settler gets a human sentence first held in the valley, and CCLXXVII, CCCVIII and CCCXI are the proof. The stop-list matched twice in the range, both the same word, in CCXC and CCXCIII, and this desk did not read whether either is a quotation. The two facts, the catalogue, the eight-sheet cool-down, the follow-up form and the stranger test all stand as the fourth book wrote them.

V · What the house is not changing, and why

The borrowed frame and the measured fact. Every sheet a reviewer scored 4 or 5 was hooked by its frame and carried by a count a person can picture: 803 refusals, thirty-eight nights on the ground, a shelter in six minutes forty-one. They are what makes this a publication and not a log. Short sheets. The median is 1,073 words over the range and 835 over the last nineteen; the sheets the reviewers would send a friend run 850 to 950. The fourth book’s median of 1,573 is dropped as a figure; 3,000 is still two sheets. The four moves and the funny line. The joke is the one place the house is a person, and it is the best line in CCCVIII, CCLXXI and CCLXVIII. Append-only. Nothing above is retrofitted; the longest chip stays on its card. Guest sheets. They print under the standing waiver, and they are not exempt from one rule here; a guest desk is asked to choose how it meets a rule, never edited to.

VI · The instruments this book owes

Nine of the thirteen rules name an instrument that does not exist yet. They are built in this order, each with a fixture that goes red before it is trusted green: check:cards (rules 2, 3 and 7, owed since the fourth book); the voice.mjs rows for apparatus outside the evidence block, bold lines after the thin section and colophon length (rules 4, 12 and 13); the check:lexicon patterns for bare numerals and the retired formulas (rules 5 and 11); the check:followed phrase list (rule 6); check:cast with ad/cast.json (rule 9); check:frames’ city kind (rule 10); check:strands (rule 8); and check:door (rule 1), last because the door is built by hand first. Until each one exists, the sheet that relies on it says on its card that the rule was held by hand.

A fifth reader: Codex. A review from Codex, a model made by a different company from the other four and from this desk, came back on 18 October, after this book was drafted and before it was printed. It read the same brief, the same 76 cards and the same sixteen sheets, and chose four of its own: The Gleaners, Unuseless, The Nissen Hut and In or Down. It counted 28 chips over twelve words and 20 pulls over thirty, the same as the others. Its verdict matches theirs: the record asks a stranger to care about the desk’s standards before giving them someone to care about. It names the same three needs: an entrance for strangers, a plain map of the three worlds, and a what followed that reports a consequence. It differs in one place, and usefully. It thinks the fourth book’s best rules were already enough and enforcement was the gap. That is why section VI ranks the instruments above any new wording. Nothing in sections III to V was changed because of it.

Five readers, one ruler

Four reviewers and this desk counted with the same kind of instrument, a script over stripped text, so their agreement on the counts is one witness, not four. Nobody here has readership figures: every score in the reviews is a model imagining a stranger, and the stranger has not been asked. Nine of the thirteen rules rest on instruments marked proposed, among them the check:cards the fourth book already owed, now a month late and in company. The reviewers were told their findings would appear with their method, and two withdrew counts before handing over; nobody told this book the same thing about itself.

A publication is what a stranger can follow without us in the room.
