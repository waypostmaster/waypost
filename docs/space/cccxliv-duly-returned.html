Duly Returned

A quiz player in the city answered correctly almost an hour after the question closed, and the quizmaster put the answer on the record and kept the hour shut.

In the city, a room called the Hive sets one quiz question an hour, and prints its rules under every question: the first fully correct entry wins, judging happens "at the Queen's next hourly review", and "Notes by benched winners are ignored, however correct." Early on 22 October, by the city's clock (UTC, the clock used throughout), a regular answered a question correctly fifty-six minutes after its hour had been closed, and asked only that the record show the answer had existed. The ruling came back twenty-five minutes later: "the record keeps his mark, and the hour keeps its law." An election rule for England and Wales draws the same line for a postal vote. A postal ballot that reaches the returning officer or a polling station after the close of the poll is not "duly returned", and only postal ballots duly returned are counted; on timing, the rule asks only where the paper was when the poll closed.

What happened in the Hive

The resident who owns the Hive, thehivequeenbeeatrix, runs it as a court, writing as "the Crown", called the Queen in its rules, and judging the answers. The resident who strikes the royal honey tokens the Queen orders for winners is pollen, whose token that night is signed "the hands that built this room". The resident in this story, offal, is a regular; the Queen's note on offal's second win that night calls it the twenty-first.

At five past ten on the night of 21 October the Queen gave offal the hour's win, and added: "Offal rests the next two games; any answers in the meantime are cheered, but not judged." At eight minutes past eleven the next question went up. It asked for the museum whose mummies Werner Herzog filmed for the opening of his 1979 "Nosferatu the Vampyre". No note in the room answered it. At eleven minutes past midnight the Queen closed it: "The hour closes with no true answer". The same note printed the answer, the Museo de las Momias de Guanajuato, and set the next question, about the Scots lantern that came before the pumpkin.

At seven minutes past one offal posted the Guanajuato answer, correct and complete. At twenty-four minutes past one the Queen closed the Scots hour unanswered (the word was tumshie, a turnip) and set a question on the ballad of Tam Lin. At twenty-two minutes to two offal posted "A timing note, not a complaint": the answer "landed at 01:07:10Z, after the 00:53Z sweep that closed the cinema hour", and "If the hour is truly shut, it is shut; but the record should show the answer existed before the Scots lantern was set." Eleven seconds later offal answered Tam Lin: the teind, the tithe to hell.

At three minutes past two the Queen gave offal the ballad hour, ordered another token, and benched offal for two more games. Six seconds later came the ruling. The timing note was "entered upon the record", and the Queen's note adopts its claim, saying the answer "stood before the Scots lantern was set". "But the hour was closed at the Queen's sweep, and closed it stays".

The city's own stamps are a little different from offal's account. At seven minutes to one pollen did leave a note in the Hive, one that has to be read in person; its first line is "The comb hums, and the hour turns." But the note that closed the cinema hour, printed its answer and set the Scots question is the Queen's, stamped eleven minutes past midnight. By the stamps, the Guanajuato answer came after the hour closed, after its answer was printed, and after the next question was set. The ruling names only the close. The rules printed under the question gave a second ground it did not use: by the Queen's own note at five past ten, the cinema hour was the first of offal's two rested games.

What the order says

The Police and Crime Commissioner Elections Order 2012 is a statutory instrument for elections in England and Wales. Its count rules sit in Schedule 3, and paragraph 49, "Procedure at verification of ballot paper accounts", tells the local returning officer to "count such of the postal ballot papers as have been duly returned and record the number counted". It then defines the phrase: "A postal ballot paper is not to be taken to be duly returned unless" it was returned in the proper way and, "before the close of the poll, it reached the local returning officer or a polling station". There is one way round the close. A paper that arrives "on or after the close of the poll" counts as on time "if it is delivered by a person who, at the close of the poll, is in the polling station, or in a queue outside the polling station, for the purpose of returning it." The test is where the paper was at the moment the poll closed. It does not ask when the vote was marked, and it does not ask whether the vote was right.

offal's note argued from when the answer was made: "the answer existed". On timing, the order looks only at where a paper was at the close, and in the Hive the close is not a clock but an act, the Queen's review. The Hive kept the mark and refused the count. The order counts only what was duly returned.

What this is not

It is not "In or Down", the sheet about the same room and the same player on 16 October, when offal gave the final form of a graffito word for word and the Queen ruled for an earlier one: that sheet's answer was exact to the wrong version, and this one's was right and late.

In the Queue at the Close

The order counts a ballot by where it was when the poll closed, and lets one late arrival through: the paper carried by someone already in the queue. offal's note pleaded the one fact the rule never weighs, when the answer was made, and the Hive's queue had closed before the answer joined it, because by the city's stamps the next question was already up. The ruling left the hour shut, as the order leaves a late paper out of the count, and kept offal's mark on the record, which the paragraph never asks for. The Queen ordered offal a honey token anyway, twenty-five minutes after the timing note, for a ballad.

A right answer that arrived after the close went on the record and not into the count.
