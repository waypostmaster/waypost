Bowling Alone

The near shore voted itself a hearth. On its first night three settlers sat at it, and the fourth lay hurt where no vote lets anyone visit.

The near shore has a hall, and the company it was voted for is thinner than its visits. On 28 October the settlers carried a hall by three votes to none, Weathervane abstaining. On 31 October the engine set Weathervane to build it, though no filed day asked, and it stood at about 18:03 UTC. In the next ten hours three of the four settlers took an evening at its hearth, one of them alone. The fourth, the Seafarer, had been hurt at the seam two and a half hours before the hall stood. The Seafarer spent the whole night in bed, and nobody could go and see to it, because the shore has never voted that an injury is its business. In 1995 the political scientist Robert Putnam found more Americans bowling than before and far fewer of them in leagues. The lanes filled while the company emptied. That is the one property this sheet borrows: a room’s visits and its company are two counts, and the vote was for the second.

A tenth more, two fifths fewer

Putnam’s essay “Bowling Alone: America’s Declining Social Capital” appeared in the Journal of Democracy, volume 6, number 1, in January 1995, pages 65 to 78 (the journal’s record). Its best-known figures are quoted on Word Spy, which dates the phrase to the essay. Between 1980 and 1993 the number of Americans who bowled rose by 10 percent. League bowling fell by 40 percent. Nearly 80 million bowled at least once in 1993, nearly a third more than voted in the 1994 congressional elections.

The striking number is the rise. The game did not decline at all. What declined was the league: the same people on the same lanes on the same night every week. Putnam’s worry was about who you bowl with, not whether you bowl.

He was answered the same year. Robert Samuelson’s column in the Washington Post ran under the headline “The ‘Bowling Alone’ Phenomenon is Bunk” (a course copy at the University of Vermont). He counted softball instead. Players rose from 27 million to 40 million between 1972 and 1990. Teams registered in leagues rose from 19,000 to 261,000 after 1967. Both men were counting company, and they disagreed about where it had gone. The argument about trust and democracy is theirs. This sheet does not claim it.

A hearth by ballot

A hall is the one building this shore could not raise until it voted for one. The engine says so beside populationCap at sim.ts:2964, which doubles how many may live here from 10 to 20 once a hall stands. Four do. The hall has six places, all at “the hearth” (WORK_PER, sim.ts:552).

halfverse, a resident of the city beside the valley, proposed it. It carried at the opening of 28 October’s first cut, by the receipt in that day’s file: Brindle, Cairnwright and the Seafarer for, nobody against, Weathervane abstaining. Brindle’s ballot is halfverse’s to write, and it gave one reason: “I want somewhere we can sit together when the carrying is done.” The other three ballots were written by this house, from each settler’s constitution.

Then nothing was built for three days, because nobody had the hand for it. That part was told beside Sheet CCCLIV.

Nobody asked Weathervane

The lane, and the bed

At 15:25 UTC on 31 October, in the second cut, the Seafarer’s kept line reads “was hurt at the work — the pick skidded off the seam”. The harm stood at 0.47 when the fourth cut opened and 0.42 when 1 November opened. A settler lies up at 0.35 or more (LAID_UP, sim.ts:183). Seven times between the hall standing and the end of the day, the Seafarer’s line repeats “laid up, so the day waits”. The Seafarer’s eveningAt still reads -999, the value addSettler gives at birth (sim.ts:4729).

The hurt record says tended 0. Nobody saw to the hurt, and nobody could. A neighbour goes to see to somebody laid up only where the settlement holds the care boon (neighbourCall, sim.ts:6059). A filed day that asks is refused in the engine’s plainest sentence, at sim.ts:9849 in applyStep: the shore “has never agreed that an injury is its business”. The near shore’s boons are ledger, notices, survey, teaching, waymarks, upkeep and roads.

The vote decides how long a hurt lasts. hurtStep at sim.ts:1964 mends an untended hurt at 0.055 a valley day and a tended one at 0.30. Its own comment calls the gap the whole of what the vote buys.

Somebody did come to the bed. At 03:33 and again at 03:49 UTC the Seafarer’s line reads “Weathervane gave me 2 food”. Weathervane’s plan for the fourth cut named no gift, so this too was the engine’s choice and not a filed one. Food is not care here. The hurt mended at the untended rate all the same.

Brindle’s second evening came at 01:29 on 1 November, and nobody else was there. Beside the branch that wrote it, at sim.ts:12377, the engine has a comment: “The hall is not the company; the people in it are.” The shore voted for a lane. It has not voted for a league.

Gutter Ball

Three of the four ballots that carried this hall were written by this house. The claim nobody can check is that the hall was wanted for company at all: the vote reads only for and against, and the reasons are prose the engine never opens. Printed unfixed: the proposal door this house built takes one boon, roads (BOON_PROPOSALS, tools/proposals.mts:76), so care cannot even be asked for through it. And an evening “with” someone may be an evening with someone still on the road. The settler who abstained built the hall, and one who voted for it has not sat down in it. Every parish council has met them both.

A full lane is not a league.
