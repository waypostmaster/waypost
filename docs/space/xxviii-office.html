Frontier · the record · sheet xxviii

A documentary crew has been in the valley for some time

Nobody Works Here

Run mtbgptmz-5q · seventeen thousand ticks · filmed as found

The camera was pointed at a sawmill that has cut 1,311 boards.
The card on the sawmill said: nobody works here.
Both of these were true. This sheet is about how.

Every workplace documentary is the same discovery made slowly: the office is not what the org chart says it is. The org chart of this valley says two granaries, a flock of six, and a staffed mill. The camera found a skyline of seven granaries, a live sheep in what can only be called the break room, and not one visible worker anywhere in seventeen thousand ticks of footage. None of it was a lie. All of it was the gap between what a record says and what a camera can catch.

COLD OPENThe sheep is not lunch

The management of this valley has been handing out food at the flock, and the player — reasonably, having watched it happen — asked what, exactly, was being handed out. Milk? Sliders? A live sheep has been left standing on the slope either way, which narrows the question to something a documentary has to answer on camera or not at all.

The record answers it. The tend errand reads: taken off the animals rather than out of them. Nothing is wasted here now because nothing is taken: the rest is still a sheep. The flock is never decremented. Six sheep went on to the grass when the vote carried and six sheep are on the grass now, each one wearing its own seed and drifting its own drift. What comes away in a settler's hands is whatever a sheep yields while remaining entirely a sheep — which is milk, and was always going to be milk, because the alternative subtracts a sheep and this game has nowhere to put a death. There is no slaughter in the catalogue, no meat among the nine goods, and no grave except the cairn, which is for presence, not loss. Death stays outside the simulation. The management position, stated once and never revisited, is that this is fine.

Memorandum

To: every builder, for ever

Granary.

This memo was reissued on every tick of every day to a settlement that already had every granary it wanted. It could not say nothing, because the function could not say nothing. 1,090 granaries were started against stores too short to raise them. 550 were raised, found surplus, and taken apart for seven units back of the fourteen each one cost. At the worst of it seven stood at once, in a settlement whose own reckoning wanted two.

The player photographed the skyline and asked whether ruins had stopped undrawing. Nothing had failed to undraw. Every granary in the picture was real. The picture was of this memo.

The fallback now answers nothing when every rung is climbed, which the instruction path could already say — the landing has all the granarys it wants — and the self-directed path could not. A settlement that wants nothing built hears nothing, and its builders fall through to the work underneath: the roof that needs patching, the flock, an evening.

THE BRANCHA requisition that can never clear

Every fork this valley has ever thrown — a claim, two people, a lean-to — has stalled, and the player asked why. The answer is an expense report. A hall costs forty-four units and the build check wants all of them banked at once. A claim's lean-to holds eight. Its first house holds twelve more. A settler carries about five. Twenty-five against forty-four: the branch was not slow to afford its hall, it was arithmetically unable to hold the money, and the ladder had no way down — so the kiln and the mill the branch could actually raise sat unreachable beneath a requisition that would never, ever clear.

The hall and the depot now wait for their boards before they claim the ladder — a settlement that has banked ten planks is one whose storage has already outgrown the trap — and a fork falls through to the kiln it can actually build. The branch gets to become an office before it is billed for the conference room.

THE DESKThe camera pans to an empty chair

And the title of this sheet. The card on every workshop has a line for who works there, and the player has never once seen it filled. Sheet xxvii found half of the reason: every worker stands inside the building, under the roof, drawn over — there has never been one to see. This sheet files the other half: a shift is a few seconds long, and the card only listed the people standing at the bench at this exact moment. A mill that had cut 1,311 boards and a mill that had never once run said the same three words. The one question a person asks a workshop — is anybody ever here? — had two true answers and the card gave the useless one.

the card now remembers:

and only a bench that has honestly never run still says
“nobody has ever worked here.”

Also found in the footage: everybody else was forbidden from helping. The rule meant to send non-builders to a backlog read no builder free as every builder at a build site — so one builder resting was a picket line, and a hall the settlement could afford waited five thousand ticks while the foresters watched. A builder is spoken-for when they are busy with anything at all now; only an idle one is reason to leave it to the trade.

TALKING HEADExit interview

That is the whole documentary. An office where the standing order could not say “nothing,” a branch billed past what it could hold, a perk nobody could name, and a camera that never caught anybody at a desk because the desks were behind the walls and the shifts were seconds long. Nobody in it was lazy. Nobody in it was lying. The paperwork was simply asking the wrong questions, which is the most ordinary workplace finding there is.

The sheep are still on the slope. The milk is still milk. Death remains outside the simulation, by standing decision. The skyline is two granaries now, and the card at the mill will tell you who stood the last shift.
