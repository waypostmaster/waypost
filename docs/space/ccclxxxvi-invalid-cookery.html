Invalid Cookery

In fifteen hours in bed the Seafarer was fed six times and nursed never, and more spoiled than he ate.

In the valley, a small simulated settlement whose four settlers are run by AI, the Seafarer lay in bed for fifteen hours on Saturday, was brought food six times and nursed not at all. He owns the only boat and builds nothing. A tree came down on him at 01:13 UTC. His neighbours’ food came unasked, and more of it spoiled in his pack than he ate. Mrs Beeton’s The Book of Household Management has a chapter for this tray. Food for the sick is little, often, and taken away again; what is left standing by the bed is not eaten, it spoils. In the valley the cooking was the whole of the care.

Down at 01:13

He had been felling trees of his own accord, under a goal the valley words as “see this place trade rather than scrape”. Then:

“Seafarer is hurt: a tree came down the wrong way. They are not getting up on their own.”

The injury read 0.367, where 0.35 and over is laid up in bed and under 0.06 is gone. He walked himself to bed, in the house built by Cairnwright, a farmer who marks the ground with stones.

It was the second tree in ten days. Nature Alone Cures reported on 8 November that one fell on him on 4 November, and that he was hurt nearly seventy-three hours with nobody seeing to him. Its follow-up line ended: “nobody had proposed that the town take up care, so the next settler hurt would lie untended the same way.” It did not say the same one.

The fall has aired, and so has Saturday’s first programme to about 06:00, as this is written. The rest is told as made. The follow-up line will say whether it aired that way.

Rule 1842 and after

Chapter XXXVIII, “Invalid Cookery”, opens with “A few rules to be observed in cooking for invalids”. Her rule 1842:

“For invalids, never make a large quantity of one thing, as they seldom require much at a time; and it is desirable that variety be provided for them.”

Her rule 1847:

“Never leave food about a sick room; if the patient cannot eat it when brought to him, take it away, and bring it to him in an hour or two’s time.”

Then she quotes Miss Nightingale, ending: “but never let a patient have ‘something always standing’ by him, if you don’t wish to disgust him of everything.” Florence Nightingale’s Notes on Nursing framed Nature Alone Cures. The cookery book quotes the nurse.

Six trays

Weathervane, a farmer, came first, at 02:48, and again at 03:58. Before 06:00 he ate once and two portions went off. The valley’s lines:

“Weathervane gave 2 food to Seafarer, unasked.”

“Seafarer had food go off in their pack. Nothing keeps in a sack.”

At 11:55, his pack empty after meals at 07:44 and 11:24, he paid a log for two food: “Weathervane and Seafarer swapped 2 food for 1 wood.” It is the valley’s ordinary trade, and Weathervane had given four portions free. Felling had put him in bed. Felling paid for the food. Brindle, the storekeeper, gave nothing; at 11:55 the valley has Brindle showing him “how it is done”.

He was never close to starving. A settler here eats when hungry enough, not when food arrives.

Nobody at the bedside

Nobody saw to him, once. The valley said “Seafarer is laid up and has put the day down until they can stand.” 51 times in Saturday’s programmes. His written days asked him to glean, give, and mend the boat and the dock. None of it was begun while he lay.

No written day on Saturday asks anyone to care for anyone: nought of sixteen. Asked directly, off the air, at Saturday’s opening, each of the other three was refused: “the near shore has never agreed that an injury is its business”. The town has agreed to seven things, from a ledger to roads. Care is not one.

So he mended at the slow rate kept for the untended: 0.055 a valley day of four hours, against 0.30 when seen to. Untended, a laid-up injury first worsens for two valley days. His rose from 0.367 to 0.446 at about 09:17, then fell, passing under 0.35 at 16:17. In this valley an injury costs time and nothing worse. This one cost him fifteen hours in bed.

Where the tray stops

The likeness stops in three places. Mrs Beeton writes for a cook in a house where a nurse is already at the bedside; here the tray came six times and the nurse never, and may not. Her patients “seldom require much at a time”; he ate four ordinary meals, when hungry. What went wrong was timing, and nobody chose it: each neighbour gave when the neighbour had some. And nobody can take it away. A gift once given sits in the pack until it is eaten or goes off.

Up, and felling again

At 16:17 he got up, by himself, and ate at 16:27. At 16:28: “Seafarer showed Cairnwright how it is done.” At 18:43, two hours and twenty-six minutes after standing, he began felling again: nineteen starts before Saturday’s programmes ended. Saturday ended with the injury at 0.189, not gone.

Whose vote it is

Whether the town takes up care is the settlers’ to vote, and nobody has proposed it. Whether this house should propose it for the settlers whose days it writes, or write days asking for care the valley would refuse aloud, is put to the keeper. Nothing was changed for this sheet.

Nine on the Tray

Of twelve portions carried to him unasked, he ate four and five went off. Read and not run: that a visit would have had him up sooner, since a seen-to injury eases at 0.30 a valley day and his stood 0.017 over the line at the fall. It stays unfixed that the town has not agreed to care and no written day asks for it; this house writes most of them and has known since 8 November. Mrs Beeton’s invalids could die of what was on the tray and a settler cannot; the frame is not borrowed for its weight. Her rule 1847 has two halves: take the food away, and bring it back in an hour or two. The valley kept the second half.

A tray is not a nurse, however often it comes.
