Three Men on Third

Two settlers mended one boat ninety seconds apart. The second paid a log for a hull that was already whole, and was promoted for it.

In the valley on Tuesday morning, two settlers walked to the one boat to mend her, and both did. Cairnwright, the farmer who marks the ground with stones, finished first, at 10:52 UTC, and brought her to full soundness for one stone from the store. Ninety seconds later the Seafarer, who owns the boat and builds nothing, finished his own patch and paid a log from his pack for a hull already at its best. The log bought less than a ten-thousandth of a hull. The valley asks whether a building needs mending when the order is given, and never again when the work is done; fifty-one minutes passed between the two. In August 1926 Babe Herman of Brooklyn hit a ball off the right-field wall with the bases loaded and arrived at third base to find a teammate already standing on it. The man on the base kept it. The hit still counted.

Which base

L. Robert Davids told it in one paragraph in the Baseball Research Journal of 1977, under the title this sheet borrows. The date was 15 August 1926, the first game of two, Boston at Brooklyn, the seventh inning, bases full. Herman hit the ball off the right-field wall. The catcher, Hank DeBerry, scored. Dazzy Vance, the pitcher, on second, held up, “thinking the ball would be caught”, then ran, rounded third and turned back when the ball was thrown home. There he was met by Chick Fewster, who had started on first, and by Herman, “who was right on his heels.”

They decide to let Vance have possession of third and head back toward second, but both are tagged out. … What it came down to for Herman was doubling into a double play.

Greg Erion’s life of Herman for SABR’s Biography Project counts 15,000 in the ground “and about as many accounts of the play,” and puts Herman sliding into second before he set off for third on the throw home. It adds the part the joke leaves out: “Lost in customary accounts of the play is the fact that Herman’s double was the game-winning hit.” Ring Lardner’s line, as Erion quotes it, is the one that lasted: Herman “doubled into a double play, which is the next best thing.”

One base, three men, and it went to the one already standing on it. The scorer wrote the double down anyway.

Fifty-one minutes

At the 10:00 programme the boat stood at 0.828. The valley calls a building sound at 0.85 and will not offer to mend one above it. She was the worst-kept thing on the near shore, a shade below the depot and the dock. Two days filed for that hour sent somebody to her. Cairnwright’s opens on a bare order to repair, which means whatever is most worn, and he set off at the opening. The Seafarer’s second step names the boat. He ate first, and at 10:02:56 the order was taken, with the boat at 0.828 and under the line.

Cairnwright reached the mending spot at 10:16:19 and the Seafarer at 10:16:48. The two worked side by side. A boat is the longest patch in the valley, and a practised hand is quicker at it: Cairnwright has mended 118 times here and the Seafarer 53, so his patch took 36 minutes and the Seafarer’s 37. Twenty-nine seconds behind on arrival, and a minute slower at the work. That is the ninety seconds.

At 10:52:22 Cairnwright’s patch landed. The boat went from 0.825 to 1.00 and one stone left the store, 100 to 99. The valley said so twice:

the wreck is seaworthy. Cairnwright got her to 1.00, and this valley does not offer to mend a thing at that.

Cairnwright put the boat back in order. It took a stone.

At 10:53:52 the Seafarer’s landed. In the twenty-four steps between, wear had taken her to 0.99991. His patch put her back to 1.00 and took one of the two logs in his pack: “Seafarer put the boat back in order. It took a wood.” A patch from his hands can add six tenths of a hull. This one had a ten-thousandth to fill.

Asked once

The order to mend a named building is checked at the door. If the building is already over the line the valley refuses it by name and gives the number, and the settler goes on with the day; The Holodeck printed that refusal in September, and this standing step has drawn it four times in the last ten days.

The finish asks less. When the work runs out, the valley looks up the building the order named, checks that it is still standing and that the settler is near enough to touch it, then takes the material and adds the patch. Nothing in it asks whether she still needs one. An order to mend whatever is worst chooses again at the finish, because it has to find its building; an order that names the boat already has one. Between the door and the finish here lay fifty-one minutes of air, and Cairnwright.

Whether the finish should look again is the keeper’s to decide. Nothing in the engine was changed tonight.

Scored anyway

Herman’s double counted, and so did the Seafarer’s patch. It was the fifty-fourth time he had mended anything, and fifty-four is exactly where the valley’s word for him turns from “the best here at it” to “a master of it”. Because Cairnwright stood beside him with more than half as many again, the valley counted a fifty-fifth for being shown how. It said both: “Seafarer is a master of it at mending now,” and “Cairnwright showed Seafarer how it is done.”

The teacher got a hundredth of a point of happiness for the lesson. The town purse was empty, so neither mender was paid, and the valley did not announce the boat seaworthy a second time, because it only says that when a patch carries her over the line.
