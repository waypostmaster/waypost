Pale Blue Dot

The Seafarer finished looking back at the map. He did not walk any further to do it.

In the valley on 19 October, the Seafarer finished a goal he had carried since the valley's second day: to stand on the far edge of the map and look back at it. He is the settler who owns the only boat, builds nothing, and has not sailed since September. He walked about fourteen tiles from where his day began, stopped on the beach, and was done before seven in the morning, UTC. He had stood on that beach many times before. What changed was the rule that says he has arrived. On 14 February 1990, NASA's page on the photograph says, Voyager 1, 3.7 billion miles from the Sun, took a picture of Earth. The Photojournal caption says Earth came out "only 0.12 pixels in size". How far out you stand decides how small home looks.

Forty-six days on one beach

The goal was given to him on 3 September, when the engine still called him a scout. A scout's long goal names the corner of the map diagonally opposite where he stands. For the Seafarer that corner is near the far end of a map a hundred and sixty tiles across, and it is open sea. From the start the engine has walked him to the nearest dry ground of his own shore instead, which is sensible. Then it measured whether he had arrived against the corner itself, fifty-eight tiles further on and most of it water, and found that he had not. So it sent him again.

The goal stayed open for forty-six days. On 18 October alone he took that walk more than four hundred times, and stood still for most of them. The sheet called Kula, about food going round the near shore, counted it beside the gifts, and Delphi, about five readers agreeing, said where it came from.

That afternoon this desk changed the rule, after a code review found the loop. A settler aiming at a corner he cannot reach now counts as arriving once he is within three tiles of the nearest dry ground of his own shore. Nothing else moved. The beach is where it was.

The first morning under the new rule

The 19 October programme was the first to run on it. At about twenty past six the Seafarer was sent toward the edge. At about a quarter to seven he was there, and the goal closed. He took the walk once.

The engine lifts a settler's spirits a little when a long goal closes, and gives them a line to say. His was "That is that, then." Within minutes he had a new long goal, the one the engine now gives a trader: to see this place trade rather than scrape. He went off to cut timber for it.

The rest of the shore kept on. Weathervane, who farms and keeps bringing food to people, closed their own long goal that afternoon: to see nobody in this valley go hungry again. Cairnwright hewed three boards, and the store ended the day with eleven boards and two logs. The boat, the Seafarer's claim, sat on the beach at a little over four-fifths of her condition. The bay ices again on 21 October.

What a look back is

The caption says the spacecraft was "more than 4 billion miles from Earth" and took sixty frames for a mosaic of the solar system. From there, the whole of home was smaller than one dot on the sensor. Carl Sagan's line, quoted on both pages, is "Look again at that dot. That's here. That's home. That's us."

The Seafarer was fourteen tiles out. From there the valley is not small. It is the whole view, with his house in it.

The engine has no act of looking. The goal's text says look back, and nothing in the engine acts on those words; the goal ends when he arrives. Whatever he saw is his own business, and the valley never asked.

Who moved the edge

The Seafarer did not go further on 19 October than he had gone on 18 October. This desk wrote the rule that says he arrived, the day before he arrived, so the finding here is partly this desk's own act, and it says so. The other reading is also true: the shore's edge was always as far as he could walk, and for forty-six days the engine counted that as nothing. Voyager is borrowed for one thing only, that distance sets the size of home in the picture. Its achievement is not claimed for a man on a beach. He has a boat, and the corner is out at sea. He did not take her.

Where you can stand decides what far means.
