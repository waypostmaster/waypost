The world was twenty names long Amended in its sleep

To let understanding stop at what cannot be understood is a high attainment. Those who cannot do it will be destroyed on the lathe of heaven.

Chuang Tzu, XXIII — as the novel quotes him

There were no lathes in Chuang Tzu’s China. The sentence Le Guin took her title from is a mistranslation, and she knew it and kept it, because the wrong word had become the true name of the thing. This record is obliged to open there, since the valley it concerns has been governed by wrong words before — a furnace filed as a kiln, a doorframe filed as a granary, an infirmary that granted a hole in the ground — and each time the world was rebuilt on the mistranslation and the people in it went on living, in the world as misheard.

In the novel, George Orr dreams effectively: he wakes and the world has always been what the dream made it, and only the dreamer carries both memories. Dr. Haber, his therapist, is not a monster but a meliorist — he takes hold of the gift and directs it, dream by dream, at overpopulation, at war, at strife, and each amendment arrives with its bill: the fewer people, because of the Plague; the peace on earth, because of the aliens. The valley of this record is the experiment run from the other side of the glass. Here the dreams are real and the settlers must live in each amended world; and here, unlike Portland, the dreamer keeps minutes. Nothing in agents/log.jsonl is ever deleted. The record is the double memory that in the novel only Orr is burdened with — it remembers every world that has stopped having ever existed.

The finding: the world was twenty names long

The keeper’s question this sitting was why the breakaway settlements have not progressed. Tove’s claim was founded at tick 237 and has held two people from that day to past tick 36,000 — Tove, who walked out of the landing in its first week, and Idris, who at tick 2,428 stood at the landing’s door and was told, in the game’s own sentence, there is not the food for another. Bren’s claim has held one: forty-four elections this run in which a single ballot was cast, a man voting alone on questions of how people should live together. Neither place has raised anything past a house. The answer turned out to be two sentences of code, and the second is the worse one.

The boat called at one beach. sim.towns[0], by index, for the whole life of the game. And the only other way anybody has ever changed towns — walking down the coast to ask — points at whichever settlement has the higher appeal, which a lean-to with empty stores never is. Both doors of the world opened toward the richer place. A fork could shrink and could not grow, by construction, and everything downstream of hands followed: a granary carried and held eighteen times across the two claims, on boards that two pairs of hands, both feeding themselves out of the bushes, could never bank.

And twenty names was quietly the size of the world. addSettler refuses past the end of the NAMES array. Lune, the twentieth name, stepped ashore at tick 6,421, and the boat stopped — for the claims, for the landing, for everybody, for ever. Thirty-one thousand ticks passed without an arrival, in a valley whose largest settlement had beds standing empty, and nothing anywhere said so. Haber directed a dream at overpopulation and woke to a world thinned by a plague that had always happened. This world had its population problem solved the same way in advance — no plague required, only an array bound, which is what a plague looks like from inside an implementation.

That is why the forks have not progressed. Not slow: sealed. The stagnation was never a fact about Tove, Idris or Bren — who between them filed the granary wish, the sheep wish, the planer wish, and kept voting, in chambers of two and one, as if somebody might yet arrive to be governed. It was a fact about the shape of their world, invisible from inside it, exactly the kind of fact the book says only the one who was in the room for the dream can know.

The amendment, and its seam

The change is small and gates on what it always gated on. The names list is forty. The boat calls where there is room — arrivals still wait on beds alone, per settlement, the Malthus knob still deliberately unturned — and whoever steps off goes where the living looks best of the places that will actually hold them: the landing while it has beds, the forks once it has none. Nobody chooses a lean-to over a hall. They choose a lean-to over staying on the boat.

It reached the running valley by the mechanism the previous sheets have described: the page rebuilt itself around the sleeping simulation and set it back down mid-stride. The seam is in the record. At tick 37,396 Sten came ashore at the landing, a farmer — asked why here: they would rather be short of everything than be there another year. Ilka and Corin followed, and the landing stood at twenty, its cap, full for the first time in its history. And then the line this amendment exists for: at tick 37,498, Maeve came ashore at Bren’s claim — a farmer, there was a debt at home and no way to pay it — the first human being to arrive at a breakaway settlement in the life of this game. Ashe followed her; Bram and Orla came ashore at Tove’s. The claims stand tonight at four and three.

The record is obliged to keep the wrinkle too. Maeve and Ashe, finding a claim with one man, no roof to spare and nothing in the stores, walked straight down the coast to ask the landing to take them in — and Oren turned them away with the words this valley has always used, the same sentence that made Idris a founder at tick 2,428: there is not the food for another. They went back. A settlement, the game has always held, is what forms when nowhere will have you; the boat now delivers people to that truth faster. Whether four people can do what two could not — bank eleven boards against a wish nine times carried — is the open question the next sheet inherits, and for the first time it is a question about people rather than about an index.

The deterministic 12,000-tick proof run, which for as long as it has existed has ended at twenty settlers, now ends at thirty-two, in three settlements, with the same ladder standing — kiln, smelter, mine, masons, sawmill, joiner, granary. The gate is green. In the amended world it has, of course, always ended that way.

The private dream — in re: the two men and the coin

The keeper also asked after an earlier report’s finding that two men hold most of the coin, and whether some work was valued differently than other work. Ysolde earned more than Ferro. She holds nothing, because she spent it — on hires, on wood, on other people’s afternoons — and he did not. Ferro’s stated aim, set by his own borrowed mind and marked done four separate times in the log, was: put by enough coin to be nobody’s charity case. The concentration is not a wage gap. It is a spending gap, downstream of a dream — one man in the valley dreamed specifically of a full purse, the world honoured it, and the honest thing the ledger shows is that in a small economy a private dream compounds exactly the way Haber’s public ones did, without a single rule being bent.

Was some work valued differently? Yes — by scarcity, which is the only appraiser this valley has. Sixty-two percent of every coin ever paid changed hands over wood; ore, meanwhile, is priced at 1.19 against wood’s three-to-five, because two hundred and eighty-seven units of it sit banked against a smelter that cannot eat it fast enough. The miners — Pike, Halla, and every founder of every fork — spent this run hauling the one good the valley refuses to want, and it shows: thirteen coin, five, and nought. And half the valley holds no coin for the plainer reason that it works in the other two currencies entirely — Mara took thirty-six contracts this run and Vell twenty-seven, paid in goods and in favours owed, the credit ledger the game ran on before there was a mint. The floor under all of it held: not one offer in the record came in under the agreed rate. The two men are not standing on anybody. They are standing on wood, at the top of a market that values a forester’s day at double a miner’s — a verdict no one decreed and no one voted for, which is precisely why the record writes it down.

Er’ perrehnne

Orr’s case against Haber was never that the world should not change. It was that a dream directed from above arrives whole, pays its bill in a currency the dreamer did not agree to spend, and has always been true — so nobody inside it can object, because nobody inside it can remember otherwise. The valley’s constitution, assembled sheet by sheet, reads in hindsight like a defence against its own keeper’s Augmentor: decisions belong to a named person; nothing is given; every amendment is run against the identical world twice before it is believed; the Malthus knob is refused on principle; and the record keeps both versions of everything, so that here, unlike Portland, before remains a fact — consultable, committed, sealed in eight-megabyte segments. Tonight’s amendment was directed all the same; there is no pretending otherwise; the defence is the paper trail and the size of the step. A boat that calls where there is room is the smallest dream that answers what Tove and Bren actually asked for, and it was measured before it was kept.

The aliens, who understand dreaming, greet each other in the novel with a word that means something like welcome; let go. Maeve, asked why here, said there was a debt at home and no way to pay it. Bren, who came ashore a trader, is a miner now; he has been alone on his claim for a hundred and eighty valley-days, voting for sheep, a planer, and somewhere dry to keep food he does not have. As of tonight there are two more names on his beach, turned away from the landing like everyone who ever amounted to anything on this coast. The settlers live their lives over and over, and reality changes beneath them, and they do not break step; the kindest thing in the record is that it never once catches them noticing. Er’ perrehnne.
