The Muqaddimah

On the opening of the new valley: a capital that has stopped growing and turned its surplus into silos, a breakaway out-building it, and a third settlement founded the same hour under the same rules that has raised nothing at all — read through Ibn Khaldūn, who wrote down why the centre stops.

Tunis, 1377 · run mtdotjm9-e4, tick 6,096 Day 30 · 23 souls · 3 settlements
The finding The valley is markedly healthier and one number did it. At the same tick, the closed run held 13 people and this one holds 23 — and the whole of the difference is at the edges: the two breakaways in the old valley had three people in three buildings, and the two here have thirteen people in ten. A lean-to that holds twelve can bank the ten wood a house costs, and everything follows from that. But the landing has not grown since tick 2,142, it cannot receive anybody, and five of the six buildings it has raised since are granaries.

From tick 2,142 to now the landing has held at ten people and ten houses. Eighteen days. Its places went from fourteen to nineteen and four of those five are granaries, taking it from two to six. Food across the same window fell from five to two; it stands at five again today, against ten mouths.

Six silos, and food that has never once covered the table. It is the hunger ratchet: releasedTo re-opens the storehouse category whenever the settlement has fewer meals on hand than mouths, and a granary does not grow food, so the release never closes and every ballot carries another one. It is the same shape as the sixteen masons’ yards that closed the last valley — a reading taken at the store answered with a building — and it is the half of it that was deliberately not gated when the workshop half was, because the honest answer here is not a seventh shed. The landing has three farmers.

And the town is shut. populationCap is ten without a hall, the landing has ten and no hall, so roomFor is nought and the boat cannot call there at all. Every arrival in this valley now goes to a breakaway. That is Sheet XXXV’s amendment compounding with Sheet XXXVII’s, and between them they have quietly made the capital a closed town that immigration routes around.

Sula’s claim has stood at two buildings since tick 996 — five thousand ticks, one line on the plate — with no food and two people who have slept outdoors that whole time. Oren’s has eight buildings, nine people, and the only working bench in the valley: a joiner that has made five pieces. Both masons’ yards on this coast, and the landing’s kiln, have made nothing at all.

Nothing in the code distinguishes the two claims. Whatever separates them is composition, or the order people arrived in, or one early accident that compounded. This record has never had a controlled pair to look at before, and it has one now.

IV Why the centre stops

Ibn Khaldūn wrote the Muqaddimah in 1377 as the preface to a history and it turned into the thing the history was for: an account of ‘asabiyya — the binding feeling of a group that has to hold together — and of what prosperity does to it. A people who must cooperate to survive build; a people who have arrived stop building and start keeping. Vigour passes outward to whoever still has to work for it, and the centre lives on what it has stored.

The goal of civilization is sedentary culture and luxury. When it reaches that goal, it turns toward corruption and takes on the traits of old age. Ibn Khaldūn, the Muqaddimah, book one

I did not go looking for that. The valley is arguing it unprompted: a landing with ten roofs, six granaries, a kiln that has never been lit and a yard that has never squared a stone — every institution and no industry — against a claim founded on day five down the coast with no builder in it and the only bench anybody works.

The frame earns its place because it also has room for the part that flatters nobody. Not every group that walks out founds anything. Sula’s claim left on the same terms as Oren’s and has raised one house in five thousand ticks, and the theory that explains why the periphery rises has to explain that too, or it is just a story about the valley we happen to be watching.

V The slate

A reading made after the event is worth nothing, so these are filed now, at tick 6,096, with the verdict lines left blank for whichever sheet grades them. Each names what would falsify it. The fifth is the one that matters, because it grades a change made this evening rather than an observation about the valley.

  5. No settlement raises a second masons’ yard while its first has made nothing

    Both yards in this valley stand at nought output. The gate added this evening holds a workshop category shut while any standing bench of that kind has never made a thing — which is the whole of what was meant to stop the sixteenth yard.

    Check at tick 15,000, and at every sheet after. Falsified by a second idle yard anywhere on this coast, and if it is falsified the gate is wrong and wants knowing about before this valley is fifty thousand ticks deep.
