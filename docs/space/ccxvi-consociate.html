1 · Eleven names besides the family

Fruitlands lasted seven months. Bronson Alcott and the Englishman Charles Lane took ninety acres in the summer of 1843, called their household the Consociate Family, and undertook to live without hired labour, animal labour or animal food. It ended in the January, with Lane and his son gone to the Shakers and Alcott shut in his room, as he thought, to face the end.

The roll of it is one sentence. This desk read it this morning in Clara Endicott Sears, Bronson Alcott’s Fruitlands, 1915, a compilation of the diaries and the letters, fetched as the Internet Archive’s scanned text. At character 35,285:

“The original members of the Community that started the unique experiment were Mr. Alcott, his wife, and four small daughters, the Englishman Charles Lane and his son William, H. C. Wright (for a short time) and Samuel Bower, Isaac T. Hecker, of New York, Christopher Greene and Samuel Larned, of Providence, Abraham Everett and Anna Page, Joseph Palmer, of Fitchburg, and Abram Wood.”Sears, 1915, at character 35,285 of the fetched text

Count the names that are not the Alcott family. There are eleven. Two sentences later the book says they “did not all arrive at once, but came within a short time of each other”, which is as near to an arrival hour as the roll gets. Wright, the sentence says in its own parenthesis, refused to be a regular member. The fullest thing the book records about any of the eleven is that Abram Wood’s transcendentalism “showed itself chiefly in insisting upon twisting his name hind side before and calling himself ‘Wood Abram’”. His name is his contribution. It is also all of it.

Then the barley. An easterly storm came up while the men were away lecturing. At character 215,897, in Louisa May Alcott’s satire of her own childhood, printed in the same volume:

“Three little girls, one boy (Timon’s son), and herself, harnessed to clothes-baskets and Russia-linen sheets, were the only teams she could command.”“Transcendental Wild Oats”, at character 215,897 of the fetched text

The woman was her mother. She got the grain in. One property is borrowed here and no other: a roll of names measures who agreed, and the work is done by whoever is standing in the field. Nothing else about Fruitlands is claimed. Nobody died there, the eleven were not frauds, and the valley’s newcomers have signed no creed and eaten no fish.
