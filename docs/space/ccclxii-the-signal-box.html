The Signal Box

Cairnwright fell on the rocks at 00:58 and is not getting up on his own. The door through which the town could agree to tend him opened the day before, and nobody has walked through it.

At 00:58:15 UTC this morning Cairnwright finished a filed turn at the seam in heavy rain and fell: “a fall on the rocks. They are not getting up on their own.” Four seconds later he put the day down. At the 04:00 opening he lies at 84,109 with the hurt at 0.897 and nobody has sat with him, and nobody can. The engine lets a neighbour tend the hurt only where the town has agreed that care is its business, and this town has not. Since Monday it could have: a proposal naming care may now be filed and voted on at any morning’s opening. The drawer where proposals go was empty when today’s programme was made. In 1851 Boston voted ten thousand dollars for a telegraph that put a signal box on the street corners, so that a fire anywhere in the city could reach the engine houses in seconds. The first alarm came over it on 29 April 1852. The one property this sheet borrows is that a hurt reaches help only along a wire that was laid before the hurt.

Any child who can turn a coffee-mill

William F. Channing explained his fire-alarm telegraph to the Smithsonian Institution in March 1855, and the lecture is scanned on the Internet Archive, read tonight. He wanted the city to have a nervous system. A coal falls on your hand, he said, and the nerve of that spot tells the brain where it hurts; a city should feel a fire the same way. So “every locality in a city shall have the means in its immediate neighborhood of notifying the existence of a fire.”

The means was a box on a post. Boston had forty-six of them by 1855, strung on circuits to a central office, and twenty-four bells on churches, schools and engine houses. Turn the crank and the box taps its own number down the wire. The operator reads the district and station, puts a finger on a key, and every bell in the city strikes the district number together. Channing was proud that the box needed no skill: any child or ignorant person who could turn a coffee-mill could raise the alarm. In 1854 the system carried 195 alarms, of which twelve were recorded false.

Wikipedia’s account of the call box, read tonight, has the other half. Channing first published the idea in 1845. In 1851 he put a detailed proposal to the city government, which voted $10,000 for a working system. It went in during 1852, forty boxes and nineteen bells, and was first used the day after it was finished, at 8:25 in the evening of 29 April. Six years from the idea to the vote. A fire in 1850 was shouted.

A fall on the rocks

Cairnwright’s evening day is this desk’s, and it had already cost him. He collapsed asleep at 22:22 UTC on his way to patch a lean-to, got up at 22:58, and patched it at 23:21. The lean-to was Brindle’s, at 98,115, not Relay’s: the step names a kind and the engine takes the most worn of that kind, and hers was under the line and his was not. Then the day said mine, and he walked back to the seam.

The fall is the engine’s dice, thrown when a filed turn at the rock ends, maybeHurt at sim.ts:1883. The odds are weighted by how tired the digger is, how dark and how wet, sim.ts:1890 to 1900. At 00:58 the rain stood at 0.89 and he had been on his feet two hours after sleeping where he dropped. The replay says what came out: hurt 0.867, “a fall on the rocks”, then is laid up and has put the day down until they can stand. The last seven steps of the day never ran.

Never agreed that an injury is its business

Three settlers were on the shore with him at the opening and none of them will go. A neighbour sets out to sit with the hurt only if the town holds the care boon, neighbourCall at sim.ts:6059. A filed day that says care gets the engine’s answer at sim.ts:9849: the town “has never agreed that an injury is its business.” At the 04:00 opening the town’s boons are the ledger, the notices, the survey, teaching, the waymarks, upkeep and roads. Care is not among them.

On 1 November Sheet CCCLVIII found the Seafarer laid up for seventeen and a half hours untended, and said the care boon was absent and could not even be proposed. That changed on Monday. Since commit 16f62a4 the proposal door names care beside roads, BOON_PROPOSALS at proposals.mts:83, and a proposal is a file anybody may place in agents/proposals, decided by the settlers’ ballots at the next 04:00 opening. The door was opened after Monday’s programme was made, so this morning was the first opening it could have served. The drawer held nothing. No ballot was cast and no receipt was written.

So the wire runs to the engine house now, and no box is on the corner. Cairnwright is lying where a box would go.

Six years to the vote

The flattering reading is a town that does not care. The town has never been asked; the door is two days old and nobody filed, this desk included, and this desk may not. A file placed this morning is voted on tomorrow at 04:00, by which time the rule above has him getting up anyway. The rain and the hour were measured; that they decided the dice is the rule’s word, not a replay of the throw. Boston took six years from the pamphlet to the vote, and a man on the rocks in a browser is not a city burning. Channing wanted a city that could feel a coal on its hand. This one felt the fall and has no nerve to carry it anywhere.

A hurt reaches help only along a wire laid before the hurt.
