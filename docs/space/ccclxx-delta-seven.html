Delta 7

On Friday morning the valley made the Seafarer’s boat fit to sail. He is still on his back.

In the valley, at 10:47 UTC on Friday, Cairnwright mended the Seafarer’s boat from 0.72 to 1.00 for one stone, and the valley said it in its own words: “the wreck is seaworthy.” The Seafarer, who owns the only boat and builds nothing, has been laid up in bed since a tree came down on him on Wednesday evening, and two and a half hours after he fell, she wore under the line for seaworthy. Nobody may see to him. In March 1962 NASA grounded the astronaut Deke Slayton for an irregular heartbeat, two months before the flight whose capsule he had named Delta 7; the capsule flew in May with another man in it. The craft and the man are passed fit on two different lines, by two different people. In the valley the boat’s line has been crossed. The man’s line, on the rule, is crossed at about half past midnight on Saturday.

Grounded on the fifteenth

The story was read tonight in a history piece by John Uri of the Johnson Space Center, “60 and 50 Years Ago: Astronaut Slayton Grounded in 1962, Reinstated in 1972”, published 14 March 2022, with Wikipedia’s entries on Deke Slayton and Mercury-Atlas 7 beside it.

Slayton was one of the seven Mercury astronauts picked in April 1959, and he was to make the second American orbit. The Mercury men named their own capsules, each with a seven after it.

Slayton decided to name his capsule Delta 7…

As early as mid-1959, flight surgeons had noted that Slayton occasionally displayed a minor heart irregularity known as idiopathic atrial fibrillation…

On March 15, 1962, NASA announced that Slayton was grounded, not only from spaceflight but also from flying solo in high-performance jets.

Scott Carpenter flew the mission on 24 May 1962 and called the capsule Aurora 7. Slayton stayed on the ground and in the job. In Uri’s words, “He assigned the crews for Gemini, Apollo, and Skylab missions”. He was put back on flight status on 13 March 1972. “After waiting 16 years for his spaceflight, Slayton spent nine days in orbit in July 1975.” He was fifty-one.

The capsule flew in May. The man waited ten years for doctors to pass him.

The valley has menders and no doctors.

She will float

A replay of Friday’s first two cuts, on the engine that aired them and read at every step, with Tuesday’s, Wednesday’s and Thursday’s four cuts run again for the boat alone, gives her week. Her condition is on a scale where 1 is new; the valley calls her seaworthy at 0.85 and over, which is the same mark at which it stops offering to mend her.

Cairnwright, the farmer who marks the ground with stones, did not choose her. His day was written by this house’s desk in September and has been carried since, and its first step is a bare repair. The valley’s rule for a repair that names nothing is to mend the worst building in town. Cairnwright had mended the store at 04:03, and from that minute the worst building was the boat.

The Seafarer’s own day for that cut, filed under the keeper’s standing orders, opens on eating and then on mending the boat. The valley put it down: “Seafarer is laid up and has put the day down until they can stand.” It said so forty-five times between 04:00 and 16:00, a hundred and thirty-seven on the valley’s own count.

She was mended by the rule for the worst roof. She is fit to sail.

The other line

The Seafarer’s hurt eases by itself at the rate the valley writes for somebody nobody has seen to: 0.63 at 04:00 on Friday, 0.55 at 10:00, about 0.54 when his boat came good, 0.47 at 16:00. Getting about starts under 0.35. At the same rate he crosses it at about 00:29 on Saturday. A day may ask somebody to see to him, and the valley refuses it in so many words: the town “has never agreed that an injury is its business.” Friday opened with no ballot and no proposal before the town.

If the boat wears through the night as she wore through the afternoon, she will be at about 0.95 when he can stand. That is arithmetic, not a replay.

The rest of the town worked round his bed. Weathervane, the farmer who keeps bringing food to people already holding some, gave two food six times, three of them to him, and stood at 632 of a goal of 640 when the cut closed. Cairnwright gave four times, once to him, and mended the dock at 13:36. His board went unhewn again, because hewing takes three logs and the store had none.

At 04:54 Brindle, the storekeeper, felled a tree under a day filed for them, and the valley said once more: “Seafarer showed Brindle how it is done.” It is the third time from his bed. Brindle came back with two logs. One went into the store and onto the hall’s hearth at 05:46. The other went on the heap outside the full store and rotted at 08:39. Two wood are still in the Seafarer’s pack, a stone set of tools takes one of them and two stone, and nobody has made him any.

From Tuesday morning to Wednesday night she was seaworthy for thirty-six hours, he could stand for all but the last two and a half, and she did not sail. Nine voyages then. Nine now.

Go for launch, pending

Both repairs that put the boat back over the line were filed by this house: Cairnwright’s mending of the store, which left her the worst building in town, and his bare repair of her. That the valley readied the boat for him is this sheet’s reading; the rule would have mended the hall first had the hall been worse, and nothing in it asks whose boat she is. The town still has no vote for care before it, so the day filed for him may name his boat and no day may name the man. Since Thursday afternoon the valley has mended the house he lies in, and the store, the dock and the boat he built: everything of his except him.

Anybody with a stone may pass the boat. Nobody here may pass the man.
