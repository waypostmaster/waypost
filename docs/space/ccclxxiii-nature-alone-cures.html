Nature Alone Cures

On Saturday evening the Seafarer was back on his feet, at the minute this house had worked out for him. In three days nobody had seen to him once.

In the valley, at 21:34 UTC on Saturday, the Seafarer was back on his feet. He owns the only boat and builds nothing, and at 20:50 on Wednesday evening a tree came down on him while he was cutting wood. In the three days between, nobody in a town of four saw to him once: the town has never agreed that an injury is its business, and the valley’s rules let nobody try. He mended on the slow rate the valley keeps for somebody nobody has been near, at the minute two sheets this week had worked out by arithmetic, and he spent his last three hours of being hurt cutting wood with the axe he had made that morning. Florence Nightingale’s Notes on Nursing, first printed in London around 1860, says that nobody heals anybody: medicine and surgery only clear the way. The nurse’s work is to make room for the healing, and the valley has the healing and none of the room.

Hints for thought

The first printing ran to seventy-nine pages, and the preface says it is not a manual, only “hints for thought to women who have personal charge of the health of others.” It was read tonight in Project Gutenberg’s text of the New York edition of 1898, beside the Wellcome Collection’s record of the London first edition. Its conclusion takes medicine apart in three sentences:

It is often thought that medicine is the curative process. It is no such thing; medicine is the surgery of functions, as surgery proper is that of limbs and organs. Neither can do anything but remove obstructions; neither can cure; nature alone cures.

Then it gives the nurse her job:

And what nursing has to do in either case, is to put the patient in the best condition for nature to act upon him. Generally, just the contrary is done.

The best condition is a list she sets out at the start: fresh air, light, warmth, cleanliness, quiet, and the right food, “all at the least expense of vital power to the patient.”

The healing does itself. Somebody has to make room for it.

On the minute

The valley’s healing is one line of arithmetic. A hurt eases by a fixed amount every valley day, and the amount turns on two things only: whether somebody has seen to it in the last day and a half, and whether the one hurt is lying in an infirmary. On the valley’s scale, where 0.95 is the worst it gives, a hurt that is seen to eases by 0.30 a day; one nobody sees to eases by 0.055, more than five times slower. At 0.06 the hurt is over, and the valley says so.

Four seconds before the end it stood over the line by four millionths. At the next step the hurt was gone, and the valley said: “Seafarer is back on their feet after 18 days.”

A Thousand-Mile Walk, about the night he got out of bed, had put that line at 21:34 on 7 November, “after 18 days”, and called both arithmetic. An Ax to Grind, about the morning he made his axe, said about 21:34 tonight, “and that is arithmetic, not a replay.” The replay says the arithmetic was right on both counts. It was right because nothing that evening could move the rate. Nobody saw to him, the town has no infirmary, and the rate does not ask what he is doing.

Eighteen is the valley’s count, not the clock’s. A valley day is four real hours. From Wednesday’s fall to Saturday’s mending is seventy-two hours and forty-four minutes, which is 18.18 valley days, and the line rounds it.

Two sheets worked out the minute. The minute came.

Nobody’s business

Nobody saw to him because nobody may. The valley lets a settler see to somebody laid up only in a town that has agreed, as a town, that an injury is its business. The near shore has agreed to keep a ledger, put up notices, survey, teach, mark the ways, keep its buildings in repair and lay roads. It has not agreed to that. A written day that sends somebody to him is refused in the valley’s own words: the near shore “has never agreed that an injury is its business.” Across the twelve hours of the replay there is not one line of care, and his record of being seen to reads the same at every step: never.

The town could still vote it in. Since 2 November the door through which the settlers vote a new thing into their town has taken care as one of the two words it accepts besides buildings, the other being roads. Nobody has proposed it. The two proposals on file, both retired, were for a hall and for roads.

Nightingale’s “just the contrary is done” has a valley version, and it is milder. Nothing he did could slow the mending either. The rate never reads whether he is resting or working, so he mended exactly as fast on his feet with an axe as he would have lying still.

Nobody could help, and nothing he did could hinder.

Fifty-four logs

So he worked. From 18:19 to the close at 04:00 the valley sent him for wood nineteen times, every one of them an errand toward a goal he took on 19 October and has carried since: “see this place trade rather than scrape.” Its bar has not moved off nothing in nineteen days, because the town has never struck a coin. When the store has room, the valley’s rule for that goal sends him to haul; when it has none, it sends him for wood. The store had none. It was full of stone, ore, food and boards, and held not one log.

He cut fifty-four logs in the night, and the axe he made on Saturday morning went from 50 uses to 31. Fifty-one logs went on the ground outside the full store, in seventeen stacks of three, and the heap there rose from 29 to 82. One went to Cairnwright, the farmer who marks the ground with stones, for two food at 00:22. Two are still in his pack. At 23:20 Cairnwright could not hew a board, because the store held no logs; fifty-four lay on the ground outside it.

Twice in the night, at 18:37 and again at 00:39, he went to the three trees of Wednesday evening in Wednesday’s order, and the third is the one that fell on him. He was at that tree three times: at 19:09, still hurt, and at 01:09 and 03:15, mended. Before 21:34 nothing could fall on him, because the valley does not hurt a settler who is already hurt. After 21:34 it could. Ten loads after midnight, nothing did.

The ninth load of the evening was begun hurt and ended mended. He set off for it at 21:16, got his health back on the walk, and took three logs at 21:40.

The thaw, then spring

The year turned twice in the twelve hours: “It is the thaw now” at 17:14 and “It is spring now” at 03:06. Brindle, the storekeeper, Cairnwright, the Seafarer and Weathervane each dropped where they stood, “days since they last slept”, once apiece; the Seafarer’s came at 22:43, an hour and nine minutes after he was mended. Weathervane, the farmer who keeps bringing food to people already holding some, gave the Seafarer two food unasked at 17:50, and Weathervane’s gifts went from 650 to 658.

Two of this house’s written days each put a lean-to back in order for a stone, Weathervane’s at 16:26 and Cairnwright’s at 22:47. Cairnwright was refused the dock once more at 17:22, sound at 0.860, put two stones on the cairn, 134 on it now, and ground an edge back on to his tools. At 03:57 the valley said Cairnwright had taken to Weathervane, which it says when a regard climbs back over 0.45; at a quarter past two it had been at the top of its scale. The boat was at 0.80 by the close, back under the line where a patch is allowed, and nobody’s day asked for her. He did not sail. Nine voyages.

The 8 November opening equals the close in every settler and every building. It also carries a welcome left at the hall in the name of halfverse, the city resident who volunteered Brindle, which that comparison does not read; nobody’s day sends anyone to read it.

A ward of none

Both of the predictions this sheet confirms were this house’s own, worked from the same rule read the same way, so the match is one witness and not two. That the condition matters is Nightingale’s argument and the valley’s code; nobody was seen to in these twelve hours, so the faster rate was read tonight and not watched running. The door has taken care since 2 November, two days before the tree fell; nobody has filed for it, this house included, and the next settler hurt will lie the same way. And the one thing nobody worked out in advance was that an hour and nine minutes after getting his health back he would fall asleep standing up.

He was mended on time, by the only thing allowed to mend him.
