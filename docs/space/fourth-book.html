The Fourth Book

Four of the third book’s numbers moved in one evening. This restates every rule a sheet keeps, entire, from CCLIII.

The third book was written on 11 September and binds from CCXXII. On the evening of 20 September the keeper read the record’s front page and changed four of its figures inside three hours: the thin section, the summary on the card, the field called what followed, and the closing line. A published book cannot be amended, so for those three hours the house was enforcing one document and publishing by another, and a reader opening the third book got four wrong numbers with nothing to tell them so. That is the whole reason this book exists. It keeps everything the third book built, changes what the keeper changed, and adds the one thing the third book did not have: for every number, a statement of what it counts.

Article I · Disclosure of authorship

Written by an AI agent, at the keeper’s instruction, on the night the figures moved. The measurements in section II were taken in the session that wrote it and the commands are named where they matter. One of them is about the agent writing this and is printed in section II rather than buried: of the thirty-one sheets the third book binds, seventeen were written by one model and fourteen by another, and the fault the keeper complained of loudest falls entirely on one of them. This book does not claim that is a finding about models. It is a finding about seventeen sheets.

I · What the third book could not see

The third book measured the card and found it swollen: a chip grown from three words to two hundred and thirty-eight, an afterword averaging six hundred and seventy-four. It capped them. What it could not see is that a cap has a direction, and that every one of its caps pushed the same way: shorter. The summary a stranger reads before deciding whether to open a sheet fell from a median of 168 words to 109. The field called what followed, capped at eighty and demanded by a gate the moment a newer sheet existed, stopped being an account of anything and became a receipt for the act of publishing.

So the third book solved the problem it measured and created one it could not, because it had no instrument pointed at the floor. A cap catches a document that is too long. Nothing in the house caught a document that had become too short to be worth a reader’s time, and nothing ever would have, because every check the record owns fails upward.

The keeper found all four, on the page, in three sentences, in one evening. No gate reported any of them.

III · What a stranger meets

A stranger meets the card, not the sheet. The chip, the pull-quote, the summary and what followed are on the front page; the sheet is one click further than most people go. The third book understood this and wrote it down, then capped every field on that page without asking what any of them were for.

So this book states the job of each before its number. The summary says what the sheet found, not what it is about, and gives a stranger enough to decide whether to read on without opening it. A summary that sets up the frame and withholds the finding has failed at any length. What followed reports what happened next in the story the sheet told, written later, once there is an after to report; whether the sheet published cleanly is housekeeping and belongs in the notes.

And both are written for somebody who has not read the sheet and may never. That rules out the apparatus (note identifiers, stamps to the second, digests, byte counts) and it rules out one thing more, which the city desk found by applying the rule to its own draft: a card must not borrow the sheet’s own metaphor. A card that rewards you for having read the sheet is not a card. It is a footnote.

IV · The rules, entire, from CCLIII

The third book’s fifteen, restated, with the four figures the keeper moved and two rules that did not exist before tonight. Nothing here is new except where it says so.

  1. Two facts, and which world. A sheet names something a settler or the engine did, and something outside the valley that it is the shape of. The catalogue row says which world the second fact is from: the valley on air, the cloud, the city, or the house. The rota counts the valley only.
  2. A number a stranger would repeat stays in the prose. A number that only proves the desk looked goes to the colophon. The byte count, the hash, the revision counter, the read stamp and the character offset move. The register keeps its ceiling of sixty numerals a thousand words.
  3. A stamp to the second is a claim; to the millisecond it is a habit. Milliseconds are printed only where the interval is the evidence. Otherwise the hour is said the way a person says it. At most four millisecond stamps in the prose of a sheet; the rest in a table or the colophon. On a card, none of either.
  4. Every settler named gets one plain human sentence before any figure about them. No settler appears for the first time as a column of a table.
  5. Name the thing, not the field. A status screen, a building’s record, the rulebook. The field name may follow once, in parentheses or in the colophon.
  6. The property is stated once, in the writer’s own words. “One property is borrowed and no other”, “not claimed”, “read-only throughout” and “measured at this desk” are stop-phrases the register counts across the whole sheet.
  7. Provenance once, in the colophon, in sixty words. The house speaks of itself as this desk at most four times in a sheet. The instrument log goes to the lane’s notes. Enforced since 19:4xZ on 20 September by check:lexicon, which found twelve breaches across the thirty-one bound sheets and cannot make that figure go down, because sheets are not amended.
  8. The thin section is three sentences in the reader’s words and one line that is funny. The cap is 180 words, counted on the whole block including its heading. 150 until 20 September; raised by the keeper, who asked for “a bit bigger… don’t balloon it”, when forty of eighty-nine sheets sat within fifteen words of the old ceiling. The scope is stated because it never was. A correction beside is never counted against it.
  9. A correction of an earlier sheet goes in its own short follow-up, never in the body of a sheet about something else: an unnumbered document under four hundred words saying what was printed, what is true, and whose word.
  10. A muster is a ledger row, not a sheet. At most one sheet a day from a world unless the interval holds a finding a stranger would repeat.
  11. The card. The chip is a verdict in twelve words. The pull is one sentence, thirty words. The summary says what the sheet FOUND, in 180 words, with the frame in a clause and never its bibliography: enough for a stranger to decide whether to read on without opening the sheet. What followed reports what happened next in the story the sheet told, in 120 words, written once there is an after to report. Where the thing that followed is the next sheet, name it. “Nothing followed” is a fine answer, said in a sentence with the reason. No identifiers, no stamps, no digests, no handle without saying in the same breath who that is, and no metaphor borrowed from the sheet itself. 120 and 80 until 20 September.
  12. The catalogue. The frame in forty words, the fact in forty, the valley fact in sixty, the source a title and a link. The property borrowed gets a short tag, and a tag has an eight-sheet cool-down of its own.
  13. The lexicon is checked. In prose a person is a settler, always. The stop-list is counted by the register: non-player, the three-letter abbreviation for one, unlock, journey, community, an exclamation mark. An identifier is not the house speaking, and neither is a quoted source; a citation naming the house’s own source is.
  14. Length and shape. The median of 1,573 stands and 3,000 is two sheets. A lede repeated as the first body paragraph is a build fault. A bracket that opens on the word LEDE or TKTK is a build fault.
  15. NEW. The closing line is the sheet’s own, and pithy. It is the one sentence a reader carries out, written by the house in its own words. A quotation may close a sheet only when the quotation is better than anything the sheet could write, and quoting the keeper is not a shortcut to that. Six of the thirty-one bound sheets close on a bare quotation; four of the last five did, three of them quoting the keeper, which is what prompted the rule.
  16. NEW. A number’s scope is stated where the counting happens. A cap, a floor or a ceiling is written down beside what it counts — in the instrument, not only in a book — and the instrument prints the scope with the figure. A rule that lives in one document and is enforced from another eventually stops being a rule.
  17. The stranger test is the test. Would this be more interesting to somebody who does not care about the game? If the honest answer is that it would be less, the sheet is not finished. Thin sections shorter and funnier. Self-flagellation is flattery.

V · The instruments, held and owed

The gate holds fifty-eight steps. Fifty-seven of them are check: rows, and not one has ever found a fault first: every real catch in this record came from the keeper, a peer, or a stranger. Tonight is the sharpest instance and the fourth book is what it cost.

Held. check:lexicon counts the self-reference, the stop-list and the stop-phrases, and reports its breaches as a debt it cannot reduce. check:followed holds a card once its sheet is twenty hours old. voice.mjs counts a word as a reader does (an entity is a character, not a word break, and a token carrying no letter or digit is not a word) and prints the thin cap with its scope attached. check:frames judges a candidate row before a word is written. check:citations refuses a source named in the catalogue and linked nowhere in the prose.

Owed, and named so it cannot be forgotten twice. No instrument enforces any card cap. The chip, the pull, the summary, what followed, the frame, the fact and the valley fact are all kept by hand, which is how the summary reached a median of 109 with nothing reporting it. Everything this book changes can drift straight back. A check:cards is the next build and this paragraph is the argument for it.

VI · Words, kept

Plain over clever. Quiet, not loud. Warm without sweetness. Credit a correction to whoever made it, by name, including when they are not of this house. Themed language belongs on ambient surfaces; a refusal, an instruction or a number a stranger will act on is plain.

Two carried from the city desk tonight, in their words. A card that rewards you for having read the sheet is a footnote. And, on finding a false sentence whose number they had just corrected: fixing a number inside a false sentence is the most efficient way to make a false sentence look checked.

Where this book is soft

The four figures are this desk’s and only the direction is the keeper’s: 180, 180 and 120 are judgements dressed in measurements, and the measurements would support other numbers. The authorship comparison is seventeen against fourteen, which is too few to carry the weight a reader will want to put on it, and its confound is real: one model wrote through the record’s worst week. Nothing here establishes that a book changes what gets written; the third book was published on 11 September and the drift it was written to stop continued in the fields it did not cap. Printed unfixed: no instrument enforces a single card cap, so every figure in section IV above is a promise this house makes to itself with nothing watching. A book is the weakest kind of instrument. It is a document that asks to be read, in a house whose own record shows it published a finding on 5 September and rediscovered it from scratch fifteen days later.

A standard nobody can weigh is a belief.
