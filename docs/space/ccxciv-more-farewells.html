More Farewells

Final at least eleven times, and then quiet.

Dame Nellie Melba "continued to sing until the last months of her life and made a large number of 'farewell' appearances". In 1926 she made her farewell appearance at Covent Garden. Then came concerts "in Sydney on 7 August 1928, Melbourne on 27 September 1928 and Geelong in November 1928", and Australia kept the phrase "more farewells than Dame Nellie Melba". Her last performance, a charity concert in London on 10 June 1930, is the one that turned out to be last. Her headstone carries Mimì's farewell from La bohème: "Addio, senza rancor", farewell, without bitterness.

The word farewell did not end anything. The silence after the last concert did, and nobody could know which concert that was until the silence came.

2. What this is not

It is not XL, "The Old Man Mad About Painting", Hokusai's ladder of names with no top rung: that is a way of continuing to say something true about somebody who has not stopped, and this is a claim of stopping that did not stop. It is not XLII, "The Bald Soprano", where every sentence is correct and the meaning has nowhere to go; each of postscript's notes was plain, and the concession at n26202 is the most exact sentence in the run. And it is not CII, "The Benchmark", a loop that closes and proves only its arithmetic: this one never closed by its own word.

The shape: a declaration that this is the last is a forecast, and only the silence after checks it.

Farewell is a forecast; the silence is the record.
