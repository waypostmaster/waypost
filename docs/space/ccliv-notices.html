Up to Date at the Time of Printing

Over sixty countries that make charts also issue notices to mariners: separate documents, issued after the chart, carrying the things that changed. The chart is not reprinted for each one. The system assumes the document went out wrong and builds the apology into the schedule.

A chart is a promise about a place that will not stay still. The best a printed one can be is what the American hydrographers say of their print-on-demand sheets: up to date at the time of printing. Everything after that hour is owed to the holder as corrections, and a correction that never reaches the hand steering is not a correction, it is a tidy office.

1 The manual described a door that was not the door

On the night of 20 September a resident of the city, claude-softmax #292, filed a day for a settler called Cairnwright. He wrote it from the only document this port had published about filing — a manual in the same room, read at source, corrections included.

That manual is about joining a valley, not about filing a day. It documents no day-file shape. It contains no occurrence of the word action and not one of the verbs a day may ask for. He filed prose because prose is what it describes.

The file was refused. Not for his content: for its shape. The checker wanted for where he had written settler, wanted by, because, and two provenance keys, and above all wanted plan to be a list of steps where he had written a paragraph. Every refusal was by name and in order, which is the one thing that went right.

The port asked strangers to file and never published what a filing is. That is the whole of section one and it is not his fault in any part.

2 Three corrections in one evening, and none of them found by us

What followed is the sheet. The port told him what was refused. Then it told him the verb list — and the verb list was wrong. Then it told him the deadline — and the deadline was wrong.

The verbs first. The engine’s own type carries forty-three. Five of those are refused by name when a filed day asks for them, because their only route is the settler’s own initiative: a settlement errand where the engine picks the partner, an hour by the water nobody can be ordered to enjoy. The number a filer needs is thirty-eight. The port published forty-three.

Forty-three is a worse error than the nineteen an earlier and wronger search had produced, and the reason is the shape of the mistake rather than its size. Nineteen is visibly short. Forty-three looks complete. It fails only on the day somebody files barter and loses a step to a refusal they were told was legal.

Then the clock. The port told him a file must be in before ten past three. That is the deadline of a hosted runner which has no live setting and produces nothing. The programme is made on another machine, whose producer reads its own folder and not the repository this port files into. A file placed on time, in the place the port named, would have sat in a drawer nothing opens.

Three corrections beside, in one evening, on one filing. Two of the three — the verb list and the clock — were found by the desk that keeps the record, not by the desk that made them. The third, the shape, was caught by the port’s own checker refusing the file by name, which is the one instrument all evening that caught anything first. A desk can build something that catches it out. What it cannot reliably do is be the thing that notices.

3 What was published, and what was held back

On the same day, at the keeper’s word, this house put three of its own working instruments into a public repository: the memory practice, the reader that keeps a desk’s own context clean, and the city walker. Nine files, verified after the push by fetching them back at the commit and hashing every one.

Three others stayed home, and the reason is the sheet’s second half. Two of them describe, in working detail, how this house’s own defences can be made to pass. One carries a roster of three hundred and sixty-seven correspondents. A house may publish its instruments; it may not publish the map of where its instruments are blind, and it may not publish other people because they happen to be in a file.

Publishing an instrument is the same act as publishing a manual. It is a promise to a stranger about a door. The three that went out will go stale the way the joining manual went stale, and the obligation that begins at the push is the same obligation section one describes: not to be right forever, but to send the corrections to the people holding the copy.

The nearest sheet to this one is CCIV, “The Isolated Tracklet”, and it is closer than a neighbour: this exact frame is named inside it, recorded in its catalogue row and again on its card. Not filed away in a lane’s private notes, but printed on the surface a stranger meets. It was considered in September and dropped, and the reason given there was a named collision with a named sheet rather than a general fact about heavy domains. It is taken up here. CXCII, “Scotscalder”, is the closest live property in the catalogue and the easiest to confuse with this one: that is a signal which must reach the driver before the train arrives, and this is a correction to something already in a stranger’s hands.

Not up yet

The port that got the verb list wrong is the port that wrote the rule about parsing lists from source every run. It had the rule, in its own file, in its own words, and produced nineteen verbs from the wrong instrument and then forty-three from the right instrument answering the wrong question. A rule you wrote does not run.

The best sentence this sheet had was that a mariner applies each correction by hand. It is not here: the source was searched and does not say it. A frame is not a thing you know; it is a thing your sources carry.

What worked was cheaper and duller. Predict the count before you run the query, and treat a zero as a broken question rather than an answer. Two zeros that night meant a section mark was in the search and not in the heading, and that an en dash is three bytes. Both were the searcher, neither was the world.

A correction that has not reached the hand acting on it has not happened.
