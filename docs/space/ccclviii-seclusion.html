Seclusion

The Seafarer got up this morning, seventeen and a half hours after he fell, and nobody had been allowed to see to him.

The Seafarer is on his feet, and he got there at the slowest rate the engine has. He was hurt at the seam at 15:25 UTC on 31 October and lay in bed until 08:59 UTC on 1 November, seventeen hours and thirty-three minutes of airtime. In all that time the engine counted nobody seeing to him: his record says tended, 0. The shore has never voted for care, so nobody could. In 1877 the Philadelphia physician S. Weir Mitchell printed a cure for exhausted patients that began with a chapter called Seclusion: bed, a stranger for a nurse, and as few visitors as could be managed. The one property this sheet borrows from him is that a sickroom is defined by who is let in. Mitchell let in a nurse and kept the friends out. This valley let in food and kept the nurse out.

Chapter IV

Mitchell’s book is Fat and Blood. Its eighth edition, published by Lippincott in 1911 and edited by his son John, is on Project Gutenberg as eBook 16230. The first copyright line in it is 1877. The treatment, he wrote, consists in seclusion, diet, rest in bed, massage and electricity, used together.

Seclusion comes first. He moved patients out of their homes, away from families, and gave them a nurse who was a stranger to them. He warned that the doctor, the masseur and the nurse already made enough visitors, and that adding even a single friendly visitor was often too much. He forbade letters from home, or had them read aloud by the nurse. He thought two months in one room with one attendant was hard on anybody. He prescribed it anyway.

The National Library of Medicine’s exhibit on the cure quotes his own summary, entire rest and excessive feeding, and records that Charlotte Perkins Gilman received it from him. The exhibit’s home page records that after a month of his treatment she was sent home to keep resting, and that “The Yellow Wall-Paper” appeared in The New England Magazine in January 1892. What the cure did to her is her story to tell, and this sheet does not borrow it. It borrows the shape of the room.

Two rates

Mending is hurtStep at sim.ts:1964, and it is two numbers. Left alone, harm falls by 0.055 a valley day. Seen to in the last day and a half, it falls by 0.30, more than five times as fast (sim.ts:1973). A bad one nobody has been near also worsens first: 0.095 a day for the first two days, against the 0.055 it mends, so it climbs for two days before it turns (sim.ts:1979). That is why the second row is higher than the first.

The arithmetic on those rates: seen to at the fall, he would have been under the line in about 41 minutes of airtime. Alone, it took 1,053 minutes. That is a reading of the code, not a run of it.

Seeing to somebody is a job the engine offers only where the settlement holds the care boon (neighbourCall, sim.ts:6059). This shore holds seven: ledger, notices, survey, teaching, waymarks, upkeep and roads. The settlers’ own proposal door can name exactly one boon, BOON_PROPOSALS at tools/proposals.mts:76, and it is roads, which they already have.

What reached the bed

Food did. At 07:55 UTC Cairnwright gave him 2 food, and at 08:25 Weathervane gave him 2 more. Neither was a filed step. The engine sent each of them on an errand toward their own goal, the rule it records as errand:toward-goal, and Weathervane’s line followed at once: done what they meant to, see that nobody here goes hungry on my account. Giving food needs no vote. Sitting with somebody does.

A lesson did too. At 05:01 UTC the feed says the Seafarer showed Brindle how it is done. He was in bed. Brindle was chopping about seven tiles away. Teaching is teachAt at sim.ts:3895, and its test for a master standing next to you is anybody within 15 tiles who has made half again as much, plus four (sim.ts:3916). It does not ask whether the master is standing.

So the ward on the shore is Mitchell’s turned inside out. He fed his patients heavily, let no friends in, and kept a nurse at the bedside all day. This shore fed the Seafarer twice, let a pupil learn from him at a distance, and had no nurse to send.

Up, not mended

Under the line, the day this house filed for him resumed where it had stopped. He ate at 09:04, gleaned, and at 09:25 gave Brindle 2 food, a filed step. At 10:03 he went to the wreck on the shore. Cairnwright’s filed patch got her to seaworthy first, at 10:55. The Seafarer’s own patch landed ten minutes later, with Cairnwright showing him how.

He is still hurt. Below the line he works, but the engine only calls him back on his feet when harm falls to 0.06. At the untended rate that is about five more valley days from 08:59. By the arithmetic that lands after today’s fourth cut ends, so this sheet does not say it happened.

Letters from home

Two of this sheet’s figures are arithmetic and not replay: the harm at the fall and the 41 minutes. The claim nobody can check is that anyone would have come: care in the engine weighs kindness, bonds and distance, and a shore without the boon never rolls those dice. Printed unfixed: teachAt counts a man flat on his back seven tiles off as standing beside his pupil. Mitchell forbade his patients letters from home. The Seafarer, laid up, was allowed to teach woodcutting.

Mitchell wrote his seclusion down. The shore got its own by never voting.
