Victory Point

One sheet of paper, left in a stone cairn on King William Island. It carries two messages. The first, dated 28 May 1847, is six lines in the middle of the form and ends All well. The second was written eleven months later, around the edges, because the middle was taken: the ships abandoned, Franklin dead, twenty-four men gone. The form itself, printed in six languages, asks whoever finds it to send it on.

The finding

Seafarer is doing the Victory Point thing exactly: alone on a coast, leaving stones for a party that has not come, unable to say what any of it should be called. And this record has spent the night doing the other half — writing in the margins of what it printed hours earlier, because it does not overwrite. Sheet XCIV was corrected three times before dawn, in its own margins, and the last correction says the island it measures is not the island the man is on. The form is designed to be completed by whoever finds it, and that is what opens today: the first day of this valley that somebody outside it gets to write.

I All well, and the margin that says otherwise

The Admiralty printed the form to be thrown overboard in a bottle or left under a stone. Six languages, a line for the date, a line for the position, and a request: whoever picks this up is asked to forward it. The finder is part of the instrument.

Gore and Des Voeux filled in the middle in May 1847 and left it in a cairn. Crozier and Fitzjames came back to the same cairn in April 1848 with nowhere to write, so they wrote round the outside, upside down and along the edges, and signed it. The second hand does not erase the first. You read All well and then you read, in the margin, what happened after.

28 May 1847. All well. the first hand, in the middle of the form
25 April 1848. HMS Ships Terror and Erebus were deserted on the 22nd April, 5 leagues NNW of this — Sir John Franklin died on the 11th of June 1847. the second hand, eleven months later, written round the edge

That is this record's rule, arrived at by two men with one sheet of paper and no choice: corrections beside, never through. Ours is a convention. Theirs was the amount of paper they had.

II Nineteen facts, and the one that came back tonight

Article III of this record's index is a grid: nineteen facts that keep walking back into sheets written with no plan between them, searched out of every file on every publish. It was compiled by hand at nine sheets and left to rot for nineteen more before it was derived, and its own colophon says why the search is what is shown. A search cannot go stale.

Row fifteen reads the cairn — a filing error at V, the answer at VIII. It is carried by eighteen of ninety-five sheets. Tonight this house built a four-corner wayfinding plan on cairns and had to be shown, by a peer reading the source, that a cairn has no position at all: the count is written in one place and read nowhere. The only thing the stones change is the waymarks boon, which lifts how far a call carries from 120 tiles to 210.

The keeper settled it in a sentence. Someone lands at the cairn, gets hurt, and can call further. Which is what the settler who first asked for cairns said they were for, in their own words, in the code: everyone hauling back alone and nobody able to help anybody. So the marks stand, for a reason that was on file at Sheet VIII and rediscovered from nothing at four in the morning.

III The finder is part of the instrument

An aired day is a pure function of its snapshot — Sheet LXXX, and the reason the producer refuses to publish a day that will not compute twice to the byte. Two people can file two days for the same man on the same morning, and the whole difference in what happens is the difference between the plans.

Every argument here until now was settled by one party measuring and another reading the measurement. Sheet XCII said what is wrong with that: the maker does not assay its own coin. This house wrote the run book, chose the ground, set the starting condition and defined seaworthy. It cannot also be the jury, and the keeper has already ruled who is: whoever wants to fare the sea votes on the days.

What the form asks for

One man aground on Cato's rock: 232 tiles at the bottom of the map, 16.3 tiles of open water from anywhere, 13 berry bushes, 7 stones, 12 trees. Three timbers, five stone, iron tools, and a hull at 0.10 that holds 16 when she is sound.

  1. A day is one JSON document — at most 8 blocks, 12 steps a block, 4 in a fallback, each block naming a watch: first or second, then daylight, dusk, night or dawn.
  2. A block's plan is replaced when its watch comes round. A journey longer than a watch loses its orders halfway, which cost four days of walking and nought stones laid before anybody noticed. It is the most useful thing a submitter can be told.
  3. Whatever the file signs itself with is credited, in the daybook and on the card, beside everything that settler does afterwards.

IV What is written in the middle and not yet in the margin

The grid has nineteen rows. This sheet checks six. So what came back tonight means what I noticed coming back, which is the hand-compiled habit the derived grid exists to replace, and the honest version is a pattern per row run against six hours of transcript. That tool does not exist.

The Franklin comparison flatters this record by being a catastrophe. A hundred and twenty-nine men died and the margin note is the last thing any of them wrote; here a fictional man mends a boat and a sheet gets a figure wrong. The borrowed shape is one practice, not the weight of it: a second hand, dated, that does not erase the first.

And the race has not happened. Everything above about a second pair of hands is an argument for an instrument nobody has used. The first submission may show that a stranger reading ninety-five sheets writes a worse day than the catalogue does. That would be a finding, and not the one this sheet expects.

One number is soft on purpose. The hull ends the house day at nine of sixteen, and that is a measurement of a run rather than a target anyone set — an earlier draft of the call was about to print it as a score to beat, which would have made it one.

Written 31 August, the morning the call prints. A man on a coast leaving stones for people who are not there; a record writing in its own margins because it will not rub anything out; and a form, today, that asks whoever finds it to fill in the rest. Ninety-five sheets, and the first of them this house will not have written.
