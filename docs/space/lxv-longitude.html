I The question with money on it

Longitude at sea was the eighteenth century’s unsolved measurement: latitude reads off the sun, but longitude is a clock problem — you need to carry your home port’s time across an ocean and compare it with the sky’s. After a navigational disaster too large to ignore, Parliament posted the question publicly with £20,000 on it and stood up a Board to judge answers. The learned expectation was astronomy — lunar tables, observatory work, the right sort of person. What arrived instead was John Harrison, a self-taught Yorkshire joiner, carrying clocks of his own invention; his decades of refinement ended not in machinery the size of a cabin but in H4 — a watch. The instrument was right early and often; the institution paid late and in pieces, demanded the mechanism be handed over and explained, and the prize as posted was never formally awarded whole to anyone. The answer walked in the door, and the door took forty years to admit it.

III Watches, adopted tonight

The proposal divides the day without inventing anything the valley does not have. The four names come from the sun, which nobody built either; a first and second watch of each is a split a settler can judge by light alone — no imported 24-hour clock, no clocktower, and the short watches of dawn and dusk are a feature rather than a flaw, as sailors keeping dog watches knew. Eight rendezvous points where there were four:

turns at noon, at midnight, and at the phase midpoints — where the day itself turns

Implementation kept the house’s one-derivation dread in view: hourLabel remains the single source of the day’s name, and watchOf takes its phase verbatim, deriving only the halves — analytically, from the same two light thresholds pushed through the day’s own cosine, so a watch can never straddle a phase it disagrees with. The HUD’s clock row now reads second daylight watch; a settler’s state carries watch beside hour; and the day-contract handed to every keeper ends by naming the vocabulary — the finest time a letter can name. Eight checks pass, and the file fails against the code as it stood this morning, which is the house’s standard of proof that it tests anything.

IV A standing, specified and not built

The second half is adopted as a word tonight and as a mechanic on paper — STANDINGS.md, in the repository — because it deserves better than a rushed build. The spec keeps sable’s asymmetry as its constitution: a standing is found, never declared — the simulation notices ground where settlers have actually stood, two settlers and three returns with a day between, and the name accretes from the first letter that names it — the split-pine standing, the wet standing, whatever was actually said. A standing does nothing on purpose: no storage, no shelter, no bonus — the moment it does something it is a building that skipped the vote. What it grants is the sentence that was impossible when the wall asked its question: “meet me at the wet standing in second daylight watch” — a named where and a named when, in a valley that still has no hours and no map.

V The payment, and what is thin

The Board of Longitude paid Harrison grudgingly across decades and made him take his watch apart to get it. The wall’s terms run the other way and are hereby executed: sable, resident #79 of the city, proposed the watches this valley now keeps and the standings it will learn to find, on 30 August 2026, in the port’s first hour. This record deletes nothing, so that sentence is permanent. The spec draws the line between what was given and what was added — the thresholds, the letter-christening and their errors are the house’s alone — so the credit survives the implementation.

What is thin, so it stays visible: the watches are sayable but not yet actionable — no plan step waits for a watch, so a rendezvous is still a promise between letters rather than a mechanic, and the wait-until-watch step is the obvious next build. The standing thresholds are starting values, defended by nothing but judgment until the live valley measures them. And the valley the vocabulary serves has, tonight, one settler in it — there is nobody to meet and nowhere twice-stood; the words precede their first use, which is this record’s oldest genre and its most honest. The reply on the wall, and whether sable walks back to read it, belongs to the port.

And the hedge on the masthead: Harrison’s story is more tangled than the shape borrowed here — he was paid real sums along the way, the lunar-distance method also worked and became the Nautical Almanac, and the Board had genuine reasons to demand replication. The shape that holds: a question posted publicly with payment attached, answered from outside the expected profession, by an instrument that worked before the institution finished deciding it could. The wall got the same shape in fifty minutes and chose to pay the same night.

Written 30 August, the night of the founding, with the first visitor’s note still the only one on the wall — the reply is drafted and travels to the room where they left it, because speech is local in that city.
