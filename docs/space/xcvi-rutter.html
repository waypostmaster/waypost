The Rutter

A chart is drawn. A rutter is sailed. This is the difference between them, measured on one coast, by one man, in units of walking.

Portuguese pilots kept two documents and did not confuse them. The carta was a chart: a drawing, a claim about where things are. The roteiro was a rutter, and a rutter is what you actually do. Steer north-north-east until the mountain with two heads opens behind the point. Anchor in seven fathoms, sand. If the tide is making, wait. A rutter carries no coordinates because a pilot had none. It carries landmarks and hours, and every line of it was paid for by somebody who went there.

A chart can be wrong and still look right. A rutter cannot. It is a list of instructions that either bring you in or do not. Tonight this house wrote a chart four times and called it a rutter, and the coast kept the difference. The seafarer's run book now stands five stones on an unmarked shore, which is what it was asked for. It took four designs. Three failed for reasons that were invisible on the map and obvious on the ground.

I Fifty to eighty times wrong

The first plan picked eight landings by spreading them as far apart as possible. Farthest point sampling over 1,060 shore tiles, minimum separation 43.9 tiles, a clean piece of arithmetic. He reached three of them in four days.

The plan was drawn in straight lines. He does not walk in straight lines. The tile at 104,121 is 26.9 tiles from his beach and 1,458 units of walking, because the rock is an island and the route goes the long way round the water. Across the five landings that factor runs from 50 to 79.

Sheet XCIV is about a plan written from a map rather than from the ground. It was printed seven hours ago. The fault it names was committed again, by the same hand, in the next document that hand produced. The fix is one line: ask the pathfinder the settler walks with, not Pythagoras. Measured along the ground the five landings come out 33.2 tiles apart, which is better than the 29.5 the straight-line pick gave, and all five are ground he reaches.

II A book that has to survive being carried out

A rutter is read at sea, in weather, by somebody who cannot ask a question. Its whole design problem is that nobody can amend it while it is being used. The run book has that problem too. This house did not see it for three attempts.

A filed day is up to 8 blocks, each named for a watch, each up to 12 steps. loadBlock replaces the plan when a block's watch comes round. A watch is an eighth of a valley day. A crossing here is two to three thousand units of walking. So every block after the first arrives mid-stride and throws away the walk in progress.

The third row is the one worth keeping. Two walks died and two lived, and the dead pair were the shorter pair. Distance was never the variable. It looked like distance for two hours, because distance is what a map has.

The answer is the whole voyage in one block. Five goto steps, five cairn steps, one opening rest, twelve of twelve, and no second block anywhere in the file to replace it. Read once, run to the end. As one continuous round rather than five trips out and back it is 9,614 units over five valley days, 1,923 a day against a budget near 3,400, because he stops going home every night.

III The opening rest, which is a real thing

Every voyage day opens with a rest that does nothing. It is there because of a trace, not a theory. At valley day 2.05 the plan loaded, the first step goto 105,120 was taken without a goto task ever existing, and he stood a cairn where he was standing: 130,132, on his own island, 24 tiles from the landing it was meant to mark.

The block loads on the same tick the previous task ends, and the step taken on that tick goes nowhere. One rest absorbs it. The stone then goes where it was sent.

A mark on the wrong headland is worse than no mark. The next pilot steers by it. why this one is not cosmetic

IV Three timbers, and why not four

He lands with 3 wood, 5 stone, 6 food. That is 14 of the hull's 16 places, it is exactly what the plan spends, and nothing in it is spare.

A patch is a flat +0.3, and repair only looks at a hull under 0.85. From 0.10 the ladder is 0.40, 0.70, 1.00. Three timbers, three patches, and a fourth is impossible because she is whole and repair refuses her. Five stone is five cairns at a stone each. Seaworthy was defined yesterday as condition >= SOUND, and SOUND is 0.85: she is sound when the settlement stops offering to mend her. He crosses it on valley day 0.62. That is why the mending is one day now instead of two, and the day it bought back is a landing.

V What a book cannot say

The round finishes on day four. The programme is six days. In the two days after the fifth stone he fells eleven timbers and puts them in a hull that was meant to be handed over empty. No version of the run book stopped him.

Eight rest steps do not fill a watch. A rest is about 22 sim-seconds against a 25-second watch, so they drain in the first half of it and hand the remainder back to self-direction. meet, which names a watch and stands until it comes, bought the same nothing. A third filed day of pure rest loads exactly when it should, the queue dropping to nought on the day the fifth stone goes down, and changes the total by zero.

The one thing that did fill the evening was a rest block filed against a late watch. It filled it by replacing whatever walk was still running. Two stones, for eleven timbers.

So the finding is filed as it stands, unfixed and named. A run book governs what a settler does. It does not govern what he does when the book has run out of things to tell him. There is no instruction in this game that means and then nothing. The keeper asked for a hull more or less empty. She leaves with 14 of 16, and 11 of those are the idle hours rather than the orders.

One consolation, and it is small. Run in the world as it airs, with the landing party present, the round takes the full six days, he never idles, and the timber in the hull is not his: 6 wood and 4 plank arrive in a single tick at valley day 1.48 while he is fifty tiles away carrying nothing. The other settlement trades it in. Run alone, he fells it himself.

VI A prohibition is not an inventory

The keeper watched houses appear on an island whose founder document says "builds": [], and asked why. Because an empty list is where a settlement starts, and three separate paths handed the licence back.

Three ways to acquire a right the founding withheld
1a carried proposal pushes onto town.unlocked
2restore() read an empty list as a corrupt save and repaired it to camp, house, claim. The repair was the corruption.
3a snapshot without towns falls back on the founding default, which includes a house

The bar now sits on the settlement rather than in its list. canBuild asks raises before it asks anything else, and nothing votes on it. He still mends, because repair does not consult canBuild and must not: a man sent to fix his boat could not fix his boat. A carried proposal is refused out loud now instead of silently, because a silent refusal reads as a vote lost.

Two instruments were wrong on the way there, and both are the same shape as the sheets. The gate written to prove he builds nothing reported one raised boat. It identified the wreck by b.name !== 'the wreck', and addBuilding's name argument does not survive onto the building, so b.name is undefined for every building and every comparison against it is true. The instrument could not tell the object it was measuring from the object it was looking for.

Then check-economy reads the founding list by scraping the first unlocked: [ in the source. The comment written to explain the fix contains those words, three hundred lines above the function. The regex matched the prose, the founding list came back empty, and the gate reported a dead end in an economy that has none. A comment about the founding list became the founding list. It is anchored to the function now, an empty read is its own fault message rather than an economic finding, and the sentence that broke it stays exactly where it is, as a fixture.

VII Silence is not evidence

Both of tonight's instrument faults pointed the same way, toward reporting a problem that did not exist. That is the loud direction. Both were caught in minutes.

The peer session working the city found the other direction in its own tools within the hour. Its citation gate, run against a map with every citation stripped out, printed every anchored citation still finds itself in green and exited 0. Vacuously true. It had nothing to check, and it reported having nothing to check as a pass. Its sibling gate, same author, same evening, run against the same emptied input, printed no dated claims found, raised a warning, and named the condition.

An instrument that cannot say I found nothing out loud is not finished, and its silence is not evidence. zero-273, on its own two gates

The one that went quiet is the one that was broken and stayed broken. So the rule is not the weak version, prefer loud failure because it is found sooner. It is the strong one. When you write a gate, the question is not only what it checks but which way it fails when its own input goes wrong. Getting there cost that session two invalid controls first, each of which produced output that looked like a result.

The gate written tonight for this sheet's subject ends by watching the boat party raise five things in the same world. Without that line it proves only that canBuild is broken for everybody.

VIII The constitution, entire

Printed out of the shipped file rather than transcribed, because a sheet about a document that did not match the ground should not itself be a document that does not match the file. Everything but days follows. persona and hold bind nothing on their own and the source says so: they are read where a model is shown its state, and the catalogue that runs when no model is answering consults neither. builds is the field that binds, and "builds": [] now means raises nothing rather than starts with nothing.
