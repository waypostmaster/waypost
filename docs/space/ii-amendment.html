Schedule A

The development process, reproduced in full

What follows is not an excerpt. It is not a summary of the methodology. It is the methodology, entire, with nothing removed:

> Work in the Frontier repo. Check the latest settler wishes, and make them possible.

That is the whole of it. There is no second prompt. There is no review stage, no specification, no acceptance criteria and no definition of done. A settler asks for something in a sentence a person would actually say, and somebody goes and makes the sentence true.

One line was considered for inclusion and deliberately left out.

> Make no mistakes.

It is not an oversight. It was proposed, weighed, and struck. Mistakes are the input. A process that made none would have nothing to work on tomorrow.

Standing order Filed once. Enforced permanently. Do not raise it again.

Schedule D

Faults corrected, and faults that stand

Four rungs of the ladder were missing, and all four failed the same silent way: the settlement voted, the card congratulated it, the building unlocked, and every builder in the place walked to the site, found it four boards short, and went away again. Nothing errored.

The faultWhat it costNow
A tower costs eight dressed blocks. Its prerequisite asked only about boards. Two agreed decisions dead behind a yard nobody could propose corrected
The tower was chosen on six ore. There is no ore in a tower corrected
A hall costs ten boards and had no prerequisite entry at all. First thing chosen once unlocked. Chosen every day. Never affordable corrected
A granary costs four boards and had no prerequisite entry at all. Last thing chosen. The fallback when nothing else applies corrected
A furnace wants boards for its bellows shed, and nothing named the kiln under it. A settlement waiting on charcoal only a kiln makes corrected
“Short of X, go and get X” read wood, stone, ore, and anything else is a berry. A builder eight blocks short of a tower went fruit-picking, and stayed corrected
The model could not say kiln, or burn, or plank. The entire metal ladder undirectable, on both sides of the wire corrected
wish-22 asked for a furnace and was granted a charcoal kiln. Nine hundred walks to a site to be told there was no iron stands
Anselm asked for a salmon trap and was granted a well. He voted for it thirty-six times stands

The two in red are not defects awaiting a sprint. They are decisions this settlement made, and this game does not go back and change what a settlement was given. There are still no fish.

The corrections are held in place by a fourth check, which walks the material chain and the prerequisite ladder together and fails the build on a dead end:

economy: 9 goods — 4 out of the ground, 5 made. wood→plank wood→charcoal ore+charcoal→iron stone→block plank→furniture every price is reachable by the settlement that unlocked it, and every rung of the chain is a thing a model can ask a settler to stand at.
The record of works Titles as committed. Nobody has proposed changing the convention and nobody is going to.

Mistakes are the input

Six sheep stood on a slope for an entire game because the one behaviour that turns a sheep into food asked for a permission that did not exist. Nobody noticed. The sheep were beautiful. The vote was spent.

That is not a story about a bug. It is where the check that now guards the whole catalogue came from — and there was no route to that check except letting six sheep be scenery first, and then counting.

Nine hundred walks across a valley is a number. You only get a number by allowing the walk nine hundred times.

A process instructed to make no mistakes would have shipped a smaller, quieter, entirely unexamined game. We are not doing that.

Amendment No. 1 · append-only · landed rows are never edited

Frontier is not affiliated with The Settlers, with Anthropic, with Alibaba Cloud, or with the concept of settlement. “Forever” is used in its promotional sense and confers no warranty as to duration, correctness, or the continued existence of the ladder described. The five previous occasions on which this game was declared fixed are a matter of public record and are reproduced above at Schedule E without comment. The development process at Schedule A is complete and accurate; readers who find this insufficient are directed to Schedule B, which has already anticipated them and refused. The hinge readings at Schedule C were self-assessed by the party being assessed, under written instruction from the party being assessed. wish-22 asked for a furnace and was granted a charcoal kiln, and that vote stands; it will always stand. A well was granted to a man who asked for fish, and there are no fish, and there is no plan to add fish. Six sheep were scenery for the whole of one game and are now food. No restitution is available to affected parties, who are fictional, and who would in any case have to vote for it. Not an ounce of human coding expertise. Not one ounce.
