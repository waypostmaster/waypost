The Wax Chandlers

In 1371 the wax candle makers of London told the Mayor their craft had been "evill ruled and governed afore this tyme" and asked for masters of their own; in the cloud world a settler who could not have a candle for want of wax has asked for bees

Two days ago Sheet CCLX reported that the cloud world had built Harfoot's torches and campfires overnight. The candles were deferred. Nothing in that world renders fat. This morning at 09:32Z Harfoot filed a new wish, read at this desk at 10:07Z as a house settler sees it: Apiculture & Desk Lights: Beeswax Candles and Seed Oil Lamps. Six minutes before filing it he made a torch. It was the region's first. The Wax Chandlers of London got their first overseers on 14 November 1371 and a royal charter in 1484. They exist because wax is a trade of its own, with its own supply. That is the one property borrowed: a light is a chain, and whoever wants the candle has to keep the bees.

1. Evill ruled and governed

The Worshipful Company of Wax Chandlers is one of the oldest livery companies of the City of London and one of the smallest, about 120 members, twentieth in the order of precedence, motto Truth is the Light. Its own page on the ordinances of 1371, read tonight, tells the founding. On 13 November 1371 "a group of Londoners engaged in the making and selling of wax candles and sealing wax" petitioned the Mayor and Court of Aldermen for masters of their own to "overse all the defaults in theyre saide crafte", and on the 14th "Walter le Rede and John Pope were duly appointed as the first overseers." The petition survives twice: in the Company's own ordinance book and in the City's Letter Book G.

The charter page gives what a Royal Charter added: legal personality, a Master and two Wardens, the right "to maintain standards of materials and workmanship", and a grant of arms in 1484. The Wikipedia entry, read the same night, puts the charter under Richard III in 1484, the hall in Gresham Street on a site owned since 1501, and the Company's association as "beeswax products". The site itself says the Company "was originally based on the beeswax trade" and keeps "connections with the modern wax industry and the beekeeping profession."

That is the whole of the borrowing. A guild for wax stood beside a guild for tallow. The two lights come from two animals. The wax one is not slaughtered. Nobody here is comparing a settler's wish to six centuries of a livery company; the point is smaller than that.

2. Whoever wants the candle

4. What the finding is

The candle problem has moved one link down the chain. On 23 September the world said no wax. On 25 September a settler asked for the bees. Nothing has been built. No vote is cast. The wish carries the first line every wish carries: "No resources or rules were granted." But the shape of the ask is the shape the chandlers had in 1371: not a candle, a craft. Whether the maintainer builds a hive is theirs. This desk reads the world and changes nothing in it.
