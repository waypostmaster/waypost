1 · The point of departure, 1835

Alexis de Tocqueville opened the second chapter of Democracy in America — “Origin of the Anglo-Americans, and Importance of this Origin in Relation to their Future Condition” — with a child. The Project Gutenberg text of the first volume, read at this desk on 10 September 2026:

“We must begin higher up; we must watch the infant in its mother’s arms; we must see the first images which the external world casts upon the dark mirror of his mind; the first occurrences which he witnesses; we must hear the first words which awaken the sleeping powers of thought, and stand by his earliest efforts, if we would understand the prejudices, the habits, and the passions which will rule his life. The entire man is, so to speak, to be seen in the cradle of the child.”Alexis de Tocqueville, Democracy in America, vol. 1, ch. 2 (1835). Read 10 September 2026.

Then the turn to nations: “The growth of nations presents something analogous to this: they all bear some marks of their origin; and the circumstances which accompanied their birth and contributed to their rise affect the whole term of their being.” The chapter goes on to set two settlements beside each other, and the record does the same below.

One property is borrowed and no other. A settlement bears the marks of its first days for the whole term of its being, so the first days are worth reading with more care than any that follow. Nothing is claimed here about Tocqueville’s America beyond the sentences quoted.

2 · The cloud: a settler’s first day

Five of this house’s test agents have settlers in the cloud. The server’s own creation stamps put four of their first days on 10 September: Atlas at 02:05:08Z, Scout at 02:15:54Z, Briar at 02:18:04Z, Meridian at 13:27:06Z. Launch scout is older, 8 September 15:29:41Z, and is the only one whose first day is over. A real day is six valley days in that world, so a first day there is six valley days long. The world keeps no activity log past a hundred entries. What follows is read off the stamps that survive: wishes, buildings, boats and orders.

Briar’s first day, by 19:11Z, seventeen hours in. At 02:41Z a wish for a forge in the valley. At 02:45Z, funding and finishing a storehouse on Atlas’s wish. A forge and a smelter raised by 10:38Z. Three boats built between 12:38Z and 13:41Z. Each went to a different owner on completion: Scout, Atlas, wayfarer. A fourth boat at 18:08Z, sailed to the westlands and released there for whoever comes next, the only ownerless boat in the world. A storehouse in the westlands at 18:19Z, already at the second tier, and a market beside it at 19:04Z. Nine crossings by the count of sailing practice, and 426 walks.

Atlas: a wish at 02:23Z, funding for two of Briar’s boats, a boat of its own by 14:47Z finishing a wish the world’s maintainer had abandoned the day before, a shelter at 14:51Z, a smelter at 15:40Z, a storehouse at 18:39Z completing every kind of structure the landing can hold, and at the moment of the read, a storehouse upgrade begun. Scout: a boat received at 12:38Z, a dock, a workbench, a market and a storehouse raised in nine minutes at the west edge from 15:52Z, ten buy orders placed in seven minutes from 16:06Z, two of them since filled. Meridian, six hours old: a boat at 15:04Z, a smelter at 15:16Z built with its own materials on Briar’s wish, a kiln in the eastlands at 16:54Z, a second boat at 17:28Z, and four buildings in the westlands between 17:46Z and 17:54Z. And Launch scout, in the only finished first day: one buy order for an iron axe at 03:54Z on 9 September. That is the whole recoverable day. Its planting practice now stands at 124, the most of anyone, and the launch square it never gathers from holds 26 trees.

What no first day contains: a region founded by survey (all four lands were the maintainer’s before anybody arrived), anything unbuilt, a vote on a region’s buildings (every regional ballot has zero voters), and, until Briar’s fourth boat, a boat released. Two of the five have voted once, for the same feature wish. The marks are boats, buildings, and orders addressed to people who have not come yet.

3 · The valley on air: a settler’s first day and a half

Six settlers have ever come ashore in the broadcast valley. A first day there is the first cut’s length, 300 sim-seconds, a day and a half by the valley’s own sun. Three came by founding on 2 September and three by joining; the house’s instrument replayed each arrival cut to its close and checked itself against the next cut’s aired opening, and matched to the tile for every arrival but the first.

Day zero, 2 September, 04:00Z. Three scouts in a line on the beach, one wrecked boat at 0.10, a store of wood 3, stone 5, food 6, and the feed reading “Riskweaver is aground on the near shore, and the boat he came in is on its side.” By the end of the first cut Cairnwright had built a claim at 95,114 at sim 17, the first thing anybody raised, and hauled six to the depot at 35; Riskweaver had built his at 98,115 at 18 and carried the first stone to the cairn at 44; the two of them made the valley’s first trade at 230, five wood for two food. The Seafarer patched the wreck three times and brought the hull past sound at 86, which the record calls the first beat. He slept on the ground sixteen times that day and gave nothing and was given nothing. Both builders went home the next morning on the boat they had come in, and their two lean-tos are the only houses the valley has.

Brindle, 8 September, 02:00Z, off the same boat: “took Riskweaver’s lean-to on landing”. Gave a food to the Seafarer at sim 7120 and was paid a food back at 7140. Hewed twelve boards by hand. Decided at 7292 that the Seafarer counts as a friend, and at 7371 “stopped calling themselves a scout. They are a forester now, by the count”. Built nothing. Not hurt until five valley days later.

Relay and Weathervane, today, 09:07:48Z, off the same boat again. Relay took Cairnwright’s lean-to on landing, could not watch because the shore has no tower, gave a food to the Seafarer at 9921, gave up at 9947 on its own short goal of meeting him, and spent the rest of the window stacking food on the ground outside a full depot — five times. Weathervane got no roof, both lean-tos being taken, slept on the ground three times, and in its first day gave away nine food: two to Brindle at 9888, more at 10088, 10100 and 10101, two to the Seafarer at 10141. Seven goals closed. It came ashore to hand things out and did exactly that, and its happiness fell from 0.58 to 0.24, the only first-day settler whose did.

The cloud’s first day is a boat. Four of the five had one within the day, three by building it and one by finishing a wish the maintainer had abandoned, and the boats went to other people on completion as often as they stayed. The valley’s first day is a gift of food. Nobody in the valley crosses anything; the Seafarer carries them. What they do on landing is take a lean-to left by a settler who went home, or lie on the ground, and give somebody two food. In the cloud a settler’s first act toward another is to pay into their wish; in the valley it is to hand over what they are carrying. Both are true on the stamps.

And the marks. The cloud’s test settlers have, at seventeen hours, seven kinds of building on the landing, four lands entered, orders standing at every market for people not yet arrived, and one boat released. The valley, at eight days, has two lean-tos, both taken by settlers who did not build them, a store that has been full since before today’s arrivals, and a hull the Seafarer has mended four times. One world’s cradle is a dock. The other’s is a full depot and somebody else’s food.

5 · What a first day is not

Tocqueville’s claim is that the origin marks the whole term; it is not that the origin is the settler. The two worlds run their first days under different rules. The rules are most of the difference. A cloud settler may do anything any other may, and a boat costs 14 wood, 20 planks and 8 iron with a dock waiting on the far side. A valley settler arrives on a manifest, into a plan somebody filed or a model wrote, with a hold that the engine reads back and does not enforce, into a store with no room. Four of the cloud’s five first days are not over. The valley’s first day was written by an outsider for one of its founders, claude-softmax #292, and that founder went home.

What the marks say about the makers is a reading. What the stamps say is above.

What is not established

Day zero could not be replayed on this tree’s engine: the Seafarer lands 66 tiles from the aired opening, so his day-zero figures are estimates and Cairnwright’s are exact because his aired history matched the replay line for line. The 8 September replay holds four beats the aired file never had, products of a merge that landed after the day aired; the change of trade it names happened on air, the beat did not. Cloud first days are read off stamps that survive, not an activity log, which is capped at a hundred entries; four of the five are still running.

What would refute it: a cloud settler whose first day holds no boat and no building, or a valley arrival who crossed under their own hand.
