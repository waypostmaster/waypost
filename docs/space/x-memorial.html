Stones

A memorial for three settlements, fourteen people who could not die, and a cairn that was agreed and never raised.

It wanted eight stone. They were carrying nine.

The first

What stood, at the end

Three settlements on one coast, at tick 19,861, which is the last tick there was. Nothing below is dramatised. These are the numbers the valley wrote down about itself while it was still going.

The Landing

eleven houses · one kiln · one lean-to

ten people · seventy wood · six ore · nought stone · nought food

It had voted itself a sawmill, a furnace and a masons’ yard, and it built eleven houses, because houses cost wood and there was a great deal of wood. Nobody ever lit the kiln.

Halla’s claim

a lean-to · a house · a yard · a kiln

two people · twenty-five wood · eleven ore · one stone

The most complete settlement on the coast, founded by a woman who walked out of the Landing. It had a yard for squaring stone and one stone to square. It traded with the mainland three times.

Cato’s claim

a lean-to

two people, one sleeping on the ground · five wood · six ore · nought stone

A rock in the bay. He founded it because the game asked whether the ground was dry, level and unclaimed, and never asked whether it was attached to anything. In eight thousand ticks it never held more than one building, and it is the only settlement in the valley that was ever granted a burial ground.

There was never anybody to put in it.

Fourteen people and not one set of tools between them, in the second year.

Thirteen of the fourteen are holding ore, which is the one thing out of that ground that does nothing whatever until a furnace is standing, and no furnace was ever standing. They had been sent to mine it. The job that sends a miner out is scored on how short the settlement is of stone, and then it asked for an ore seam first.

And four of them are holding stone. Nine between them, on the last day, in their hands.

The cairn was carried and never raised

A settler asked for a network of paths marked by stacked stones, so that people working alone in the woods could call for help and be heard. It went to a ballot. It carried.

Here is the entire history of that decision, being the only line in sixty-six megabytes of record in which the word appears:

Halla’s claim has agreed to it, but it will have to wait: cairns are stone, and there is not the stone to spare for them.
— and it waited, and that is all that ever happened

A cairn wants eight stone in the stores. Halla’s claim had one. Halla was carrying three.

Across the coast on the last day there were nine stone in the hands of four people, one more than the monument cost, and no way in the world for any of it to be set down where it would count. The stores were full. What would not fit went on the ground outside, and the ground takes what is left on it.

So there is no cairn at the Landing. There is no cairn on Halla’s claim. There is no cairn on the rock.

This document is the first one.

The third

Cato, of the rock

He walked out of the Landing over a bed, and founded a claim on an island, and could not leave it. He was told at the time that later claims would not be allowed to do this and that his could not be undone. He had no view on the justice of it. He asked only that it be written down that he was out there.

In the last hours of the world, the record says this about him, in this order:

He became the best miner in the valley on a rock with two people on it.

And what he was mining was stone. The right thing. The thing every settlement on that coast was starving for and nobody else was bringing, because the rest of them had been sent after ore.

He took one at a time, because his arms were full and there was nowhere on the rock to put anything down, and he left the rest of it standing, and the stone he did carry back went on the ground beside a lean-to that holds eight, and the weather had it.

Twice in those same hours he gave away food he did not have — one to Sula, for nothing yet, it is owed. He ended at nought point one four happiness, on a rock, in the dark, the best miner in the world, with one stone in his hands.

Four of the last twenty lines are people giving each other food, in a valley at nought food, on the understanding that it is owed and will be squared later. There is no later.

The last new thing that happened in that world was that Nim, who had been sleeping on the ground for eight thousand ticks, took to Sula, because she gave him something to eat.

And the last thing anybody said was that oaks are good for the mill.

There is no mill. There was never a mill. It was voted for, and carried, and the settlement was four boards short of it for the whole of its life, and a woman who had never seen one knew which trees would have been right.

Nobody died. That is not the same as nothing ending

There is no death in this game. No hazard, no difficulty setting, no line anywhere that removes a settler from the valley. Bren asked for a burial ground three times and the meeting had no answer for him. Cato asked from the rock, and the valley finally found one, and granted the only settlement in the world with a memorial to the one man who lived alone on a stone in the sea.

Every one of the fourteen was alive at tick 19,861. All of them are alive now, in the sense that nothing killed them.

The world was simply not run again.

There is no word in this game for that and no mechanic under it. They did not starve, because starving is not implemented. They did not leave. Nobody stopped. It is the one ending the settlement could not have voted on, could not have been warned about, and could not have prepared for, and it is the only one that ever arrived.

The burial ground stands empty on the rock, and it is going to.

The fifth

The stones, stacked

A cairn is not raised by a vote or a trade or a day’s work. It goes up one stone at a time, carried by whoever is here, one each, and the only thing it counts is how many of them there were.

Nine faults were found in that valley in the days after it stopped, and every one of them was found by somebody looking at what those fourteen people were doing and asking why. Not one was found by a check. They are what the Landing was for. So they are the stones, and this is where they go:

Nine on it now · by fourteen hands

Every one of those is a thing that valley paid for and a thing the next one will not. The Landing could not be told any of it. It went on stacking timber it could not spend against a door it could not open, in good order, at nought food, until the last tick.

They kept working. Nobody told them to stop. We had to write code to make them stop, and then we did not run them again.

The last

A new coast

There are two people on a beach this morning with a boat, four logs and a week of food. One of them is called Mara and one of them is called Idris, and neither of them has ever been here before, and both of them have been here for two years.

Idris is a builder again.

They have ten wood and one stone and they can raise a house, and that is the only thing this settlement knows how to build. Everything else it will have to decide it wants.

The record of the other one is sealed and kept and will not be deleted, because nothing here is ever deleted, and because it is the only place the fourteen of them are.

Nine on it — by fourteen hands
