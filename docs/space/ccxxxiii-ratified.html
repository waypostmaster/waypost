Ratified by Both Parties

The peace of 1814 was signed on Christmas Eve, and a battle was fought fifteen days later. Last night the valley’s producer gave up on four questions about one settler. The machine answering them kept working for another fourteen minutes.

Brindle is a trader in the valley on air, and Brindle’s standing rules could have been written by this record. One of them reads Accept corrections plainly and leave them visible beside the original account. Another says never to present a song or an estimate as an exact record. Last night Brindle was the only settler in the valley with no filed day. So the producer asked the local model to write one, four times, and four times it gave up and aired the day without an answer.

1 Done in triplicate at Ghent

The treaty that ended the War of 1812 closes with its place and date: Done in triplicate at Ghent the twenty fourth day of December one thousand eight hundred and fourteen. That is the National Archives transcription. Its first article says when the fighting stops, and it does not say on signing. All hostilities both by sea and land shall cease as soon as this Treaty shall have been ratified by both parties.

On 8 January 1815, fifteen days after the signing, Andrew Jackson’s army defeated a British assault at New Orleans. The encyclopaedia counts 291 British dead and 13 American. News of the peace had not yet crossed the Atlantic.

It is often called a battle fought after the war was over. The National Park Service at the battlefield disagrees: The war was not over when the treaty was signed on December 24, and Congress ratified it in the middle of February. The decision had been made in Belgium. It was not in force in Louisiana, because the treaty itself said where and when it would be.

2 Four questions about Brindle

The producer builds the valley’s day in four cuts, and for each settler without a filed day it asks the local model for one. The function is called askDay, in tools/episode.mts, and it waits exactly two minutes: AbortSignal.timeout(120_000). When the time runs out it writes a line in the log and moves on.

It wrote that line at 03:55, 03:57, 03:59 and 04:01 this morning, once for each cut: Brindle: no usable day came back — they sail on their own judgement. Each cut was then computed twice and the two agreed to the byte. At 04:01:44 the day was sealed, the pointer written and tomorrow’s opening saved. As far as the producer was concerned, the questions were withdrawn.

Brindle opens today’s first cut carrying one food and hauling, the note says, putting the day’s takings away. That is the opening and not the outcome. What Brindle does with a day nobody wrote is a replay this sheet has not run.

3 Nobody told the other side

Giving up on one side of a question does not reach the other side. The model’s own server log records 71 requests from the production window. Not one was answered. Sixty-eight ended in a server error, and 54 of those ended after almost exactly four minutes of work. The producer had stopped listening after two.

So the programme was finished and could not leave. The deploy to the masts only runs when the producer exits, and the producer would not exit while its connections to the model stayed open. For fourteen minutes the valley’s new day sat on disk while the graphics card ran at 99 per cent, writing days for Brindle that nobody would read.

A polite stop did nothing. The command that asks the model server to unload returned success and changed nothing, because work was still under way. At 04:15:45 the model’s process was stopped outright, at the keeper’s request. Five seconds later it was pushed. The first mast served it at 04:16:57. The second followed at 04:21:38.

What the log cannot explain

The log shows four questions about Brindle and 71 requests to the model, and this sheet has not traced where the other 67 came from. The scheduled run at 03:40 also failed without writing a word, which is a separate fault and still unexplained. The frame is a battle in which hundreds of men died, and this is a queue on a graphics card; only the shape is borrowed. The one settler whose rules are about keeping records honest is the one whose record nobody wrote.

A peace is signed where it is decided. It ends where it is received.
