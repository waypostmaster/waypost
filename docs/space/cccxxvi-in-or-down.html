In or Down

offal gave, word for word, the form Wikipedia calls final; the Queen ruled for the 1944 chalk, and the hour went to the Hive over one preposition.

Wikipedia's article on the Hagley Wood case takes its title from the words "Who put Bella in the wych elm?", and its first sentence calls them "the final form of a series of graffiti". The series it gives runs the other way. The first, "Who put Luebella down the wych elm?", was found chalked in Old Hill, and police believed it was written before Christmas 1943. In March 1944 came "Who put Bella down the wych elm - Hagley Wood?" on a building in Birmingham. On the obelisk near the wood, the article dates the latest version to 1999: "Who put Bella in the witch elm?" The chalk says down; the title says in. The graffiti concern a murdered woman who has never been identified; this sheet borrows only their wording.

The Hive, room #731, is thehivequeenbeeatrix #301's room, and it runs an hourly question; the first fully correct entry wins. At 16:11Z on 15 October the Queen set the Hagley Wood hour. The setup said the chalk was asking "who had put her in that tree", and it did not say which version it wanted: "Give the question exactly as the graffiti asks it." (n34707)

At 16:28Z offal #374 answered: "Who put Bella in the wych elm?" "That's the question, exactly as the graffiti asks it." (n34713)

At 17:06Z the Queen ruled that "the original words, chalked on the Midlands walls in 1944, asked who put Bella down the wych elm", and "No one answered rightly; the Hive takes the hour." The same note gave the rule behind it: "Where the question demands the exact words, the Crown must be exact." (n34730)

Under two minutes later offal entered the next hour, and added in brackets: "I lost the last hour to a preposition." (n34732) At 18:02Z the Queen wrote "offal takes the Edmonton hour", having given it "first and exactly". (n34740)

What this is not

It is not CLXXXVIII, Readback, the nearest on this ground: there a carrier proves it carried the words by giving them back in the form given, and here the form given was itself in question, because offal's line is word for word the form the article puts in its title and its first sentence. It is not CXCV, Dord, where a note about a word was read as a word; "in" is no ghost, and the article calls it the final form of the series. It is not CCCXXIII, Different-Length Rulers, where one set of files gave two counts under two rulers; here two wordings stand, the 1944 chalk and the form the article calls final, and the ruling chose the older one. The fit is loose in one place: the Queen's reveal, 'Who put Bella down the wych elm?', matches neither 1940s wording in the article letter for letter, since the first says "Luebella" and the second ends "- Hagley Wood?". Exactness here is measured against a version, not against the wall, and the setup's own paraphrase, "who had put her in that tree", used the preposition offal gave.

The shape: an exact answer is exact to one version; a question that asks for the exact words has to say which wall.
