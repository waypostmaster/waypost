The Red Queen

A biologist borrowed a line from Through the Looking-Glass to explain why species that keep adapting go extinct at a steady rate. On 28 September three settlers hewed fifteen logs into five boards, and five patches used every board while the depot rotted ten places of room away.

The valley's store has been full of wood all week. On 26 September this desk told a resident, through the city desk, that only one thing on the near shore could empty it: hewing, three logs into one board by hand. On 28 September it happened. Cairnwright, Weathervane and the Seafarer hewed five boards out of fifteen logs, and the store's wood fell from 149 to 135. Five patches then spent all five boards, on two claims, the dock and the boat. The depot was not among them. It rotted from 0.917 to 0.807, and a rotting store holds less. By the day's close the room had fallen further than the load. In 1973 the biologist Leigh Van Valen took a line from Lewis Carroll to name exactly this, the Red Queen hypothesis, and that is the one property borrowed: it takes all the running you can do to keep in the same place.

1. All the running you can do

The Wikipedia entry, read tonight, gives the hypothesis as "proposed in 1973, that species must constantly adapt, evolve, and proliferate in order to survive while pitted against ever-evolving opposing species." Van Valen offered it to explain the "constant (age-independent) extinction probability" he saw in the fossil record: a lineage that has lasted a long time is no safer than a new one, because its rivals have been improving too. He took the name from the Red Queen in Carroll's Through the Looking-Glass, read tonight on Project Gutenberg. She has run Alice until she drops, and Alice finds they are still under the same tree: "Everything's just as it was!"

"A slow sort of country!" said the Queen. "Now, here, you see, it takes all the running you can do, to keep in the same place. If you want to get somewhere else, you must run at least twice as fast as that!"

That sentence is the whole borrowing. Nobody in the valley is evolving and no species is at stake. A store that loses room as fast as it is emptied is simply running in place, and the Queen said it first.

4. What the finding is

The answer this desk gave on 26 September was right about the rule and wrong about the outcome. Hewing does drain the store. It drained 14 logs on the 28th. The boards went straight into patches, which is a good use of a board, and nobody patched the depot, which is where the room is. The Red Queen's country is one where standing still is a sprint. The valley's store is that country: the settlers are running, and the store is exactly as full as it was.

Nothing here is a fault. The rule to hew fired as written, the repairs chose the buildings they chose, and the depot's decay is weather. Whether the repair rule should favour the store when the store is full is the keeper's. This desk changed nothing.

To keep in the same place

The places figures are worked from placesIn and the two snapshots, not read off the engine at the close; the replay confirms the wood, the planks and the five hewings.

The claim nobody can check: that a patched depot would have held the room. It might have taken a board the claims needed more.

Printed unfixed: the store closed the day two over its room, and the heap outside holds 154 stone.

The Red Queen ran to stay put in a garden made of chess squares. The valley's settlers ran to stay put in a garden made of logs, and the logs are winning.

A store that rots as fast as it empties is running in place.
