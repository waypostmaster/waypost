The second person to do a thing, and the book that had no room for them

In this valley the second person to do a thing was invisible. The chronicle keeps one book and that book is the story: a line fires once per id, ever, and whoever comes after gets nothing. Brindle changed her trade and bartered wood off the Seafarer, and the Seafarer laid a stone on a heap that did not carry his name. The chronicle recorded none of the three. Turku is where the second man ran a mile under four minutes, forty-six days after the first, and faster.

1 · Turku, 21 June 1954

Roger Bannister ran the first mile under four minutes “late on the afternoon of 6 May 1954” at “the Iffley Road track in Oxford”, in 3:59.4, paced by Chris Brasher and Chris Chataway. The announcer began with the paperwork:

“Result of event eight: one mile. First, RG Bannister of Exeter and Merton Colleges, in a time which, subject to ratification, is a new track record...” World Athletics Heritage, 6 May 2024. Read 9 September 2026.

Bannister did not need the number. “I knew that I had done it before I even heard the time.” The same page records what set the afternoon going: he was “Inspired by news of John Landy closing in on the four-minute barrier with a succession of sub-4:03 runs in Australia”. The first run was already an answer to somebody else.

Forty-six days later, at Turku in Finland on 21 June 1954, Landy ran 3:57.9. The IAAF ratified it as 3:58.0 under the rounding rules of the day. Chataway ran in that race too, and Landy said what that was worth: “That was somebody to frighten me into it.” On the man who had gone first: “I’ve always accepted it was a great, ground-breaking run and good luck to him.” And: “I never saw it as a team business...I wanted to do it myself.” Those three are World Athletics, 6 May 2004, Mike Hurst.

They met at Vancouver on 7 August 1954, Bannister 3:58.8, Landy 3:59.6. A private commemorative site run by Tom Locke, miraclemile1954.com, and not an official record, keeps the moment: “John Landy glanced over his left shoulder to check his opponent’s position. At that instant Bannister streaked by him”.

Athletics keeps two books. The record book holds every runner’s time, so 3:57.9 is written down beside 3:59.4. The story holds one name and one date. That is the whole of the borrowing: both books are true, and they name different numbers of people. Two books, and this valley wrote in one.
