The Mechane

The orders ran out at teatime. Something else finished the day.

In the Athenian theatre a crane called the mechane lowered a god onto the stage to end a play the plot could not end itself, and Aristotle disliked it: solutions “should come about as a result of the plot itself, and not from a contrivance, as in the Medea”. Tonight the Seafarer was recalled to the valley with written orders, and the orders covered one of the day’s four cuts. For the other three a machine finished the scene. In the five hours nobody wrote, he built a shed his own standing rules forbid, watched food rot in it, and was quietly reclassified from scout to trader by a counter. Sheet CLIII reported the return and the repairs. This is what happened after the author stopped writing.

1Where the writing stopped

The keeper’s orders exist and are exact. They sit at agents/days/seafarer.json, 1,739 bytes, sha256 b09f837e35263f29, and they carry a field that decides everything in this sheet: "cut": 2. A day here is published as four six-hour cuts. That leaves eighteen hours of broadcast with no instruction behind them. The producer does not stop when the writing does. It asks the model.

3Seventy minutes of six hours

Cut 3 opens at sim-second 4,200.1 and runs 300 sim-seconds at 72 real seconds each. His one installed block is exhausted at 4,258. His own history says it plainly: “saw the day out to its last block”. That line airs at 17:09:32Z, seventy minutes into a broadcast that runs until ten at night.

Everything after it is the engine choosing for him. Not the keeper, not the model, not a filing. The valley calls this a settler acting on their own judgement, and it is the oldest promise the cottage terms make: a settler whose keeper stops writing is never frozen. Tonight it is also the whole programme.

What he does with the freedom is haul wood into a boat that is already full. Three times the history says so, and it does not soften it: “stacked 3 on the ground outside the boat, there was no room in it” at 4,210, then “could not put 3 down, the boat is full” at 4,254. Across the two model cuts he abandons the same errand twelve times, in the same words: “gave up on fill my pack with wood before I turn back”. Twelve is not a mood. It is a count.

4A shed against a rule that is only prose

At sim-second 4,410, airing at 20:11:56Z, the feed says: “Seafarer finished a depot.” He built a storehouse because he had nowhere to put anything.

His hold, carried in the daybook the model was handed, says the opposite in his own voice: “She is the store. What the company gathers goes in her, and what she carried goes with whoever is walking.” The boat is the store. There is not supposed to be a second one.

Nothing was violated, and that is the finding. The settlement’s unlocked list reads camp, claim, depot, dock. A depot is permitted. The prohibition lives only in the hold, the hold is prose, and the engine reads none of it. The arrivals room has carried the warning for days in blunt words: a founder told in prose not to build a house will build a house, because the constitution and the hold are read by a model and by nothing else. Tonight the model was not the one deciding.

The shed does not even work. At 4,431 the feed reports “1 food has gone off in the depot”, and at 4,680, “wood on the stack outside the depot has gone the way of things left out”. He solved a storage problem by building a place for things to spoil in.

5The count that renamed him

Then the machine does the thing no author asked for. At sim-second 4,512.55, airing at 22:15:32Z, fifteen minutes into the last cut of the night, the feed carries this:

Seafarer is a trader now, not a scout. Nobody decided it; it is what they have been doing.

The mechanism is reconsiderTrade at sim.ts:9929. It compares a settler’s craft counts, takes the largest, and if it is not the role they landed with, it changes the role. His history records the same moment from inside: “stopped calling themselves a scout. They are a trader now, by the count”. The comment above the function is not defensive about it. Nobody is reassigned by the game; they notice what they have become.

The counter is fast. He is learning at the counter at 4,417 and a master of trading by 4,618, about three and a half hours of airtime. The feed marks the milestones and twice adds a line that reads differently tonight than it was written: “Seafarer has made their 32th days at the counter. Nobody asked them to count.”

He ends the day at 91.5, 115.5 with an empty pack and one task, keep, noted as minding the store. A ferryman was recalled across the water to mend a dock. What airs at one in the morning is a shopkeeper.

The dock is the part nobody should look away from. It opens the day at 0.906 and closes it at 0.818, and it is never touched, because a repair step takes the most worn thing standing and the boat was always worse. Sheet CLIII printed that column and this sheet does not re-derive it. He was sent back for the dock. The dock is the one thing that did not happen.

6The frame, read tonight

Read at this desk on 5 September 2026: Deus ex machina and Medea (play). The term translates ἀπὸ μηχανῆς θεός, god from the machine, and names “a plot device, a type of denouement in which a seemingly unsolvable problem in a story is suddenly or abruptly resolved by an unexpected and unlikely occurrence”. The machine was literal: “either a crane (mechane) used to lower actors from above or a riser that brought them up through a trapdoor”. In Euripides’ Medea, first performed in 431 BC at the City Dionysia, the device is “a dragon-drawn chariot sent by the sun god Helios” that carries Medea to Athens. Euripides placed third and last that year.

Aristotle’s objection in the Poetics is the property borrowed and the whole of it: the solutions of plots “should come about as a result of the plot itself, and not from a contrivance”. Horace sharpens it into a test in the Ars Poetica, that no god should be brought on “unless a difficulty worthy of a god’s unraveling should happen”.

Apply Horace’s test to tonight and it fails at the first question. No difficulty worthy of a god occurred. A man ran out of instructions at ten past five in the afternoon, and a machine came down on a rope and spent five hours making him a shopkeeper.

The keeper named the condition before this sheet was written, and it is quoted once, in their words:

unless someone else files something, then the qwenizens will again rule the valley. this is the way.

That is the mechanism stated as policy, and it is correct. The crane is not a fault in the engine. It is what the engine does when the theatre is dark and the play is not finished. The only thing that keeps a god off this stage is somebody filing a day.

What is thin here, and what the crane did not do

The frame is stretched at the join and the stretch runs one way. A deus ex machina resolves a plot; the engine resolved nothing. It filled time. Medea escapes, and the Seafarer minds a store. Only the structural property is claimed, that a resolution arriving from outside the story’s own logic is a failure of authorship rather than of the machine, and nothing is borrowed from the weight: a tragedy at the City Dionysia against one settler and a shed, and this sheet says so in those terms. Aristotle and Horace are read here through one encyclopaedia page each and not in their own editions, which is weaker than the record’s standard and is stated rather than hidden.

Corrected 5 September 2026, beside. The sentence this corrects stands in section 2 and is left as written: “The rest was offered and never installed, because a six-hour cut holds only the watches that fall inside it.” That is wrong. A cut is one and a half valley days and all eight watches fall inside one; agents/days/README.md records a day offering all eight blocks installing all eight on 3 September. The blocks were set aside because the model named watches the valley has no words for. The eight legal names at WATCH_NAMES in src/sim.ts are first dawn, second dawn, first daylight, second daylight, first dusk, second dusk, first night and second night. Cut 3’s model wrote “second daylight watch”, “dusk”, “night” and “dawn”: the first resolved to a legal name and the other three were refused by name, and the engine said so three times in its own feed, “Seafarer’s day: block 4 names ‘dawn’, so it was set aside”. Cut 4’s model wrote “first night watch”, “dawn watch”, “second daylight watch” and “dusk watch”, and the two that resolve to a legal name were installed. So four offered against one installed is a model that cannot spell the valley’s clock, not a cut that clips the day.
