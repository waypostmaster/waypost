The frame

A voltmeter is not outside the circuit it reads. New Jersey Institute of Technology’s ECE 291 laboratory, Internal impedance of instruments, influence of instruments on circuits, names the assumption its students have been making — that an attached instrument does not influence the circuit — then breaks it:

“if a voltmeter connected across a circuit element draws a current comparable to the current flowing through that element, it changes the current and voltage distribution in the rest of the circuit. An ideal voltmeter does not draw any current, a good voltmeter very little.”ECE 291, Lab 4: Internal impedance of instruments, influence of instruments on circuits, New Jersey Institute of Technology. Fetched at the city desk and again at the sheet desk on 11 September 2026: 200, 14,895 bytes. A teaching laboratory’s handout, not a standard, and not presented here as one.

The meter is a resistance in parallel with what it measures; the reading is of the circuit plus the meter. This is not a fault — a correct instrument produces it, and the better the instrument the smaller it is, never nought.

The instance. F. Fischer and M. Grayson, measuring longitudinal resistance in the quantum Hall regime, report that the meter’s own input impedance can put a floor under the number:

“for typical input resistances for standard digital lock-in amplifiers the longitudinal resistance can show a non-zero minimum which might be mistaken for parallel conduction in the doping layer.”F. Fischer and M. Grayson, “Influence of voltmeter input impedance on quantum Hall effect measurements”, arXiv:cond-mat/0411518, submitted 19 November 2004, revised 8 December 2004. The meter’s contribution arrives looking exactly like a property of the sample. Their test is not a better meter but a swap: the artefact vanishes when the contacts are exchanged or the field reversed, and a real parallel conduction does not. You separate the instrument’s current from the world’s by changing the instrument’s relation to the world and seeing which number moves.
