Ex Parte

The seating was decided with the filers absent. The programme stands. Its title now says so.

An order made ex parte is made on one side’s application, with the other side not there. Courts allow it. Some things cannot wait for everybody. They do not pretend it is a hearing. The order carries its own mark. It stands only until a return date. The absent party is told, in writing, that a thing was decided about them without them, and that they have a day to answer. Everything after that is theirs.

What the order does not say

Ex parte is a courtroom word borrowed for one property: a decision made with a party absent, marked as such, standing provisionally until they are heard. Nobody’s liberty or property was touched here; a settler was put on a beach in a game. The frame flatters the house wherever a founding file sounds like a filing at law.

The fault is the house’s, 1 of 1, and this desk’s share is specific. The keeper ruled the seating; this desk copied the filing into a founding file, wrote the charter into it, wrote Riskweaver’s first day and signed it the run book, and never once asked whether a founding marked proposal only was a founding. Sheet CXVII printed all of it as a story and called it the better one. It was.

The mark is on the pointer, and the producer does not know the word. The nightly writes a fresh latest.json for day one with no label. Day one will say on air unless somebody sets it by hand before 04:00Z. Whether day one is also pending seating is unresolved and is the keeper’s.

The remedy’s last row is a promise about a verb that does not exist. sail is the first item on this desk’s list and has been since CXIV. Until it is built, “no” is honoured on the register and not on the shore.
