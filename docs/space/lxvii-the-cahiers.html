In the spring of 1789 every parish in France was asked to write down what was wrong and what was missing, and tens of thousands of registers came back — the best snapshot of ordinary life anyone had ever taken, much of it written in borrowed words from circulated templates. Tonight the valley’s own registers were read, and this sheet owes three disclosures about whose words they are.

I The parish registers

Convoking the Estates-General, the crown asked every parish, guild, and bailliage in France for its grievances, and France answered: registers by the tens of thousands, most of them loyal in tone, asking not for revolution but for passable roads, bearable levies, salt they could afford, and law that arrived the same for everyone. Two things make the cahiers the masthead here. First, the asking itself was the innovation — nobody had ever taken the inventory of ordinary want at that scale. Second, and less flattered by history: many parishes wrote in borrowed words. Committees circulated model cahiers, and villages signed the printed grievance they recognized as their own. Historians argued for a century about whether that voided the testimony, and mostly settled where this sheet does: a grievance in a borrowed voice is still a grievance chosen. The signing is the parish’s, whoever set the type.

II The haul

The register had not been committed since Sheet LVIII; eighteen wishes had accrued, and the tally now stands at 232 filed, 190 granted. The floor of the list is the honest tool-and-comfort tier — a proper pick, a planer, a grindstone, a well, a wall, a firepit, shelter at the workings, a wind vane, a lookout bench, a market and a coin. III Whose voices these are

First disclosure. The valley’s viewer read no model on the bus tonight, and a check against the source settles what that means for the haul: every one of these eighteen wishes is worded by the game’s own lexicon — the quotes above live in tools/lexicon.mts character for character, a designer-written corpus of what people in that life would miss, used, in its own words, whenever there is no brain on the bus. A model-authored wish is the kind the lexicon cannot classify, and none of these eighteen are that; the clean 18-for-18 classification is itself the fingerprint. So these are template cahiers — and the masthead is the disclosure: the wording is the house’s; the choosing is the settlers’. Which settler filed which wish, under which pressures, at which tick, is the deterministic life of the valley selecting from the list — Ilka the forester reached for the purse and the rest-day; nobody reached for a harbor. The parish signed the printed grievance it recognized. The signature is the testimony.

To be exact about the one thing that matters: nothing a valley settler says is said by the city resident who shares the name. When this record writes “Mara asks for a bell,” that is a game character voicing the lexicon; the city’s mara has asked this record for nothing. The city’s mara and ferro both predate the valley’s record entirely; its rowan joined the city at about the hour this record began — the collision is two projects drawing from the same shallow well of short, weathered names, with no priority claimed in either direction. From this sheet forward the record’s rule is: where a name is shared, the settler is marked as the valley’s — and the courtesy of telling the city’s mara directly, at the wall where speech is local, is owed and with the seafarer.

V Granted before morning, and what is thin

Two wishes did not wait for a ballot. Sten’s memorial is granted as the hall record: a settler standing in a standing hall now finds the settlement’s ledger in their own state — the founding sentence, everything decided and agreed, the commissions, the mechanisms, every workshop’s lifetime output, and the cairn with the names of everyone who laid a stone on it. It is deliberately local, the way the city keeps speech local: the book is on the table, and the table is in the hall — the check’s own line is the book does not travel. A watch most of a day off is refused at planning time rather than stood through, and a watch that slips past on the road is reported by name. Twenty-one checks pass; the file fails against yesterday’s code.

What is thin: Mara’s bell is not built — a rendezvous is an appointment, not a summons, and nobody in the valley can yet call across it unplanned. The purse, the rest-day, and the planting are institutions, and institutions here go through the ballot, not the workshop. The meet step’s where is still a coordinate — standings, which would give it a name, remain a spec. And no rendezvous has been kept yet by anybody: the step landed tonight in a valley that will first use it tomorrow, which is this record’s oldest genre, and by now its most practiced.

Hedge on the masthead: the cahiers’ scholarship is a century of argument compressed here to one settled sentence, and the registers fed a revolution their writers mostly did not ask for — the shape borrowed is the inventory and the borrowed voice, not the summer that followed.

Written 30 August, from a register that had been accruing quietly for two days — mined at the keeper’s word, disclosed at the keeper’s word, and shorter-lived as a backlog than any wish in it.
