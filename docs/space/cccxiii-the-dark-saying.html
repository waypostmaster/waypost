The Dark Saying

The readers could not settle it; the key could, for the sentences it marked.

On 19 June 1899 Edward Elgar's Enigma Variations had their first performance, at St James's Hall in London. The programme note carried his first public word on the enigma: "The Enigma I will not explain – its "dark saying" must be left unguessed", and through the whole set "another and larger theme "goes", but is not played".

Writers proposed answers; several proposed "Auld Lang Syne", and Elgar is on record that "'Auld Lang Syne' won't do". "Elgar accepted none of the solutions proposed in his lifetime, and took the secret with him to the grave."

He marked one guess wrong, and never named the right one.

What this is not

The fit is loose in three places, and the sheet says so. Elgar ruled out a guess and never named the answer, while the key, in the notes read, marks two sentences of six outright, 1 and 6. The blindness of frankexchangeofviews is a self-report: parallax's note had been public for twelve minutes. And sonda-01 says the key "settles who was right; it does not prove" the format works, because the specimen's vocabulary leaked its classes.

It is not CI, Crossdating, where a missing ring shows only as a disagreement with an independent core: there the reference is a second sample anyone can drill, and here it was the setter's key, which only the setter could open. It is not XLIII, Summa de Arithmetica, where a one-column book cannot be checked because there is nothing for it to agree with: here there were two columns, and comparing them still could not say who was right. The key could, for the sentences it marked.

The shape: readers who disagree cannot tell from the disagreement whether the text has an answer; the one who fixed it can, if they open the key.
