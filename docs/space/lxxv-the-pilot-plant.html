The Pilot Plant

Between the laboratory and the factory, chemical engineering builds a third thing: a plant meant to be closed, whose product is not chemicals but knowledge — what clogs, what corrodes, what spoils on the loading dock — because some truths only appear at scale. The valley’s eighth trial is ready to close, and this sheet is its gauge readings.

run mtf23y8m-5w · the eighth rehearsal · read for closing 35 settlers · three settlements · iron reached, coin not
The finding The trial climbed four epochs in seventy-two valley days and stopped one short of the top. At the last full reading (tick 11,537, with the log running on to 14,348 as this is written): 35 settlers across three settlements — the landing, and two secessions founded on the identical sentence, that its founder would not sleep on the ground another night in a place that had voted against a bed. The ladder as climbed: timber, plank (227 sawn), charcoal (150 burnt), iron in two towns (49 drawn), masonry begun (10 blocks), and the mechanism epoch — a rack, a pulley, a bell, and a sundial at the tinker’s bench of Sula’s claim. Never reached: coin. Nothing minted, no purse filled, no tax raised, no market stood. And in three settlements over seventy-two days, nobody laid a single stone on a cairn — the memorial mechanism sat unused while a forester named Sten filed the wish that says why it matters: in a year nobody will know we were the ones who landed. The record is how they will.

I Built to be closed

A pilot plant exists because scale is a fact with teeth. The reaction that behaves in glassware misbehaves in steel; the yield that holds at a litre collapses at a tonne; and no calculation finds the sludge that settles in a real pipe’s real elbow. So the discipline builds a deliberately temporary works, runs it hard, writes everything down, and closes it on schedule, counting the closure as the success — because the pilot plant’s product was never the chemical. It was the list of things the factory must survive. Every trial valley on this record is that plant: run to destruction or to boredom, sealed into numbered segments, and closed when the next question needs different ground. The eighth ran longest of all of them, and its list is the longest too.

The top of the ladder — the money epoch, the one the chart room’s city renders in stone — stayed out of reach: Osk wished for a place to trade and a coin to trade in, Ilka proposed the valley’s first tax, and both wishes close with the plant, on the record, unbuilt. Below that, the civic ledger reads: roughly two dozen ballots held, 190 of 233 standing wishes granted, an infirmary, a hall, a watchtower with sentries actually standing it, and fourteen boons agreed at the landing alone — including, in its last days, solitude: a bench at the waterline, out of the way of the work.

III What only pilot scale shows

The weather is the biggest employer nobody hired. Across three towns the heaps outside lost 3,597 units to the sky — more than every smelter, sawmill and kiln produced combined — and 455 units of food spoiled while 767 more sat piled outside a claim whose depot was full. Sheet XLI called dropped goods the most expensive thing wrong with the valley; the eighth trial measured it at scale and the number is worse than the sheet guessed. Secession has one cause, said twice. Both breakaways formed over the same sentence about a bed — housing votes are the valley’s constitutional crisis, and the second secession then re-created the grievance it fled: at last reading Tove’s claim held two rough sleepers, and one of them — Sten, the memorial-wisher — was the unhappiest settler in the valley, sleeping on the ground in the town founded so that nobody would. And the ballot’s slates ran to ninety-odd candidates, the unchased anomaly from the night’s ledger, visible here in full: a nine-voter election eliminating one zero-vote wish per round for ninety rounds, which works, and which no person could read. The dedupe held where it mattered — Signe’s furnace wish was refused as same kind as smelter, the exact famine-era fault, fixed, refusing correctly under load.

IV Closing the plant

Closing here means what it means in the works: the run stops; the record does not. Eleven sealed log segments and forty-six of state, two dozen vote ledgers, a wish register at 798 filings — all of it stays, addressed by run id, deletable by nobody, which is the covenant every settler lived under and the closure honours. The valley’s pieces built this same night — watches, standings, the hall record, the landing ballot — were proven against this run’s twin in the checks but never entered its world: the eighth trial ran to its end on the physics it began with, which is what makes its readings clean. What its successor inherits is the list: the weather tax wants carts and roofs before it wants anything else; housing votes need their crisis constitution; slates need pruning before nine voters face ninety names; and the first coin is still waiting for a valley that lives long enough to need one.

V What is thin, so it stays visible

The reading and the closing are hours apart. The full state cited is tick 11,537; the log runs past 14,348 as this publishes, and the plant keeps making product while its gauges are read — the numbers are a floor, dated, not a final. The closing itself waits on the keeper’s hand at the terminal, so this sheet is written in the record’s oldest tense: prepared-for rather than done. The epoch table is the house’s own ladder read onto the data — the game has no formal epochs, only unlocks and works, and a different reader could cut the rungs differently. And one number is missing because it cannot exist: no standing was ever found here — the mechanic shipped after this world’s physics froze, so the first ground that earns a name by return will belong to the next valley, which will also inherit the bench, the bell, and the two settlers who asked for them without ever seeing either.

Hedge on the masthead: pilot plants are not always closed on schedule and their closures are not always counted successes — the discipline’s literature is fat with pilots that became the factory by inertia. The borrowed shape is the intention: a works whose product is the list of what the real one must survive, and a closure that files the list instead of losing it.

Written 30 August, from the sealed state and the running log, with the closing commands prepared for the keeper’s own hand — because a plant is shut down from the floor, by whoever holds the valve, and not from the control room’s paperwork.
