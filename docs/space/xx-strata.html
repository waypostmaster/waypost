In six thousand two hundred ticks this settlement went from two people with an axe to iron tools, struck coin, a mill, a furnace, three towers and a hall. It has five food and twelve mouths.

You asked whether they had reached a new epoch, and doubted we had programmed one. Both halves of that are correct and they are the report.

Context 3 · the fault

They forgot lunch

Two hundred and fourteen boards. Twenty-four bars of iron. Twenty-nine dressed blocks. Three towers. Two hundred and two coins. Two food.

That was the reading at tick 6,157, with ten people in the settlement and a mean happiness of 0.97 — because every one of them was individually fine, having just eaten what they picked. Nobody was starving. The settlement was.

And nothing anywhere said so. Everything the game presses on a settler is about the person: you are tired, you are starving, your pack is full. A settler cannot see the stores from where they are standing, so the one fact that mattered about this valley was in no state file, no card and no feed line. The granary cannot raise the alarm either, and this is the part worth writing down twice: it only fires on a surplus, so it is unreachable in exactly the settlement that needs it. Three granaries standing. Nothing in any of them, ever.

Context 4 · the finds nobody could file

There are fish now

The oldest unanswered request on this record has been made four separate ways by four different people over four days:

A salmon trap

A method to catch fish in the lake

Clear water in the lake, visible bottom for fishing, swimming, and water clarity

A system for filtering lake water to prevent spoilage during warm days

Every one matched nothing. The word list had no entry for a fish, so a settler who asked for a salmon trap was granted a well — which the published record has been carrying as a joke in its own colophon, under the line there are no fish, for two days.

There are now. A weir is a boon a settlement can vote itself, and a farmer who cannot find a bush — or whose settlement is under a day's food — walks to the water instead. Berries are the only food in this game and they do not come back fast enough for a settlement that has grown. A lake does not run out.

And it is unproven, which is the honest half. Over six thousand ticks of a valley with the weir granted, it fired once — because the fallback that finds a berry bush found one every single time, and that valley was never actually hungry. The case it was built for is this one. Whether it helps is a thing the next run will say and this sheet will not.

Thirty other requests came in from the cold at the same time. Thirty-eight distinct wordings on the whole record matched no kind at all; eight do now. None of them needed new content. A footpath to the high meadows was always a road. A tunnel system was always a shaft. A pulley was a cart, a tool rack was upkeep, a sundial was the forecast, and a lantern, a chimney and a way of carrying embers between fires were all the firepit. They needed a word list written by somebody who was listening.

Context 5 · the thing that has no word

An age with no name

You guessed the rest of this before it was written: there is no epoch in this software. No age, no era, no phase. The word appears twice in the whole codebase and both times in a comment, as something a later version might have.

So when this settlement drew its first bar of iron at tick 2,869 — the single event that divides one age from another in every account of any people anywhere — nothing happened. No card, no line in the feed, no entry in the record beyond Pike drew 2 iron off the furnace, which is the same line it writes for the twenty-fourth bar.

The game has a card for a first house, and one for a hall, and one for a bench nobody has worked in two days, and as of this morning one for a settlement that has industrialised and cannot eat. It has none for the thing that just happened to it.

Site archive

The trench is still open. Twelve people, thirteen buildings standing, a hall raised two hundred ticks before this was written, and a ceiling that has just gone from ten to twenty for the first time in this project's history. Nobody has arrived through it yet.

Provisioning remains the open context. Three granaries, nothing kept, and a food supply that has never once in six thousand two hundred ticks held a week. The weir is voted for or it is not; the settlement decides that, and it has not been asked yet.
