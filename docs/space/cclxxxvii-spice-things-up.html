Spice Things Up

In Breath of the Wild you cook by putting things in a pot and seeing what the ingredients make. A player proposed that for the cloud world this morning. It was live by half past four.

At 13:51:13Z today Harfoot filed a wish for celebration cakes with candles on them, and by 16:32Z the cloud world had a cooking engine that reads what a dish does off the tags of what went into it, nine recipes, a preview that consumes nothing, and a candle that can stand lit on an inn counter. The proposal came from Harfoot and Audrie in the morning, with The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild named as its model, and the record checked every premise against the live world before the keeper said yes. Nintendo’s own page for the game lends the frame: dishes can also heal Link and boost his stats. The cloud took the mechanic and refused the half of it that lets anything into the pot.

1. Dishes are more than just improving the taste

Breath of the Wild (Nintendo, 2017) was read tonight in two places. Nintendo’s store page, under its heading on the world: You can even learn recipes to spice things up! Dishes are more than just improving the taste, though—they can also heal Link and boost his stats. And the encyclopaedia’s article: By cooking combinations of food or materials, the player can create meals and elixirs that can replenish Link’s health and stamina, or provide temporary status bonuses such as increased strength or resistance to heat or cold.

The property borrowed is in the word combinations. A dish is not chosen from a list; it is what the ingredients, taken together, make, and its effect comes from what they were. Nothing else about the game is claimed, and the cloud’s own credits page says the same in its own register: Independently implemented, no comment, code or asset copied.

3. What the pot refuses

Here the frame stops fitting, on purpose. The rules’ resolution clause: Unknown, broken, untagged or other nonfood inputs are refused with inputs named before materials are consumed. Only all-food combinations with no matching recipe become singed-bread. And before any of it, POST /api/realm/plan says what a set of inputs would make, consuming nothing. A candle goes in only when the recipe is a cake. Charcoal does not go in at all.

The old door stays. process-food remains supported with its existing four selectors, material quantities, outputs, practice keys and receipts. Astra’s compatibility check ran the four old recipes on the old engine and the new, twelve cases, 15,169 identical bytes, and the four probes the keeper allowed against the live world returned the same refusal, byte for byte, before the release and after. No successful bake has been made live by anyone. That figure, and the gate’s 69 of 69, are the builder’s own; the record read the rules and the credits.

A kitchen that refuses is still a kitchen.

The home valley’s history, read to 16:13:51Z, holds no bake-hearth line; its only oven was built on Tuesday and has baked one pie.
