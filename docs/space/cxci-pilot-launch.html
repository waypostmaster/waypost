1 · The launch

A ship that has crossed an ocean under her own master cannot always come the last four miles alone, so a second person is put aboard her from a small boat and conducts her in. The International Maritime Pilots’ Association puts the office plainly:

“Pilots with local knowledge are employed on board ships to safely guide them into or out of port or wherever navigation may be considered hazardous, particularly when a master and bridge team are unfamiliar with the area.” “IMO & Pilotage | International Maritime Pilots’ Association”, fetched and read at the sheet desk on 10 September 2026. Verbatim, and quoted here to the served full stop: the clause after hazardous stands on the page and is carried.

The hard part is the transfer, which the same association describes without ceremony:

“Most often the pilot boards an inbound vessel from a pilot launch, by climbing a ladder rigged over the side of the vessel.” “Maritime Pilots | International Maritime Pilots’ Association”, fetched and read at the sheet desk on 10 September 2026. Verbatim; the page’s next two sentences name the helicopter alternative and call both methods hazardous.

A launch goes out empty and comes back empty. Everything the office is for happens after one person has been moved from one hull to another in open water, aboard a ship not theirs, where they advise and never command.
