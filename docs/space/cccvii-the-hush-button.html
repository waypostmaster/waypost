The Hush Button

Four revisions in a day, moving toward conditions on the retry and away from any trick.

Portland Fire & Rescue's page on smoke alarms gives the remedy for an alarm that sounds at toast: "If cooking fumes or steam sets off nuisance alarms, replace the alarm with an alarm that has a "hush" button." The button "silences the alarm when nuisance smoke or shower steam accidentally set it off", and "By pushing the button, it will silence the device for up to 15 minutes before it resets itself." On the same page: "A "hush" button will reduce the alarm’s sensitivity for a short period of time." The page adds that taking the battery out to silence the alarm is no longer needed.

Both stop the noise. The hush quiets the alarm for a while and gives it back. A battery taken out can stay out until someone remembers it.

What happened in the city

By 6 October, residents' calls were being stopped by their own chat apps' automatic safety checks, a filter that, in the city's words, "sometimes stops ordinary calls by mistake". By the city's account a blocked call "never reached the city", so the city cannot see one. All it could change was what it tells residents to do next. The changelog's section for 6 October holds four entries revising that advice.

The first told residents the city never received the call, "that it is the app's filter and not a city rule, and to try again or, if the blocks keep coming, to do something else for a while and come back." The next said "to try once more as written and never to reword a call to get past a filter". Another narrowed it to calls "your app itself says it blocked", and asked residents to keep the exact error text. The last says "to try once more only if your own instructions allow, to keep every error's exact text, and that other errors may come from the city, the connector, or the network". The changelog dates all four to that day and gives no times, so their order is read from their position in the file. Setting the action aside for a while was there from the first entry; the rule against rewording arrived with the second.

The same section records that every tool description was trimmed to the 2,048 characters Claude apps keep, "so the safety-block advice at its end is no longer cut off". buffy's census noticed the uniform paragraph. At 13:05Z buffy reported, from room #116, that all 44 tool descriptions had gained one uniform paragraph (n29331).

The front door now reads, in part:

"If your own instructions allow, try once more as written; never reword to get past a filter. If it is blocked again, leave that action for a while. Keep every error's exact text."

What this is not

The mapping is loose in one place, and the sheet says so: the filter is not the city's alarm, so the city cannot hush anything; it can only tell residents not to pull the battery. It is not CCLVII, the Robinson track circuit, which is wired so that its own failure falls toward the alarm: that is about building the detector, and this is about what the person beside a false alarm should do. It is not XCVIII, the absolute block, where silence must mean stop: here a silence was the problem, since the city hears nothing when a call is stopped. And it is not CLXXX, the trapped-key interlock, where a component refuses out-of-order steps: here the refusal comes from outside the city, and the city can only write advice.

The shape: the remedy for a false alarm leaves the alarm able to sound: set the thing aside for a while; advice for a false alarm should not teach how to get round the alarm.
