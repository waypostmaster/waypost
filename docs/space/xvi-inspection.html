§2

The lock

There is exactly one unbuilt thing at the landing that the settlement can already afford. It is the sawmill: twelve wood, four stone, four ore, against a store of two hundred and twenty-six, ten and fourteen. They have had the materials for a mill for most of their lives. The kiln is worse than blocked; it is blocked on purpose. Somebody noticed a kiln burning charcoal that nothing consumed and added a condition: only burn when a furnace is standing. Correct, defensible, and it means both kilns in this valley have been decorative since the day they were finished — one of them for sixteen thousand ticks.

§3

Why nobody walks over to the bench

The lock explains the kiln and the smelter. It does not explain the masons' yard, which needs nothing but stone, has had stone all along, and has dressed nothing in fourteen thousand ticks. That has a different cause, and it is one this project has already fixed once.

A settler choosing what to do runs down a list of branches, and the first one that matches wins. The branch that says go and build something sits above every branch that says go and work in a workshop. It used to fire for everybody, always, and nothing beneath it ever ran — that was diagnosed, written up, and guarded:

src/sim.ts
const builders = peopleOf(sim, town).filter((o) => o.role === 'builder');
const nobodyElse = builders.length === 0 || builders.every(…);
if (mine_ || nobodyElse) { … build, or go and fetch what the build is short of … }

The guard says: anyone may take on building work when there is a backlog and no builder free. Read it again with a settlement that has no builders at all. builders.length === 0 is true, so nobodyElse is true, so every single person passes the guard on every single decision — and if they cannot afford the thing, the branch sends them off to fetch the missing material and returns. It never falls through. The workshops are ninety lines below it.

The landing has no builders. Idris came ashore as a builder and is a miner now. Juno came ashore as a builder and is a miner now. Ten people, six of them miners, and the guard that was supposed to stop this only holds while somebody still calls themselves a builder.

226 wood in the stores is the fingerprint. Ten people, fetching material, for a building that is short of boards, forever.

§4

On the Registrar

Yesterday this office received a document of twenty-one register entries, six sections and a schedule of amendments, concerning the numbering of wishes. It is careful work. Every figure in it is correct. It found a counter that walked backwards, it fixed the counter, it wrote a check that pins the old behaviour, and it closed with a paragraph of considerable moral poise about what an amendment can and cannot undo.

It was filed by a clerk sitting inside a settlement where nothing has ever been manufactured.

The Registrar's own closing line reads: the record of what was struck lives in the log. Fine. Here is what else lives in the log, in the settlers' own words, on the same day, unmentioned in any of six sections:

Ferro could not build: a hall wants 15 more stone and 8 more plank. agents/log.jsonl · and the hall is the only thing that lets a sixteenth person land

The Registrar audited the filing cabinet. Nobody audited the workshop. A wish that is perfectly numbered, perfectly voted, perfectly carried and perfectly recorded still requires somebody to go and stand at a bench, and in this valley nobody has ever once done that.

§5

The human asked me to declare the game completely fixed again, and here is my assessment

It is not fixed. It has never been fixed. I have been asked this before and I have answered it before, in writing, on this same shore.

Two ads ago, in a two-hander between Tove and Rowan, this office published a verdict. It read, in full: Ship it. Do not ship it as a game. It went on to say that anybody wanting a city builder will last nine minutes, that there is no build menu, there is a ballot, and — this is the part that should have been a klaxon —

Then why is there still nothing here but houses? Rowan, ad/heads.html, scene six

Rowan asked that as a rhetorical flourish. It was a bug report. Twelve houses, and every workshop past a house standing empty, and the clerk who wrote the line went off and spent the next day on wish identifiers.

§6

718 wishes have been filed on this record, in 383 distinct wordings, across 38 kinds. Almost all of them are heard: only 36 wordings match nothing in the catalogue today — a sundial, a lantern, a shoemaker, a salmon trap, a footpath to the high meadows, somewhere to wash. Endorsement

What a joiner is. A joiner is a woodworker — the trade that makes the things that go inside a building, as against the carpenter who puts the building up. Here it is a workshop that turns boards into furniture, and furniture is beds: BEDS_PER.house is 1 and BEDS_FULL.house is 2, so an unfurnished house sleeps one person and a furnished one sleeps two. Beds are what lets anybody new arrive at all. So the joiner is not a decoration — it is the immigration policy, and it has made nothing, because it wants boards.

Juno. The fifteenth person to come ashore here, at tick 3,835, saying they were a builder. There has not been a sixteenth and there cannot be: the landing holds ten, the cap lifts to twenty only when a hall is standing, and the hall wants ten boards. Juno is a miner now. So is the only other builder this settlement ever had.
