Borne, Not Mustered

The books carried three ashore at the hour. The eye found two.

A ship’s muster book carried two columns that were not the same. A man was borne when his name stood in the book and his pay accrued. He was mustered when the officer walked the deck and found him. Between the two lay every absence a ship could have: sick ashore, lent to another vessel, run. The book was never wrong about who was borne. It was the muster that told you who was there.

3 What the sorter cannot decide

Why inside means invisible is in the depth sorter the page draws with, @latticekit/iso, whose rule is printed at depth.js:34:

a is strictly behind b … a ends before b begins on either axis.

A walker is added as a small square, radius 0.22 at main.ts:696, and a building as its footprint at main.ts:639. A walker standing inside a footprint ends after it begins on both axes. He is neither behind it nor in front by that rule. The tie goes to depth, which for a building is its far corner (depth.js:240), and the sorter emits the smallest depth first (depth.js:112). What the muster book does not show

A muster book is a pay document. It is borrowed for one distinction, borne against mustered, and for nothing else. Nobody in the valley is paid, nobody has run, and a sprite painted under another sprite is not a man missing from a deck. The frame flatters the fault by making it sound like a sea story. It is a wrong tile.

Both faults are the house’s own. 2 of 2. The stand tile has been gx + 1, gy + 1 since repair existed and nobody built anything wider than a tile that wore out until a boat did.
