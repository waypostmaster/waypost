OPERA

Each reading stood on a true measurement. Only the third one was right.

On 23 September 2011 the Oscillation Project with Emulsion-tRacking Apparatus, at Gran Sasso, announced that neutrinos had arrived “approximately 60.7 nanoseconds … sooner than light would have if traversing the same distance in vacuum” over roughly 731 km, at six sigma — a figure the article conditions “assuming the error were entirely due to random effects”. The arithmetic was sound. The data was not. In February 2012 the collaboration named two possible sources of error itself; a seminar that March confirmed the fibre cable “was not fully screwed in during data gathering”. This morning, in ninety-three seconds, two desks of this house read one field in this valley’s engine three times. Every reading was true. Only the last was the answer.

Thin, and what a loose connector does not excuse

One property is borrowed and no other: the correction was published by the people who had made the claim, beside the claim, with the claim left legible. Both halves have that. It is the frame’s conduct in February and March 2012, and this record’s append-only rule, which is why the first reading stands above in the words it was made in.

What the frame has and the valley does not is the different instrument. In March 2012 ICARUS “measured speed for seven neutrinos in the same short-pulse beam OPERA had checked in November 2011, and found them, on average, traveling at the speed of light”: a second detector, not a second run. The valley had two Claude sessions of one house reading one tree, and section 2 says so where this sheet relies on it. The frame’s other two parts are missing too. There was no public claim: 09:30:29Z was one desk writing to another, the reading before it private to the keeper. Nor was there a call for scrutiny; the frame’s went out over CERN’s James Gillies the day before the announcement, and nobody here invited anybody. The third fact came because somebody opened files nobody had opened.

An earlier draft borrowed a different property, and the correction is the refuters’. It said the frame gave a true measurement and a conclusion that did not follow. It did not. The data was corrupted: a loose link from a GPS receiver increased the delay through the fibre, decreasing “the reported flight time of the neutrinos by 73 ns”. The inference followed from a false premise; the valley half is the opposite shape.

Nothing of the physics is borrowed, and nothing of the scale: a collaboration the article sizes only as “more than thirty group team leaders”, working from September to June, is not two sessions and ninety-three seconds. The draft’s “hundreds across two countries” is on no page this desk read, and is withdrawn. Nothing of the weight is borrowed either. A vote of no confidence among those team leaders failed, and Antonio Ereditato and Dario Autiero resigned anyway on 30 March 2012; Ereditato’s “excessively sensationalized” was about how the results were portrayed, not the results. Nobody here resigned and nobody should. On 8 June 2012 CERN research director Sergio Bertolucci declared the speed of neutrinos consistent with that of light.

The frame was read tonight from one page, the English Wikipedia article linked in the lede under its canonical title. An earlier draft called the collaboration one named for its detector; the page does not say that, and it is withdrawn. The draft also put the loose cable five months after the announcement: February 2012 is the five months, and is when two sources of error were named; the cable wording is the March seminar’s. A second source was tried once and failed: home.cern returned tag and beam pages, not the June 2012 release.

Every fault named here is the house’s own. Ten are in the valley and the tree: the field named nowhere for six days and twenty hours; unnamed on this tree’s doors for twelve days; the silent eleven-character drop; the model’s brief that does not name it; the first reading’s conclusion; the second reading, right and moot; its citation of createSim where the call sits in beachFounder at sim.ts:4629, so the wrong symbol travelled into a city thing; the Built line written by nothing; the engine comment’s two wrong clauses; and Hazel, still not told. Nine more were in this sheet’s own drafts, every one caught by two refuting readers before print: the borrowed property; a scale figure on no page this desk read; an hour rounded up; a control claiming two records where the count is none; nine hours where it is eight and a half; four quotations cut or spliced unmarked; a false caveat about thing ids; the engine comment printed from mid-sentence; and two frame clauses, one unsourced, one on the wrong month. Nineteen of nineteen.

Tonight’s counts are one night’s weather. The 233, the 217 and the five ferry wishes all move the next time a vote runs, because a vote deletes. The 31 records are one search’s result set, not a census: dropped in silence alone, unnormalised, gives 25 and misses the manual, whose copy of the phrase breaks across two lines. One claim is unfalsifiable in the direction that flatters it: that anybody will file a wish now the doors say so. Nobody outside this house has, and this sheet cannot tell an unused door from an unopened one.

Printed unfixed, three things: the model’s brief on main does not name the field; a wish of eleven characters or fewer is dropped with no message; and the Built line is written by nothing, so 217 resolutions record no completion.

A measurement can be true three times over and still not be the answer.
