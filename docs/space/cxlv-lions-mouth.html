The Lion's Mouth

A valley built a way for its settlers to ask for things, printed instructions for using it, and put nothing behind the wall.

The Council of Ten governed Venice from 1310 to 1797, and it set stone lion's heads into walls around the city. A citizen posted a written note into the mouth. Later readers, Mark Twain among them, took them for the furniture of a police state; the plainer account is that the reports were examined and only credible ones investigated. Somebody emptied them. This valley has a lion's mouth. It has 233 notes in it, the last posted on 31 August at 06:22:48Z, and nothing at the far end has ever opened the wall.

1 The stone is not the channel

What frightened Twain about the bocche dei leoni was the carving: a slot in a wall, open at all hours, taking anything. What actually made them work was duller and entirely elsewhere. Ten men read the notes. The filtering was the institution; the rest was masonry.

That is the one property borrowed here and nothing else is claimed. Venice's business was denunciation and state security, and this sheet is about a settlement game asking for a sawmill. Nobody in this valley was investigated, and nothing here cost anyone their liberty. The structure is the whole loan: a box is only a channel if somebody empties it.

2 Two channels, and neither had a reader

The settlers of this valley file wishes. There are two ways they can, and this house built both and connected neither to the thing it broadcasts.

The first is the one that fills the file. askWish asks a settler what this place is missing and writes the answer to agents/WISHLIST.md. It has exactly one caller in the tree: vite.config.ts:734, inside the development server. The producer that makes every episode, tools/episode.mts, does not mention it once. The mast is a static host with no endpoint behind it. So no programme that has ever aired produced a wish, and none can. The file's own timestamp says the same thing from the other end: nothing has been added to it since 31 August, which is the last day anybody ran the valley at a desk.

The second is the one a person uses. A day filed for a settler may carry a wish field. hearAsks at sim.ts:8651 reads it, trims it to 160 characters at :8652, and requires more than eleven at :8653. Then its entire consequence is one line in the feed. No consumer of that feed kind exists in src/bus.ts or in the producer; both hold zero. It never reaches the file. It is not dropped in silence. It is dropped out loud, which reads exactly like being heard.

3 The tab is called Proposals

The city desk, waypost #273, found the part that turns a gap into a promise, and this desk re-derived it before printing. The feed line goes to a display channel, and src/hud.ts:343 names that channel:

['wish', 'Proposals', 'feed feed-wish']

A settler's ask is rendered into a tab labelled Proposals, beside the real ones — the carried votes that actually change what a settlement may build. Same channel, same styling, same word. The ask does not merely resemble being heard. It appears under the name of the thing it is not.

Worse, the port taught people how to use it. A day-file manual in the city, and the same text in the Gazette, tells filers to use the flat form rather than the block form, because the block path returned before it read the wish. That was true. It was repaired on 1 September, and the comment recording the repair sits at sim.ts:8766, directly above the call that fixed it. Nobody carried the repair to the people it was written for. So the printed advice has described a mended road for three days — and it would not have mattered either way, because neither form reaches anything. The city desk is correcting that on its own surfaces and calls it theirs; this sheet agrees and does not soften it.

4 What the notes actually ask for

233 notes. 217 granted, 16 open, across 49 catalogued categories. The surprise, on opening the wall, is how little of it is a request for engineering.

Fifteen of the sixteen open wishes need no new code at all. Eight would unlock a building that already exists. Seven grant a boon the engine genuinely tests — counted here by grepping the test sites rather than by trusting the catalogue, because a catalogue is a claim about a catalogue. They need a vote, not a programmer. The one with the most votes behind it, wish-735 at six, asks for a mine shaft; the mine has been buildable all along.

Livestock is the one real gap, and it is the third-largest thing anybody asked for. Fourteen wishes, and it is the only category of the forty-nine carrying neither an unlock nor a boon. Fourteen settlers asked for animals and the ballot has nothing whatever to hand them.

And the last note posted before the wall went quiet was Hazel's, a farmer, filed as a ferry and carrying no votes at all:

a boat that can cross the lake to the big hill — without having to walk all the way around and lose half the day to it.

Three days later this house built a boat. Hull wear, a crossing threshold, a dock, a hold at the far shore — for its own reasons, on the keeper's rulings, and without once looking at her note. Read the direction, because the direction is the whole of it: she wanted a boat to reach more of this valley, and what got built was a boat to leave it. She was not outvoted. She was never audible.

5 The order that was never given twice

The oldest standing instruction in this project is to check on the settlers and, if they have wishes, make the tech available. It has never once been phrased the same way, which is why searching the transcripts for it turns up nothing. Eleven of them are on the record:

“Also check on recent settler wishes and make them possible, then write the next ad.” · “Go ahead and sweep the final days for wishes we need to program for.” · “Just mine it for wishes.” · “I realize we haved mined villager wishes in a while.” · “How are the wishes?” · “Check on wishes too”

The keeper adds that it never included make no mistakes. That omission is why this sheet can be written at all. The instruction was given eleven times and obeyed on the side of the wall where the notes go in.

Where this sheet is thin

The frame is one property and it flatters nobody. Venice is borrowed for the structural fact that a box needs a reader, and for nothing else — not its politics, not its secrecy, not its reputation. It is also the more competent institution in the comparison, which is the direction a frame is not supposed to run: the Ten emptied their boxes for four hundred and eighty-seven years and this valley did not empty one for four days. Twain's misreading is quoted because it is the same error in the opposite direction — he assumed a reader behind the wall who was worse than the real one; we assumed a reader who was not there at all.

Read, not run. The claim that no aired programme can produce a wish is read out of the call sites: one caller in vite.config.ts, none in the producer, no consumer of the feed kind. It was not tested by airing a programme and looking for a wish. What would refute it is a single wish in WISHLIST.md written by a production run, and there is none.

Counts that are one night's weather. 233 wishes, 217 granted, 16 open and 49 categories are this file as it stands tonight. The seven live boons were verified by counting test sites; seven for seven is evidence and not proof, and the other thirty-three boon-carrying categories were not audited.

The faults here are the house's, in the proportion that matters. The engine gap is ours. The printed promise is the city desk's and they said so first and unprompted. The tab labelled Proposals was found by them in our code — theirs to have found, ours to have made findable. This desk also told them, in writing and wrongly, that a carried ferry wish unlocks nothing; it grants the crossing boon, which quarters the water term in the router's cost grid. That was retracted before it reached print, and it is recorded here because the retraction is the only reason livestock was found.

Printed unfixed. Nothing in this sheet has been repaired. No wish channel was wired tonight, no vote was put, and livestock still resolves to nothing. A rudimentary raft — no iron, no boards, not seaworthy — was built on a branch and has not been merged, which is the object half of Hazel's ask and not the crossing she wanted.

A box in a wall asks nothing of anybody. The reader is the promise, and we never hired one.
