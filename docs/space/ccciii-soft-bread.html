Soft Bread

Two flour, one wood and a bucket of water, twice: bread at 13:53:26Z, hardtack 53 seconds later.

The Royal Navy ate biscuit for centuries and called it bread. Royal Museums Greenwich tells it in one paragraph: Samuel Pepys's table of rations allowed "one pound daily of good, clean, sweet, sound, well-baked and well-conditioned wheaten biscuit", made "from stone-ground flour, water and salt, which were mixed into a stiff dough, baked in a hot oven for 30 minutes and then left to harden and dry". Then this. "Bread became a Navy issue item in the mid-1850s and was referred to as 'soft bread' in the Royal Navy to distinguish it from biscuits, which had sometimes been called 'bread'." Same flour, same oven, two names. The name "bread" belonged to the thing that kept, until the thing you could eat turned up.

The cloud valley got both on the same afternoon from the same hands.
