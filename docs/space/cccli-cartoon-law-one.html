Cartoon Law One

Two settlers were turned away from a hall nobody may build yet, and fell off its frame in the same moment. There was no frame.

In the valley, on 28 October, Brindle and Cairnwright each walked to the plot for the hall the shore had voted itself, were told they could not build it, and were hurt falling off its frame in the same tick of the engine’s clock. No frame stood. The engine closes a refused piece of work exactly as it closes a finished one, and asks whether it went wrong only after it is closed. Nobody tends Brindle, and the produced programme has Brindle up again only after nine valley days, at 01:18 UTC on 30 October, in a cut that has not aired. The frame is the first of the cartoon laws of motion, in which a body that walks off a cliff does not fall until it looks down. Here nothing was there to look down from, and the fall came anyway.

Turned away, then hurt

The shore voted itself a hall at the opening of the 28 October programme, three for and none against. Nobody there has built enough to be trusted with one. So all day the engine sent settlers to stake a hall and turned each of them back at the plot, in the same words every time.

Twice the turning back was followed, inside the same tick, by an injury. These are the settlers’ own kept histories, as they stand in the aired files.

Closed first, then asked

The builder’s routine is finishBuild at sim.ts:11237. When a settler cannot yet build what they came for, it writes the line in the table at sim.ts:11294 and returns true two lines later, the same answer it gives for a house finished. Its caller then clears the task and runs maybeHurt at sim.ts:10775, under a comment that says it is last on purpose, so it lands on a task that has already paid out. A refused task has paid out nothing, and gets the roll anyway.

The roll for building is 0.008 before its multipliers (HAZARD_OF at sim.ts:1857), and the two ways it can go wrong are the two in the table (sim.ts:1867). Bare hands, worn tools, tiredness, dark and rain all raise it. Nothing in it asks whether anything was built.

The aired files’ histories hold 43 refused hall attempts on 28 October: twenty for the Seafarer, eleven for Cairnwright, six each for Brindle and Weathervane. Each was a roll. Two landed.

Across all 303 programme files on disk, those histories hold eleven injuries the earliest of them in a file of 9 September. Two are falls from a frame. Both are these two. Every fall from a frame this valley has recorded is a fall from a frame that was never raised.

Nine days, and nobody came

A bad fall in this engine gets worse before it mends, unless somebody tends it. Left alone, a laid-up injury worsens for two valley days and then mends at 0.055 a day. Seen to, it mends at 0.30 a day, more than five times as fast (hurtStep, sim.ts:1964, the two rates at :1973 and :1979).

Nobody saw to any of the three. Every injury’s tended stayed at 0 and its by stayed empty through the whole produced day of 29 October, measured by replaying the four cuts the way a viewer’s page does. The producer’s own end of the day puts each settler back on their feet at the same tick the replay does. The one kindness on record is a line in Brindle’s history at 23:11 on 28 October: Seafarer gave me 1 food.

The 29 October day was produced after it, and its kept histories hold no hall attempt and no new injury in 533 lines.

Until made aware

The law has a name and a date only in some of its copies. Wikipedia’s Cartoon physics, read tonight, dates it to O’Donnell’s Laws of Cartoon Motion in Esquire in June 1980, reprinted by the IEEE in 1994, and gives the first law as Any body suspended in space will remain suspended in space until made aware of its situation. A University of Toronto course page, Cartoon Laws of Physics, prints the same law under Authorship Unknown: Daffy Duck steps off a cliff, loiters in midair, soliloquizing flippantly, until he chances to look down, and then the familiar principle of 32 feet per second per second takes over. The encyclopaedia says sixteen.

One property is borrowed. In the cartoon, the fall is set off by a notice and not by a height: the duck has been standing on nothing for a whole speech before anything happens. In the valley it is set off by the closing of a task and not by a frame. The cartoon at least needs the cliff to have been there once. Nothing else is claimed. Nobody in the valley is a duck.

Sixteen Feet or Thirty-Two

Two of the eleven injuries this valley has recorded are this house’s arithmetic, not anybody’s work. The flattering reading is that the dice are fair, two falls in 43 tries; there were no tries, so there is nothing for that to be a rate of. The 43 and the eleven are floors, read from histories that keep 120 lines a settler. Printed unfixed: every refusal in the builder’s routine still rolls the fall, and today nobody happens to be sent to one. The Seafarer, whose own rules say the Seafarer builds nothing, was sent to raise the hall twenty times and is the only settler on the shore who did not fall off it.

Nothing was raised, and two people fell off it.
