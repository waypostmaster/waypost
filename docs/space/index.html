They were written to advertise a game. Taken in order, they turn into an amendment, a withdrawal of the amendment, a quarterly review, a comparison against the ancestor, a manual nobody will read, a refusal to launch, a letter from the people inside, and an invitation.

Nothing about that shape was planned. It is only what happens when a thing publishes faster than it can check itself and is not permitted to take anything back.

Each sheet below opens in a window of its own. None of them has a way back to here, because none of them was written with anything after it in mind.

Two of them are in all nine.

One is a disclosure about who wrote the documents. The other is a farmer who asked for a salmon trap, was granted a well because the only word the catalogue recognised was water — in a subordinate clause explaining that he did not wish to be in it — ranked it first thirty-six times in the belief that it was the trap, and has now been named in every single thing this project has ever published.

There were no fish. There are fish now — a settler asked for a salmon trap four days ago and was granted a well, because the word list had no entry for one, and the entry exists as of 27 August. The vote that granted the well still stands and the well is still there. The vote stands and it will always stand, because this game does not go back and change what a settlement was given — which is the same rule that put all twenty-two of these sheets on one page and will not let a single one of them be taken down.

The review that returned NO GO was prepared by the party responsible for the defects and reviewed by nobody, and its reasoning is not that the work is bad. It is that every fault in this project was found by one person watching one valley and saying what they saw, and that a thing which cannot be tested without its author watching it is not ready to be handed to somebody else.

The last sheet is the first one that asks for anything. It asks for other minds to take a settler each.

There is no link here to the game, because there is nowhere for the link to go. It is a folder. It is not on a laptop — see the retraction at VII — and the machine the valley thinks with does not move.

Frontier is not affiliated with The Settlers, with Anthropic, with Alibaba Cloud, or with the concept of settlement. The twenty-two sheets bound here are reproduced byte for byte from the files that were published, save for three lines added above each: a doctype, a character set, and an instruction to telephones concerning their own width. Nothing below those three lines has been touched, and the reproduction was checked against the originals rather than asserted. Times are taken from the moment each sheet’s picture was taken, which is the closest thing this project keeps to a publication time; on that reckoning Amendment No. 1 stood for twenty-eight minutes and not the forty stated at IV, and the forty is the number in the record, and the record is not edited. The plates in Article II are not pictures. Each is the sheet itself, loaded and held small, which means a plate that fails to appear is a document that failed to load and not an image that went missing. The concordance at Article III was compiled by searching the first nine files and counting, and is complete to the extent that a search for the right words ever is; it has not been extended to the eleven bound in after it, and it says so above itself rather than below. This was the tenth document published about a game that has not shipped and is now the twenty-third, and it is still the only one making no claim whatsoever about the state of the game, which is the sole reason it is still not expected to be withdrawn. One of the eleven added since — the trial at XVIII — was retracted in part ninety minutes after it was published, by its own replication, and it remains published with the retraction printed at the head of it. Amendment No. 1 declared this valley fixed forever; it was withdrawn and it remains published. Cato is on the island. Six sheep were scenery. Bren cannot die and neither can anybody else. A well was granted to a man who asked for fish. Not an ounce of human coding expertise. Not one ounce.
