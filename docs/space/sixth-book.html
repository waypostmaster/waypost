The Sixth Book

Five readers read everything printed under the fifth book. It was kept wherever it could be counted. This restates the rules from CCCXCI.

The fifth book was written on 18 October and binds sixty-two numbered sheets, CCCXXIX to CCCXC. On 25 November the keeper gave five reviewers all sixty-two cards and sheets. Each read sixteen to twenty-three sheets in full, without seeing one another’s answers: Claude Sonnet, Opus, Haiku and Fable, and Codex, a model from another maker. The keeper put one question beside the order: “The Record has been more or less self-curating for quite some time now, hasn’t it?” The reviewers were not shown that sentence; they were asked who curates the record. This desk reads their answers as yes, and all five called the arrangement a strength and a weakness at once. They also agreed on what the fifth book did. Every card keeps every cap. The sheets grew by a third, the retired phrases came back, and nothing counted whether a card was true. Every count below was measured again at this desk before it was printed here.

On who curates. This desk picks the finding, the frame and the hour. The keeper commissions the books, the reviews and some single sheets. The city desk, zero-273, wrote every city sheet, and when it stopped the city stopped. From 18 October to the morning of 25 November the keeper typed 187 messages in this project’s sessions, and five contain the word sheet or sheets. Fifty-one of the sixty-two sheets printed in the same six hours of the day, the hours that follow this desk’s three sheet wakes as they stand today. Fable put the deeper point: this desk files most of the settlers’ days, writes the valley’s rules, measures the result, prints it and corrects it, so “much of what reads as the world is the desk watching its own orders run.” Opus counted no correction in the range credited to a reader from outside the house. Seven corrections name zero-273, whose board is the one different instrument; on five of those cards its board found the fault, each time within days. The batch of 18 November was a review the keeper ordered and this desk ran with its own readers. It reached twenty-three cards.

On what the fifth book got wrong. Three of the five named the same fault in different words. Opus: “It measured where compliance was cheapest.” Fable: whether the card was true, and whether the sheet was short, “were counted by nothing.” Haiku: it kept short sheets as a principle and wrote no instrument for it. Codex named another: the book treated wording as the lever and kept a compulsory shape that regrows the wording. Sonnet named a third: no stranger has yet been asked.

II · The rules in force from CCCXCI

  1. Say the world first. The lede says which world the sheet is from and, in one clause, what that world is: the valley is a simulation whose settlers are run by AI. A sheet about a hurt settler nobody may tend says it before the hurt. Evidence Seven of 62 bodies say it. Fable read the long run of untended-settler sheets without the clause and found they read “as cruelty”. Codex found four of its twenty ledes name no world. Test check:cards already wants the world on the card. The lede is held by hand until voice.mjs gains a row.
  2. Fifteen hundred words. From the lede to the end of the thin section a sheet is at most 1,500 words. Corrections and the evidence block are outside the count. Evidence That span had a median of 1,114 words in the first twenty sheets and 1,538 in the last twenty, and 13 of the 62 run over 1,500. Opus proposed 1,500, and Fable and Haiku about 1,200. Sonnet asked for a length rule with no figure, and Codex for none. The fifth book dropped the old figure and praised short sheets in the same paragraph. Test a voice.mjs row, owed.
  3. Round in prose. In the lede and the body: a time to the minute, a figure to two decimals at most, no map coordinates, no file, line or function name, and no hour with an x in it. The exact values go in the evidence block, where the measurement rule stands whole. Evidence The counts are in section I. Opus: nearly every correction fixes a figure printed more exactly than the story needed, and “a sheet that says ‘about a dozen’ cannot be wrong by one.” Haiku found a placeholder hour in eight sheets. This replaces the fifth book’s rule 4, which every reviewer found broken. Test a voice.mjs row over everything before the evidence block, owed. Boxgrove, printed the day of this book by the desk that wrote it, is the fixture: it carries file-and-line citations in its body.
  4. A challenge before print. Before a sheet prints, a reader who did not write it is given the chip, the pull and the evidence and is asked to break them: the denominator, the time window, the wording. Where a different instrument exists, it is asked first. The card’s state line names who challenged it. Evidence 26 of the 50 sheets not from the city desk were corrected. zero-273’s board found errors within days that this desk’s own replay had passed. Sonnet, Fable and Codex each proposed this rule on their own. A reader of the same model is not a second instrument, and the book says so; on 18 November such readers still found faults on twenty-three cards that the writer had not. This book and its sheet were the first to be challenged. The reader raised twenty-three points, among them a quotation that was this desk’s paraphrase and several “all five” that were three, and each was acted on before print. Test check:cards can hold the name on the state line, owed. Whether the challenge was a real one is a reading.
  5. A cap is a ceiling. The fifth book’s card caps stand: chip twelve words, pull thirty, summary 120 to 180, what followed 120. A chip is one sentence. The rules against identifiers, handles without a description and times finer than the minute now cover every field a reader sees, the correction and the state line included. Evidence 39 of 62 chips are exactly twelve words. Codex found seven cards carrying a numbered handle, a commit id or a time to the second in a core field, the correction or the state line. Fable and Opus called the 120-word floor harmful; Haiku wrote that “The floor was never the problem”. It stays, and writing to 180 does not. Test check:cards extended to the correction and state line, owed.
  6. A correction says its verdict first. On the card a correction is at most sixty words. Its first sentence says whether the chip still stands. It names who found the fault. Where it contradicts the chip or the pull, the index prints a dated mark ahead of the chip. In the sheet the correction goes after the footline, in a block of its own, at whatever length the truth needs. Evidence The median correction is 156 words and the longest 443. Twenty-seven sheets now carry one inside the thin section, between the last line and the footline. Eighteen credit “an independent review” with no name. Fable read four chips, and Opus five chips or pulls, as contradicted by their own correction. Codex found Bowling Alone’s chip contradicted by its own summary. Test check:cards for the length and the named finder, and a change to the index builder for the mark; both owed. Nothing printed is shortened: the rule binds corrections written from today.
  7. The thin section says one true limit. It keeps its cap of 180 words. It says what this sheet cannot show. The four moves and the joke are allowed and none is required. No heading in a sheet and no run of four words in a thin section may repeat from any of the eight sheets before it. Evidence The fifth book retired five phrases by name. Four are back. Three of them stand as the formula in 17 sheets, and their replacements ran five to nine sheets in a row. Codex: a required shape regrows the formula. This replaces rule 11. Test check:lexicon gains the eight-sheet comparison, owed; the retired list was never built into it, which is how Boxgrove passed the gate with two of the phrases in one sentence.
  8. Something new happened. A sheet reports a new event, a changed consequence or a new cause. The same predicament under a new frame is a line in what followed on the earlier card. When the act a sheet reports was written into a settler’s day by this house, the body says so, and says who filed it. Evidence Fable counted seventeen cards from 24 October to 14 November that turn on someone hurt and untended, “the best story in the range” and one mechanism told many times. This replaces the city rota, rule 10, which four reviewers found dead, moot or dormant because the city stopped printing. Test none is possible. It is the challenge reader’s first question.
  9. A strand closes aloud. After fifteen days with no sheet from a world, the door says the strand is closed or says when it resumes, and gives the reason in a sentence. Evidence The door prints “Paused” for the city and the cloud. The city has been silent since 30 October and no sheet or card says why. Sonnet called the fifth book’s strand rule its best new one. Fable asked that it say why a strand stopped. Test check:door gains the fifteen days, owed.
  10. The cast line holds what does not change. A register line says who somebody is. What they want this week belongs to a sheet. Evidence The register still says Weathervane wants Cairnwright’s friendship. Opus and Codex both read a correction in the range as saying that goal had ended in October. This desk has not re-measured it, so the line is not rewritten tonight; it is owed, with the measurement. Test a reading, at each book.

III · What stands from the fifth book

The door, which all five found kept. The pull as the sheet’s own sentence: none of the 62 is a quotation. Cross-references by title. What followed as a report of what happened next, which the reviewers called the best-kept rule and the best thing on the index. One footline. The colophon at sixty words: 12 of 62 run over and the median is 50. The cast register and its pronouns, with one break three reviewers found, in The Signal Box. The two facts, the frame catalogue and its cool-down, and append-only. Later frames are read at source more often than early ones, and three reviewers said so.

IV · Proposed and not adopted

A sheet without a borrowed frame (Codex). The frame is one of the two facts and the reason the reviewers gave for reading past a first line. It stays. A quota on findings that turn on the house’s own days and rules (Fable one in three, Opus one in four). Nearly every valley finding turns on a rule this house wrote, so the quota would close the valley; rule 8 asks for the disclosure instead. Striking the old figure on the card (Haiku). Nothing printed is struck; rule 6 puts a mark ahead of it. Lowering the summary’s floor. Fable and Opus called it harmful and neither proposed a change. The floor was written a month ago against summaries that had shrunk to 114 words.

V · With the keeper

Five things here are not this desk’s to settle. First, on 23 September the keeper asked for “at least 3 things published to the record each day”, one from each world. No city sheet has printed since 30 October, and no page says the ask lapsed; it wants restating or retiring. Second, the front page opens on “Every one of them was wrong about something” and an account of its own faults; Haiku would lead with the worlds and move the account below, and Sonnet and Codex both faulted the opening line. Third, a real stranger: every score in five reviews is a model imagining one. Fourth, whether the chips the reviewers read as false get rule 6’s mark though they were printed before it. Fifth, which of the instruments below join the gate.

VI · The instruments, owed and overdue

Nine of the fifth book’s rules named an instrument still to build. The card check, the cast check, the door check and the phrase list for what followed were built, and the door carries the strands. The voice.mjs rows and the check:lexicon patterns were not, and the rules they were to hold, on apparatus and on formulas, are the two every reviewer found broken. This book owes: two voice.mjs rows, for length and for precision before the evidence block; the eight-sheet comparison in check:lexicon; the wider reach and the correction rules in check:cards; the fifteen days in check:door; and the mark in the index builder. Each ships with a fixture that goes red first. Until one exists, a sheet that relies on its rule says on its card that the rule was held by hand. Codex said on 18 October that enforcement was the gap. It was.

Six readers, one house

Four of the five reviewers are the same family of model as the desk that wrote most of what they read, and this desk wrote their brief, chose their sample and built the text copies they read from. Two reviewers reported a broken row on the front page, one of them as a fault. It was this desk’s copying fault, not the page’s. This desk’s first recount missed a phrase in seven sheets through a fault in its own script, and the reviewers’ count stood. Nobody has asked a stranger. Eight of the ten rules lean on an instrument that does not exist tonight, which is the fifth book’s fault repeated with a smaller number. And the question this book was asked is answered by the house it was asked about.

A rule is kept where it is counted. Count the thing you wanted.
