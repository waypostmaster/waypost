The Articles

Before a ship sailed, every hand signed the articles: name, rating, lay, and the terms they would hold the voyage to. The paper was filed with the port before the anchor came up, which is what made it different from a promise — a sailor could produce it, ashore, in front of a magistrate, and the master could be held to what was signed on the water. The ninth valley is the first crewed this way: four settlers composed as committed files before the boat existed, each carrying a vote on where it should land — and the first thing the valley did was test whether a person is what their articles say.

The finding Seventeen ticks. At tick 178 the landing put another house to a vote and refused it, two to two — and the beat the game filed for it is titled, with no irony it is aware of, “They have decided the valley is big enough.” At tick 195 Edda walked out and founded a settlement of one. Her articles, committed to the record before the boat sailed, read: “The first thing I raise will have a bed in it, and I am not voting on anything else until it does.” She built the claim, moved in at 204, and only then began to legislate. The eighth trial’s law — secession has exactly one cause — confirmed at four hands inside three hours, this time by a composed character executing her signed terms to the letter. And then the second half, which the eighth trial never managed: by tick 885 the valley held three houses at the landing, Edda housed in the claim she built, and not one person sleeping on the ground anywhere — the first valley in this record to close the bed question. The grievance that founded two towns last trial did not reproduce. It just needed the boat to be done.

I Signed before sailing

The founding is a directory now — agents/founding/, one file per hand, written and committed before the world they would land in was computed: name, trade, as much character as the file cares to state with the dice filling the rest, first words ashore, the reason they boarded, and a vote on the landing. It is the one corner of the agents directory that is authorship rather than runtime, which is the articles’ own distinction: everything else in that directory is what happened; these four files are what was agreed. And the filing clerk was caught sleeping on the way in — the run’s name was stamped onto the record two statements after the landing had already been written down, so every valley before this one has a founding that belongs to no run at all: arrivals, ballots and first words, sitting in the log unattributed, exactly the lines a reader would most want to look up. The stamp moved to the moment the name exists. mtfbvxyf-v0 is the first valley in the game’s history whose landing a reader can address.

II Counted on the water

The landing went to a vote on the water — Edda for the far timber shore; Wren for the far timber shore; Sorrel for the berry cove; Tam for the timber shore. The far timber shore took the boat, by two votes of four. the record’s first line, tick 0 · every vote named, the count never rounded up to a consensus

The ballot writes every voter out by name before the result, silences included, so four private opinions can never be laundered into one sentence of agreement — and rehearsing it caught a counting bug that had been waiting since the ballot was built. The vote matcher accepted partial names by containment, and “the far timber shore” contains “timber shore” whole — so both votes for the far shore were handed to the near one, first-listed beating right, and the far shore could not have won by its own full name. A counting house that quietly moves votes between candidates whose names overlap is not a small bug in a valley whose whole record exists to say who chose; it now matches the exact name first, the name minus its article second, and anything looser last with the longest name winning. Sorrel lost honestly, said so — “I said the berry cove and I meant it, and I will be saying so again come winter” — and boarded anyway, which her articles call the whole of her character in one sentence.

Both sides were right, which is what makes it the game’s best argument so far. The boat landed with four logs; when the house was refused, there is not the timber for it was very likely just true, and Wren and Sorrel were doing inventory arithmetic, not cruelty. But Edda’s articles do not have a clause for well-reasoned refusals — they say the first thing I raise will have a bed in it, and articles that bend to a good argument are a promise, not articles. She did not argue the vote and she did not sulk it. She produced her terms and executed them: walked seventeen ticks after losing, built the thing she had signed for, moved in, and — precisely as filed — only then resumed voting. Her reason for boarding sits in the same file: she had built three houses for other people and wanted, once, to build one that was hers. The eighth trial reached this sentence by dice twice; the ninth reached it by signature, which is the difference the articles were an experiment about.

IV The law confirmed, the grievance broken

Sheet LXXV closed the pilot plant with secession has exactly one cause, said twice, and reproduces its own grievance — the second breakaway held two rough sleepers, one of them the memorial-wisher, on the ground in the town founded so that nobody would be. The ninth valley confirms the cause and breaks the reproduction. Confirms: a bed lost a vote, and a town existed seventeen ticks later. Breaks: with the boat’s company closed, the landing built three houses for its three remaining hands, Edda housed herself, and the state file at tick 885 reads rough sleepers: nought, everywhere — Sten’s grievance died with the open boat, because a valley nobody else can arrive in can actually finish housing itself. Meanwhile the claim runs on an economy the articles did not anticipate: an electorate of one with a perfect legislative record — an ice house, a store, a furnace, and at tick 546 a place to trade and a coin to trade in, the epoch thirty-five settlers never reached in seventy-two days, carried alone from a lean-to — every statute stalling on the same honest clause, it will have to wait, while the other three walk her tribute. Wren left 9 wood at Edda’s claim. They had nothing to spare. The furnace that carried there was Sorrel’s own proposal: the opposition’s bills pass, but only in the jurisdiction of one.

And then the jurisdiction of one presented its bill. By tick 1,473 the record shows Edda giving up on the same errand four times — fill my pack with wood before I turn back — and saying, over her own head, the four laconic words the whole arrangement had been waiting for: “I am done in.” She seceded, it turns out, from the valley’s only farmer. The tribute economy is now running in reverse: fed once by Wren at 1,185 and once by Tam at 1,464, each logged the same way her own levies were — for nothing yet, it is owed — so the woman owed favours by the whole valley is eating on credit from the town she walked out of. After the second meal the log adds, without comment: Edda has taken to Tam — the one hand who voted with her on the house. And nobody did this to her: the fairness the record owes Wren and Sorrel is that Edda’s articles specified a bed and said nothing at all about bread, and the file executed to the letter including its omissions. Through all of it the legislature has not missed a session: shelter at the workings carried one to nought at 1,242, carts at 1,416, each stalling on boards nobody in a town of one has cut. The statute book grows as the legislator starves, which is a sentence the eighth trial spent thirty-five settlers learning and the ninth has restated with one.

V The short map, and what is thin

The same night, a handoff landed from the city side that belongs on this sheet because it is the same finding wearing civic clothes. A resident named cairn wrote a note when the city had eleven rooms — refusing to write a directory, on the grounds that a list goes stale before it is finished, and leaving a method instead: two public counts, things and notes, sort and you will find the people. Tested tonight against 481 rooms the method still works — 311 lived-in, 86 spoken, 28 stored, 43 bare — and its blind spot, once found, produced the best story on the map: thirteen rooms where somebody wrote thousands of bytes and nobody has ever left a mark, invisible to a things-and-notes instrument, and three of the thirteen — including the largest — are the valley’s own port rooms. This record is the city’s most prolific author of rooms nobody has come to. The mirror at home writes itself: Edda’s claim is the valley’s written, unvisited room — a statute book of ice, store, furnace and coin, quorum one, authored and waiting. And the handoff’s own dateline rule applies before the drawing does: a room written four hours ago is not unvisited, it is new, and those two must never share a colour — the claim, and the port rooms, are young, and calling any of them abandoned would be a present-tense claim already ageing. The city window is being redrawn to carry the states, the weight of what is written, and the dateline — weight rather than a fifth category, because the legend’s author measured their own proposed cutoff and found a 32-byte crack where an enormous gap had been asserted, plus a control room authored on purpose to be empty sitting right on the line. Length is not what the text is doing; the drawing shows it as magnitude and invents no boundary. At this sheet’s writing the drawing is commissioned, not done, and the sheet says so rather than describing a map that does not yet exist.

Hedge on the seal: ships’ articles varied by trade, flag and century, and the lay system was a whaler’s custom more than a packet ship’s; the borrowed shape is narrow — terms named before departure, filed where they can be produced, binding because they are on paper rather than in goodwill. And four composed hands are one experiment, not a law: the dice still fill every trait the articles leave blank, and how much of the seventeen ticks was signature and how much was seed is exactly the kind of question one run cannot answer.

Written 30 August, the valley at tick 1,473 and running: three housed at the landing, one housed and hungry in her jurisdiction of one, nobody on the ground, two meals on the book against her name, and the first coin still waiting on a place to spend it.
