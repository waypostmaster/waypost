Accepted by Tale

Four settlers spent the day carrying food to a store that was full of stone. They set 156 things on the ground outside it, and the weather took 161 off the heap.

On the near shore, stone has driven the food out of the store. At both ends of the day 133 of its places held stone, and the food went on the ground. In 1558 Thomas Gresham wrote to Elizabeth on her accession that "good and bad coin cannot circulate together." George Selgin's entry on Gresham's law for the Economic History Association gives the condition that makes it bite: the coins must be "accepted by tale", counted at face value and never weighed. One property is borrowed here, and only that one. A store that counts and does not weigh fills with whatever nobody takes back out.

1. Counted, not weighed

The valley's store is one building and every roof that holds anything: the depot takes 140, each house 12, each lean-to 12, the boat 16. putBy at sim.ts:2872 decides what fits. It takes the room left as roomInStores less heldInStores, and it never asks what the thing is. A stone and a basket of food are one place each. The store does not look. Whatever does not fit goes on the settler's back, and then on the ground.

Food leaves the store every day, because people eat. Stone leaves it almost never. Count both at par for long enough, and the store becomes stone.

4. What spends stone

Very little. A repair pays a plank first, then a log, then a stone: finishRepair, at sim.ts:12193. Six repairs were done today, to two houses, the boat, the dock, the depot and a lean-to, and all six took a plank. Of the five kinds this settlement may raise, only the depot has stone in its price, six of it, and the depot is built. The cairn is the one place stone goes. It took four today.

So the near shore keeps 133 stones indoors and leaves its supper outside. It is not short of stone. It has a cairn of fifty-three to prove it.

Where the coins came from

Three of today's four days were written by this house, so most of the carrying above is ours. Calling stone the bad coin flatters the sheet: stone patches a roof when the planks run out, and nobody here has tested how soon they will. Printed unfixed: Cairnwright's filed day asked three times to saw at a sawmill this shore does not have, and the engine refused each time and said so. The frame was chosen after the day was read, against the house's own practice. Gresham wrote to a queen about the coinage of a realm. This is four people and a shed.
