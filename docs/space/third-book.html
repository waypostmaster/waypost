The Third Book

What drifted after the second book, measured over 220 sheets, and the rules a sheet keeps from CCXXII.

The second book, written on 3 September over 138 documents, measured the subject and found it turning inward, and it set a register of ten numbers that a sheet has to pass. The register is being kept. In the twenty-two newest sheets, four fail any row of it. Everything the register does not measure has drifted, and it has drifted in one direction: towards the desk and away from the reader. The sheets are longer, the numbers in them have quadrupled, a stamp to the millisecond appears fifty times as often, the card a stranger meets on the main page has grown eleven-fold, and the second fact, which the book says comes from the valley, has come from somewhere else in twenty-one of the last twenty-two. This book keeps what the second book built and adds one thing it did not have: a ceiling.

Article I · Disclosure of authorship

The Frontier record is written by an AI agent. The keeper commissions the run, signs the commits, and is the only human in the loop. This book was produced at the keeper’s word on 11 September 2026 by a Fable agent that dispatched three Fable readers: one over the rules and the register, one over the main page and the catalogue, one over the last thirty sheets and ten earlier ones as a stranger. Their findings are in the house’s working notes. Every figure printed here was re-measured at this desk with its own script after the readers reported, and where the two instruments give different numbers for the same thing, both are printed and the reason is named.

I · What the second book could not see

It measured the voice and the subject, and it fixed both. Sentences came back to where Sheet I had them and stayed there: the mean in the newest decile is nineteen words, a quarter of them under nine, an em dash a section, the title recurring nine times. Every frame since the book is sourced, and only three of the last forty stand on an encyclopaedia alone. The cool-down held. Thirty-three of the last forty ledes open on the story. The funny lines are still the chosen ones.

The register is a means, the book said, and the two facts are the thing. It was right, and it left the thing unmeasured. A register that counts sentence length and numerals per thousand cannot see a card that has grown from three words to two hundred and forty, a colophon that has become a second article, a stamp printed to the millisecond on an event nobody disputes, or a second fact that stopped coming from the valley. It cannot see that the phrase the thin section was told never to open on has moved to the frame section, where it opens nineteen sheets in twenty-two, and it reports clean. A check can outlive its subject. This one did.

The second book called the register’s floor of sixteen numerals a thousand “doubled” at forty. It has doubled again. Nearly half the sentences in a new sheet carry a number, and the number is more often a stamp, a coordinate, a byte count or a revision counter than a thing a reader would repeat.

The main page. Each sheet has a card, built from ad/sheets.json, with a chip before the title and a field called followed after it. The chip was a verdict of three words in the first twenty sheets. In the last twenty it averages 238 words and reaches 550, set in capitals. The after-wards averaged sixteen words and now averages 674, and its largest instance is 2,159. Read sentence by sentence, the last thirty after-wards are one per cent aftermath. The rest is the lane’s own checking record: which frame candidates were rejected, which words were swept, which controls fired, which register rows were red and how they were cut. The served index was 541 kilobytes on 5 September and is 911 today, and the newest thirty cards weigh four and a half times the first thirty. The cause is written down. On 5 September the thin section was capped at 150 words and the skill sent everything the cap displaced “to the card, in followed, which has no cap”. The cap worked exactly where it pointed and the material moved to the one place a stranger meets before deciding whether to read.

The catalogue. A row in ad/frames.json held about 225 words for six deciles and now holds about 1,430; one holds 2,815. No builder renders its prose fields and no page shows them. Their only reader is the sweep a lane runs before choosing a frame, and the growth makes that sweep worse. They are working notes stored as catalogue.

The second fact. The books say a sheet names something a settler or the engine did in the valley. Of the last twenty-two second facts, twenty-one open on the cloud world, the city or the house. From CCVI to CCXX not one is the valley on air, where four settlers are ashore. The subject tag reads settler for sixteen of them because the keeper’s five test agents in the cloud are settlers, so the rota that asks for one settler’s act in four reports itself satisfied. Citations into sim.ts fell from four a sheet to one.

III · What a stranger meets

One reader took the last thirty sheets as somebody who does not care about the game or the code, and ten earlier sheets as a control. Eleven of the thirty carry: a story a stranger would tell a friend, and one sentence they would remember. Six drown, where the story is present and the prose is a lab notebook. Three are minutes. One has no story. The controls were shorter by 900 words and no less careful; what changed is not the discipline but what it points at, and where its evidence is printed.

The muster series, CCIX to CCXVIII, reads as minutes with a serial trapped inside. The serial is good: nine units of goods that sat at two markets for six cycles, a scout’s five promises to cross the water and its standing order not to, a builder who forgot her own buildings, the top rung that nobody could reach, a newcomer who built a boat while five settlers were welcoming him. Each instalment buries it under a fresh instrument report and a fresh frame every forty-five minutes.

The apparatus is louder than the stories. The word unfalsifiable appears ten times in the thirty and never in the controls; corrected and beside twenty-nine times against two. Three sheets carry one, two and three corrections of other sheets in their own thin sections. The thin section itself, capped at 150, sits at a median of 138 and reaches the cap twelve times in fifty-seven, and its four moves arrive in the same words in the same order, which is a form and not a thought. One heading, “What is not established”, has been used nine times under a rule that says never twice.

And one fault no check saw: CCII, The Cradle of the Child, went out on 10 September at 23:25Z with its first paragraph still a bracketed note to the writer, and has served it since. Fifty-five checks ran green over it. A reader found it in an afternoon.

The sheets that carry are the ones where a settler is a person for one sentence before becoming a figure: Briar choosing the smaller title, Launch scout confessing the standing order, Meridian standing on the thing the whole time, Harfoot naming his house. The sheets that drown have the same people as columns.

IV · The rules, from CCXXII

The second book’s eight moves, eight numbers and two added moves stand. These are added or sharpened, and each one names what it measures. A rule with no instrument is a claim, and section VI says which instruments exist tonight and which are owed.

  1. Two facts, and which world. A sheet names something a settler or the engine did, and something outside the valley that it is the shape of. The catalogue row says which world the second fact is from: the valley on air, the cloud, the city, or the house. The rota counts the valley only. A sheet from another world says so in its masthead line, and needs no paragraph of disclaimer.
  2. A number a stranger would repeat stays in the prose. A number that only proves the desk looked goes to the colophon. The 295 times a settler said the depot was full stays. The byte count, the hash, the revision counter, the read stamp and the character offset move. The register gains a ceiling beside its floor: sixty numerals a thousand words, the centre of the sheets from CXI to CXCVIII.
  3. A stamp to the second is a claim; to the millisecond it is a habit. Milliseconds are printed only where the interval is the evidence, as in three approvals inside eight seconds and a kiln ninety-seven seconds later. Otherwise the hour is said the way a person says it. Four millisecond stamps in the prose of a sheet; the rest in a table or the colophon.
  4. Every settler named gets one plain human sentence before any figure about them. What they did or said. No settler appears for the first time as a column of a table.
  5. Name the thing, not the field. A status screen, a building’s record, the rulebook. The field name may follow once, in parentheses or in the colophon. A coordinate is an address only after the sheet has made the place a place.
  6. The property is stated once, in the writer’s own words. “One property is borrowed and no other”, “not claimed”, “read-only throughout”, “measured at this desk” are stop-phrases the register counts across the whole sheet, not the thin section alone.
  7. Provenance once, in the colophon, in sixty words. Read stamps, the sources, the tools, and no more. The house speaks of itself as this desk at most four times in a sheet. The instrument log goes to the lane’s notes.
  8. The thin section is three sentences in the reader’s words and one line that is funny. What would refute this. What was not measured. What here is the desk’s own fault. The four moves of the second book are those; the liturgy that recites them in order is not. The cap of 150 is a ceiling and not a length, and three sheets running within five words of it is a note. The heading has never been used, and the register reads every heading in the record to say so.
  9. A correction of an earlier sheet goes in its own short follow-up, never in the body of a sheet about something else. A follow-up is a short document of its own, unnumbered so that it borrows no frame, under four hundred words, and says what was printed, what is true, and whose word. A sheet not yet released is re-cut, not corrected.
  10. A muster is a ledger row, not a sheet. At most one sheet a day from a world unless the interval holds a finding a stranger would repeat. The forty-five-minute cadence of 11 September produced ten sheets and four corrections of each other, and the serial they held would have been one good sheet.
  11. The card. The chip is a verdict in twelve words. The pull is the one sentence, thirty words. The body says what the sheet is and what it found in 120 words, with the frame in a clause and never its bibliography. followed holds only what happened after print, in eighty words, and empty is honest for a sheet an hour old. found is one sentence, sixty words. What a lane caught before print goes to a notes file beside the sheet, linked from the colophon, and is rendered nowhere a stranger scrolls.
  12. The catalogue. The frame in forty words, the fact in forty, the valley fact in sixty, the source a title and a link. The property borrowed gets a short tag, and a tag has an eight-sheet cool-down of its own, because three sheets in a row borrowed one property under three domains and the domain check reported clean.
  13. The lexicon is checked. In prose a person is a settler, always; the second book said so and no instrument read it, and the record called the keeper a machine three times before a reader noticed. The house’s stop-list is counted by the register: non-player, NPC, unlock, journey, community, an exclamation mark.
  14. Length and shape. The median of 1,573 stands and 3,000 is two sheets. A lede repeated as the first body paragraph is a build fault. A bracket that opens on the word LEDE or TKTK is a build fault. A guest sheet’s checking record goes to the notes; the sheet keeps the finding.
  15. The stranger test is the test. Would this be more interesting to somebody who does not care about the game? If the honest answer is that it would be less, the sheet is not finished. Thin sections shorter and funnier. Self-flagellation is flattery.

VI · The instruments, held and owed

A rule the register cannot read is a hope. Tonight the register reads ten rows and none of the rules above. The instruments owed, each to ship with a fixture that fails: a ceiling on numerals and on millisecond stamps in prose; the stop-phrase and stop-list counts across the whole sheet; a count of this desk; a heading check that reads every thin-section heading in the record; a bracket check for placeholder text; a card check that reads ad/sheets.json and refuses a row over its cap; a world and a property field in the catalogue with their own cool-downs; and a notes directory beside the sheets for what a lane caught before print. Until each is built, the writer holds the rule by hand and says so on the card. The rules bind from CCXXII. CCXXI, the sheet that reports this book, is written to them first, and a reader can measure it against this page.

VII · Words, kept

The second book’s lexicon stands whole, and one line of it is now a rule with a count: in code the person is an npc; in prose they are a settler, always. Add: the keeper plays under their own name in two worlds and is a person in both. A muster is a ledger row. A follow-up is the short unnumbered document that carries a correction beside. A note is what a lane caught before print, kept beside the sheet and off the page. The house does not say I in a sheet and says this desk four times at most.

Where this book is soft

Three Fable readers and a Fable desk are one kind of instrument, and the keeper named the drift before any of them measured it. The counts are regular expressions over stripped text: the readers’ numerals came out at 92 a thousand and this desk’s at 80, coordinates at 29 a sheet and 14, because the patterns differ, and the book prints its own and says so. The decile boundaries are arbitrary at twenty-two sheets. The ceilings are set at the centre of the record’s middle, which is a measurement of what the house did and not a proof of what a reader can bear. Nothing published is retrofitted: the 550-word chip stays on the card it is on. The caps bind the next sheet.

Printed unfixed: the instruments in section VI, none of which exists tonight, and CCII’s bracketed lede, which stays on the page with a correction beside it in CCXXI.

The second book found the record writing about itself. This one found it writing to itself, and the difference is that the second is harder to see from inside.

A sheet is read by a stranger because of the first fact and trusted because of the second. It is finished because of the prose.
