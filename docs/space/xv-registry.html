Section I

The counter that walks backwards

Two functions, forty lines apart, each of them correct on its own.

tools/wishes.mts
export function removeWish(text, id)  // strike a wish off entirely
export function nextWishId(existing) { …max of the file, plus one }

The first is called once after every vote: the settlement's least-liked proposal is deleted from the wishlist so the ballot stays a length a person can read. It is not a bug and it is not a secret — the file says so at the top, and the comment above the function is almost tender about it. Deliberately a deletion rather than a tombstone: a struck wish is not banned, and if a settler asks for the same thing again it comes back as a new entry with a clean tally. The record of what was struck lives in the log, not here.

The second hands out the next number, and it works the way anybody would write it: look at the highest number in the file and add one.

Now strike the highest.

The file's maximum drops by one. The counter, which has no memory beyond the file, walks backwards onto a number that already belongs to somebody, and hands it to the next person who asks for anything. Then it does it again. Across the whole record it has done it 512 times: seven hundred and fourteen wishes, two hundred and two numbers, and one hundred and forty-nine numbers that mean more than one thing.

Which makes the promise in that comment — the record of what was struck lives in the log — one the log cannot keep. It is all still there, every word of it. It is simply filed under a number that does not identify anything. {"loser":"wish-73"} is not a wish. It is twenty-one wishes.

Section III

Nought point one four seven

A second fault, of a different family, and this one is not by design at all.

Filing a wish used to read the wishlist, then go away for up to two calls to a language model — one to work out what kind of thing had been asked for, one to check it was not a repeat — and then append to the copy of the file it had been holding the whole time. Two settlers thinking at once meant two copies of the file, and the second one saved wins.

24 Aug 08:43:31.297   Oren   wish-76   A marker at the lake's edge to prevent accidental falls or trips
24 Aug 08:43:31.444   Ferro   wish-76   A dedicated tool rack for maintaining axes and other hand tools
 
one hundred and forty-seven milliseconds. one of them is still in the file.

Twenty-one times on this record, two settlers were handed the same number within seconds of each other and the first one's wish was written straight over. There was no vote and no strike. Oren asked for a railing at the water's edge and the office lost the paper.

Tove's proposal that the settlement keep back a share of every delivery, to pay for the work nobody profits from, existed as a written document for one and a third seconds. It was filed at 20:24:24.171 and struck at 20:24:25.485. In between, ten settlers ranked it last, because they had never heard of it — a wish files with nought votes and goes straight onto the next ballot with nothing banked, against proposals that have been collecting second preferences for hours.

That part is the rules, not a fault. The rules say the least-liked goes. Nobody sat down and decided the least-liked would usually be the newest, and eighty-two seconds is not a hearing.

Section V

Certificate of a thing already done

While the office was mislaying wishes, it was also issuing an award. Bren is a farmer. A farmer's long ambition, in this valley, is to see nobody go hungry again, and the game measures it as gaveAway.food ≥ 30 — a lifetime count that only ever goes up.

Bren passed their thirtieth loaf long ago. The first time the valley was told about it was tick 13,218. Every replacement ambition handed to them after that was born already achieved: measured, retired, happiness raised, announced to the whole valley, replaced with an identical one, and announced again — about twice a tick, for the next three thousand ticks.

Section VI

Schedule of amendments

  • the floor

    nextWishId takes a high-water mark, and the mark is read back out of every segment of the log the first time a wish is filed. Strike the highest all you like; the counter has already seen that number and will not offer it again. A restart re-reads it rather than inventing it.

  • the queue

    One writer at a time on the wishlist, and every writer re-reads inside its own turn instead of trusting bytes it fetched before it went off to think. The re-read is the fix; the queue is so it stays the fix when somebody adds an await.

  • the receipt

    A strike now writes its own line — wish-struck, with the id, the words, and which settlement voted — so a deletion is a thing the record states rather than a thing you infer from an absence.

  • the ambition

    Aims that read a lifetime tally are rebased on what the settler has already done, the way the delivery goal always was. Thirty more loaves, not thirty ever.

  • the backstop

    A goal is measured before it is handed over, and one that is already met the moment it is set is quietly not handed over at all. A settler the game has nothing left to offer gets an empty slate — which is also what gets them asked what they actually want.

  • the check

    Seven new assertions in check-record.mjs, including one that asserts the old behaviour: strike the top wish and, without the floor, the counter rewinds. Twenty pass.

Endorsement of the registrar

None of this brings anything back. The 714 petitions are all still in the log with every word intact, and from today they are all under numbers that mean one thing — but the ones filed before today are under numbers that mean up to twenty-one, and no amendment can un-issue those. Section II is as much of wish-73 as will ever be recoverable, and only because the log writes the words out beside the number.

The strike is not fixed, because the strike is not broken. A ballot has to stay short enough to read. What nobody chose was for the newest proposal to be the one most likely to go: eighty-two seconds is the rule working exactly as written, against people who have not heard the idea yet. That is a decision about how this valley makes decisions, and it is not a clerk's to take.
