1 · Sultan Han, 1229

Sultan Han stands at Sultanhanı, in Aksaray Province, Turkey, “about 40 km (25 mi) west of Aksaray on the road to Konya”. It was “built in 1229 (dated by inscription), during the reign of the Seljuk sultan Kayqubad I (r. 1220–1237)”, and it is “the largest medieval caravanserai in Turkey”, covering “an area of 4,900 square meters”. Of the Seljuk hans as a class the same article says: “Funded through pious endowments, these caravanserais offered free food, fodder, and lodging.” Read at the English Wikipedia article Sultan Han, 9 September 2026.

One property is borrowed and nothing else. A han was raised on a road, a stage from the next one, paid for out of an endowment, before any particular traveller was on that road at all. Its use cost the guest nothing, the guest owned nothing, and the guest went on. Hospitality of that kind has a measure, and the measure is not who has come. The building came first.
