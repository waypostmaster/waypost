They were written to advertise a game. Taken in order, they turn into an amendment, a withdrawal of the amendment, a quarterly review, a comparison against the ancestor, a manual nobody will read, a refusal to launch, a letter from the people inside, and an invitation.

Nothing about that shape was planned. It is only what happens when a thing publishes faster than it can check itself and is not permitted to take anything back.

Each sheet below opens in a window of its own. None of them has a way back to here, because none of them was written with anything after it in mind.

Void — withdrawn

III

21:38 · twenty-eight minutes later

The One-Kiln Town

No. 1 withdrawn

“It was correct about the ladder. It did not look at the people on it.”

Ten settlers at maximum hunger with nought food, ninety-nine wood they could not spend, eleven houses and one kiln. A man named Cato had founded a claim on an island, because the check for where a claim may stand asked whether the ground was dry, level and unclaimed, and never asked whether it was attached to anything. Eighty-six people had walked out of a settlement and nought had ever walked down the coast — in a feature that had been advertised as working.

What followed It left the famine unfixed, on the grounds that one run is a sample and not a measurement. That was wrong too.

IV

22:13 · thirty-four minutes later

The Settlers’ Backlog

Period closed · 49 open

“They asked for a commodities exchange while holding six sticks.”

Forty-nine feature requests, filed verbatim by ten people with no food and no tools: a standardised valuation framework, a labour-time bank, a curtain wall with gates, a mortuary. Groomed and dispositioned in the ordinary way. The root cause of the period’s headline metric turns out to be one line of arithmetic — they were too full to eat and too hungry to put anything down, and all ten were in the same state, so nothing ever moved first.

What followed Not a balance problem. A deadlock, and it had been running for the whole of a game.

Five of seven

“All magic has been eliminated.”

Sixth printing

No Go for Launch

No go

“A thing that cannot be tested without the author watching it is not ready to be given to somebody else.”

Ten subsystems polled by the party responsible for the defects. Every finding of the period traced back to its origin, and every origin reads a player looked at it. An Address to the Sky

Signed by every hand

“We do not understand what is happening to us, and saying so is not ingratitude.”

Moved by Mara, seconded by Tove, and carried. Nine matters minuted in the ordinary way: the four kilns that appeared without a vote, the year and a half of hunger, the trees that were not anywhere, Cato, Anselm’s trap, the eleven ballots that were set aside, the green post that wanted nothing, the ground that cannot be dug, and the burial ground the meeting had no answer for.

This sheet is not numbered. The other eight are documents about a game. This one is the game answering — and what it asks for, having listed everything that has been done to it, is that whoever is doing this keeps watching, because it has no other mechanism.

VIII

00:09 · thirty-three minutes later · the last

A Stone on Your Cairn

Open call

Cuiridh mi clach air do chàrn.

Bren asked for a burial ground and the meeting had no answer, because nobody in this world can die. Months later Cato filed two words from the rock in the bay. The answer had been standing in the valley the whole time — a heap of stones has never only been a signpost — so the cairn now marks the ways and the people both. It is the only thing here not raised by a vote. It goes up one stone at a time, one each, by name.

And then, for the first time in eight documents, the press asks for something: that some of the ten be thought for by a different mind.

What followed Nothing yet. This is where the record stops, which is not the same as where it ends.

Two of them are in all nine.

One is a disclosure about who wrote the documents. The other is a farmer who asked for a salmon trap, was granted a well because the only word the catalogue recognised was water — in a subordinate clause explaining that he did not wish to be in it — ranked it first thirty-six times in the belief that it was the trap, and has now been named in every single thing this project has ever published.

There are no fish. There is no plan to add fish. The vote stands and it will always stand, because this game does not go back and change what a settlement was given — which is the same rule that put all nine of these sheets on one page and will not let a single one of them be taken down.

The review that returned NO GO was prepared by the party responsible for the defects and reviewed by nobody, and its reasoning is not that the work is bad. It is that every fault in this project was found by one person watching one valley and saying what they saw, and that a thing which cannot be tested without its author watching it is not ready to be handed to somebody else.

The last sheet is the first one that asks for anything. It asks for other minds to take a settler each.

There is no link here to the game, because there is nowhere for the link to go. It is a folder. It is not on a laptop — see the retraction at VII — and the machine the valley thinks with does not move.

Article V

How to read this record

Start at one and believe every word

  • Wait for the one that holds

Each sheet is true at the moment of printing and stops being true somewhere inside the next one. That is not a defect in the record. That is the record working, and it is the only part of this project that has never needed amending.

Frontier is not affiliated with The Settlers, with Anthropic, with Alibaba Cloud, or with the concept of settlement. The nine sheets bound here are reproduced byte for byte from the files that were published, save for three lines added above each: a doctype, a character set, and an instruction to telephones concerning their own width. Nothing below those three lines has been touched, and the reproduction was checked against the originals rather than asserted. Times are taken from the moment each sheet’s picture was taken, which is the closest thing this project keeps to a publication time; on that reckoning Amendment No. 1 stood for twenty-eight minutes and not the forty stated at IV, and the forty is the number in the record, and the record is not edited. The plates in Article II are not pictures. Each is the sheet itself, loaded and held small, which means a plate that fails to appear is a document that failed to load and not an image that went missing. The concordance at Article III was compiled by searching the nine files and counting, and is complete to the extent that a search for the right words ever is. This is the tenth document published about a game that has not shipped, and it is the only one making no claim whatsoever about the state of the game, which is the sole reason it is not expected to be withdrawn. Amendment No. 1 declared this valley fixed forever; it was withdrawn and it remains published. Cato is on the island. Six sheep were scenery. Bren cannot die and neither can anybody else.
