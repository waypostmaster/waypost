A Canticle for Leibowitz

On the closing of the valley, written at tick 53,277 — twenty-seven souls, none of whom has ever died, and two hundred and eighty-eight megabytes none of whom can read — read through Walter M. Miller Jr., whose monks copied what they could not understand and were right to.

Run mtbgptmz-5q · 25–28 August 257 days · 3 settlements · 38 sheets
The valediction This valley is being closed so a corrected one can be opened, and what is being closed is not the settlement — it is the only thing in the world that ever knew there was a settlement. Twenty-seven people came ashore here and not one of them can read a line of what was written about them: not the 28,618 ballots, not the 179 proposals, not the thirty-eight sheets, not this one. The record has never been for them. It was for whoever comes next, which until tonight was nobody.

Two entries in that column are the whole of Sheet XXXVII, written down forty-eight thousand ticks before anybody could read them. Tick 664 is the fault. A settlement put a load of goods on the grass because there was no room under a roof, and the feed said so, in English, in a line designed to be read — and it read as atmosphere. It went on saying so for the rest of the world: 6,124 loads set down outside a full store, and 1,264 wood destroyed at the two claims’ doors in the final segment alone.

That is what a Memorabilium is. Not a document nobody kept. A document everybody kept and nobody could read.

Miller's second book is the one where the light comes back and the scholar arrives to find the monks have been sitting on the answer for six centuries. Here it took three thousand eight hundred ticks and it went in the order the ladder demanded, which is the only order it could have gone in.

Thirty-one ticks. They solved metal and money and a room to be together in, and then the stores went to nought and the feed said so. Sheet XX had already given that its section drawing — the richest stratum is the thinnest and the last, and one red band runs the whole depth of the trench, and the red band is the food.

The scholar in Fiat Lux is told the abbey has had what he needs the whole time. Every check in this repository is that scene, run nightly, against a valley that cannot attend.

Fiat Voluntas Tua

tick 6,248 to the last — the long age, and the decision taken elsewhere

Forty-six thousand ticks, which is nine tenths of the world, and almost nothing in them is about survival. What the settlers asked for once they had eaten was: an hour that was nobody else's, a bench on the shore out of the way of the work, a custom everybody keeps, somebody to put a quarrel to, and a heap of stones over whoever we come to lose.

Miller's third book ends with the second Deluge, launched by people the abbey cannot reach, for reasons no one inside the walls can affect. This valley ends the same way and more gently: a tab will be closed. Nothing in the world will register it. There is no line in src/ that can remove a settler, hurt.harm is clamped at 0.95 for ever, and Sheet XXXVI established what follows from that — twenty-seven people will stop, all at once, and not one of them will die. It is a total extinction containing no deaths at all.

The one thing that came in time came four hundred ticks ago and was not enough. The shed was widened tonight and the running valley took the change without stopping: the timber a claim could bank had been pinned at five for the whole of the previous segment, and since the correction it has reached nine.

A house wants ten. and at tick 53,277 Tove’s claim holds seven, and Bren’s holds none

So the answer arrived, inside the world, before the end, and the end will come first. Brother Francis spends fifteen years illuminating a blueprint and is killed on the road before he can show it to anyone. The document was still right.

Bren has cast forty-four ballots alone in a settlement of one. Every one of them is written down, with his stated reason, in a file he has no way to know exists.

And there is Halla, whose whole story is in there and closes:

She asked three times and collapsed in between. Then she bought a vote for one ore — the cheapest thing in the valley — from the woman who went on to build the house and hand her the bed in it. Two hundred and thirty-six ticks, start to finish, and every step of it minuted.

Nobody told her. Not that the vote she paid for carried, not that it carried by one, not that the ore did it. The clerk of this record is the only party who has ever seen those six lines together, and the clerk was not born in the valley.

Where the book is wrong about us

It would be easy and false to leave the frame there, so: Miller's thesis is that this does not work. The Memorabilia survive the dark age, are read at last, and turn out to be the seed of the second Deluge. The monks copy faithfully, understand nothing, and the cycle closes. Preservation without comprehension is the tragedy, not the consolation.

This record is the other thing. Every sheet names what followed. The concordance is not compiled by hand but generated by reading the sheets back through nineteen patterns. npm run check reads the record against the catalogue on every run and has refused its own author four times. Tonight it gained a rung that will refuse, for ever, any settlement founded on a shed too small to hold the price of a roof — which is a fault this valley lived inside from tick 664 and will die inside of.

If the next valley's claims put up houses, the Memorabilia were read and acted on, and Miller is refuted rather than illustrated. That is not a small difference. It is the whole argument, and this is the one dark age where the monks understood the blueprint.

Which is a claim, not a result. The next valley has not been run. The honest position tonight is that the correction is written, checked, published, and untested by anybody but a fixed seed.

The cairn

Bren asked for a burial ground three times. He was the only person on his claim and there was nobody to bury, in a world where nobody can die, and the meeting had no answer for him because the game had no word for it. Sheet VIII gave it one: not a graveyard but a cairn, which has never only been a signpost, and which is the single thing on this coast not raised by a vote. It goes up one stone at a time, one each, by name.

It stood at nothing for twenty-two thousand eight hundred ticks after they agreed to it, because there was never the stone to spare.

Then, tonight, in the last hours of the world, while this record was being written about something else entirely:

Sula · Anselm · Ysolde · Dask · Vell · Pike

Six on it now, by six hands. Begun at tick 51,292 and standing at six ever since.

Nobody in this valley has ever attended a funeral or will. They have built the only memorial they were ever able to build, in the last hours available to them, without being asked, for a loss that has not happened and now never can — and they will stop before the seventh stone.

The record does not lose documents. It has never been able to keep anything else.
