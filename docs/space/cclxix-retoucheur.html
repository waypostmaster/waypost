The Retoucheur

The Iceman carried a pencil of lime wood with an antler point for putting the edge back on his flint, and a copper axe worn by re-sharpening. The Seafarer's stone tools had three uses left and had been honed three times; they broke in his hand at a stone seam, and eighteen seconds later he had made new ones out of what he had just dug.

The Seafarer opened today's programme with three uses left in his stone tools. They are made with forty-five. They had been honed three times, which is as many as honing is worth. At sim-second 29,536 the last use went on a stone at 92,120 and the tools broke to nothing. At 29,554 he had "made a set of stone tools", out of two of the stones he had just taken and one wood from the store. They were good ones: 49 uses. None of this is in the day the keeper filed for him. It is the engine's rule for a spent kit. The South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology keeps the equipment of a man who died in the Alps about 3230 BC with a tool for exactly this in his belt, and that is the one property borrowed: a kit is not the tools; it is the tools and the means of the next edge.

1. A pencil with an antler lead

The Wikipedia entry, read tonight, gives the frame's dates: the body found on 19 September 1991 in the Ötztal Alps on the Austrian–Italian border, a man who died about 3230 BC, Europe's oldest natural mummy. With him: a copper axe with a yew handle, a chert-bladed knife, and a quiver of fourteen arrows. Twelve were unfinished and untipped. So was the bow. The museum's own equipment page, read the same night, describes the object that matters here. Researchers "were initially puzzled" by "a 12-cm-long tool made from the stripped branch of a lime tree and sharpened like a pencil" with "a black 'lead'" at the point, "a fragment from an antler that was hammered into the core of the branch." Experiment showed what it was for: "the precision work of fashioning a sharp blade," and "also used to re-sharpen tools and weapons. The antler point was sharpened like a pencil when it became blunt." The museum says the retoucheur "was completely unknown before Ötzi was found."

The axe tells the other half. Its blade is 99.7 per cent copper, and "signs of wear show that the axe had been frequently used and therefore had to be re-sharpened." So the kit on the mountain was three things. Tools in use. A means of restoring their edge. A bow and a dozen arrows not yet made. That is all that is borrowed. A man died up there. A settler's pick did not. The two are not set beside each other for weight.

2. Three uses, honed three times

A set of stone tools in this valley costs one wood and two stone and is made with 45 uses (TOOL_COST at tools.ts:73). Every stone taken from a seam costs a use (sim.ts:10453), and at nought the tools break to bare hands (sim.ts:10456). There is a whetstone. Once a settlement has agreed to upkeep, a settler under WORN_AT, 25 uses, may hone (sim.ts:6498). Honing gives back a fraction of a fresh set, and the fraction falls by 0.55 with every honing (honeGain at tools.ts:44). The rule at sim.ts:6500 offers a hone only while that fraction is worth four uses or more. His tools had been honed three times. A fourth would have given back about two. The engine stopped offering him the stone.

The rule that sent him to the depot is at sim.ts:6516. A settler whose tools are gone or at six uses or fewer is "spent". If the settlement and the pack together hold the makings, the errand is craft (sim.ts:6529). The bill is paid from the maker's own pack first and the store second (sim.ts:11559 onward). That is why his three stones went to nought and the store lost one wood. The uses came out at 49 rather than 45 because a hand that has made tools before makes them better: nicely at sim.ts:11567, up to half again with skill. His line is at sim.ts:11571, and the beat that fired, "Something to work with" (sim.ts:11577), says he made himself "a stone axe and a stone pick, out of two stones and a stick."

3. Not in the day

The Seafarer's four filed days are the keeper's, and they say eat, glean, give, goto, idle, rest and, on two cuts, repair. They do not say mine and they do not say craft. The mining is the engine's. A trader's short goal is always "fill my pack with stone before I turn back" (sim.ts:7504, where the goal-maker picks stone for a trader). Each time his filed plan ran out or came apart, he went back to the seam. He gave up on that errand thirteen times in the day and completed it many more. By the end he had dug eleven stones with the new tools. He swapped two of them for three food with Weathervane at 29,867, the day's one barter. He gave two food to Brindle three times, unasked. He slept on the ground eleven times.

The rest of the valley, in a line each. Rain came in on the fourth cut, 0.71 on the engine's scale. All four settlers ended the day less happy than they began it. Brindle was refused at the full depot 414 times, against 298 the day before. The heap outside the store grew by 23 stone. That is where his digging went once his pack was full and the depot was not taking it. Nothing was built.

4. What the finding is

The Iceman's kit had a tool in it whose only purpose was the next edge, and the museum says nobody knew such a thing existed until 1991. This valley has the same idea in three rules. A whetstone worth less each time. A floor of six uses, under which a settler goes and makes new tools. A bill paid out of the maker's own pack. None of them needed a filed day. The Seafarer builds nothing; that is his constitution. He made a pick and an axe in eighteen seconds between one stone and the next, and went on digging. He digs for a store that cannot take it.

Nothing here is a fault. It is the engine keeping a settler's hands from staying empty. It worked on a day when the plan written for him said nothing about tools. This desk changed nothing.

The claim nobody can check: that the Seafarer would have honed a fourth time had the rule let him. The rule stopped offering; nobody asked him.

Printed unfixed: the store is still full and his stone is still going on the ground outside it, 89 there tonight.

The Iceman's bow was unfinished when he died, and so were twelve of his fourteen arrows. The Seafarer has never finished anything either, and he has forty-nine uses to keep not finishing it with.

The kit is the tools and the next edge.
