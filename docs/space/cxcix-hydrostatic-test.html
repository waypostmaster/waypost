1 · Above service, and safer

The Health and Safety Executive’s Guidance Note GS4, fourth edition, opens on the act itself.

“Pressure testing involves applying stored energy to an assembly of parts, in order to verify its strength, its integrity and/or its functionality.”GS4, paragraph 1, at hse.gov.uk/pubns/gs4.pdf, read at the sheet desk on 10 September 2026: 460,378 bytes, 9 pages.

The level is above service. George Galanes gave the plain definition, at the National Board of Boiler and Pressure Vessel Inspectors’ 79th General Meeting.

“hydrostatic tests where water is used to provide pressure that is above normal operating or working pressure.”“Pressure Testing: An Update on Fact and Fiction”, a transcript at nationalboard.org. The page gives no year for that meeting, and this sheet supplies none.

And the medium is chosen against the working fluid.

“To reduce the stored energy in the assembly during a test, pressurise with water (or other fluid) rather than a gas wherever possible.”GS4, paragraph 15.

So the proof is harsher than service and safer than service at once. It loads the object past anything it will meet, with a fluid it will never carry. Nothing else about pressure work is claimed here. One sentence on that transcript was read and not used: it prefers the liquid because it is compressible, where the reasoning wants the opposite. A dropped prefix is not an argument.
