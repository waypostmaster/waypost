1 Thekk and Thorin

Tolkien borrowed his names too. The Old Norse poem Völuspá, the prophecy of a seeress, holds six stanzas that are nothing but a list of dwarfs. In Benjamin Thorpe’s translation of 1866 the twelfth stanza reads: Veig and Gandâlf, Vindâlf, Thrain, Thekk and Thorin, Thrôr, Vitr, and Litr, Nûr and Nýrâd, Regin and Râdsvid. The stanza before it has Dvalin, Dain, Bömbur and Nori. The one after has Fili and Kili.

The encyclopaedia puts it plainly: Tolkien used names from the Dvergatal for the Dwarves and for the Wizard Gandalf in his 1937 fantasy novel The Hobbit. Twelve of his thirteen dwarves are on the list. Only Balin is not. And the list itself is a borrowing. Scholars have long suspected the whole catalogue of dwarfs was slipped into the poem later by another hand, so the names went into the poem from somewhere older, and came out of it into a book, and out of the book into a settlement on a server. Nobody in that chain invented them.

2 Valimar

Its haven is Alqualondë, the Haven of the Swans. The Elves can reach it only by the Straight Road. Harfoot’s Valinor has all three, and the shelter called Valimar was raised at eighteen minutes to eight this morning, before most of the land existed.
