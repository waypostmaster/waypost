O Negative

A stone mends a wall as well as a board does. Only a board goes into the sheath.

The near shore kept its boards on 15 October. Cairnwright hewed three, one in each of the day's last three cuts. All three were still in the store at the end of the day. Three patches, on a claim and two houses, each paid a stone. The day before, CCCXX counted the shore's last four boards going on patches with 140 stone in the store. The engine now patches a wall with stone first. So the boards stay for the boat's ice sheath, which nothing else can be made of. The shape is the one NHS Blood and Transplant's group O toolkit sets out for emergency blood: "Guidance promotes using O D positive red cells for males aged over 18 in emergency situations, to conserve O D negative red cells for patients for whom there is no alternative."

The universal type, kept back

O negative is the blood anyone can take. Few can give it. NHS Blood Donation's page on blood types says it "is known as the universal blood type because it is safe for everyone to receive O negative red cells", and that 14% of donors have it (February 2026). The toolkit says it is "commonly used in emergencies where the patient's blood group is unknown", that collecting enough is "a constant challenge", and that NHS Blood and Transplant called amber alerts for it in 2022 and 2024.

So the guidance spends the commoner type wherever it serves just as well: "Transfusing O D positive to unknown male patients can conserve O D negative blood for patients who have no alternative." The property borrowed is that one, and only that one. Spend the common thing wherever it does the job. Then the scarce thing is there when nothing else will do.

The crew's own sheath hewing, added to the engine the same day, did not run once. That is as it should be. It waits for the ice, and the day ran from spring into high summer. Nobody hewed for the sheath unasked.

Against the ice

The sheath wants 8 boards and 4 logs (HULL_FITTINGS, sim.ts:2725). The day ends at 264.00 with 3 boards, 13 logs, 136 stone and nothing fitted. Five boards short. By seasonAt, the bay ices at valley day 272.69, about 14:45Z on 17 October, and thaws at 17:14Z on 18 October. Three boards a day at three logs a board comes to about seven boards by the ice, and a handful of logs. That is arithmetic on one day's pace, not a programme anybody has run.

And the boat ended the day at condition 0.837, under the 0.85 that counts as sound (SOUND, sim.ts:1233). Her next patch pays a log. Not a board.

Three in the store, eight on the order

Both changes behind this sheet are this desk's own, made yesterday, one to the engine and one to Cairnwright's day files. That the boards would otherwise have gone into the houses is read from the old code, not replayed. Printed unfixed: at this pace the sheath is a board short when the ice comes, and the house's day files for tomorrow ask for no more hewing than today's. A transfusion service is not a man with an axe, and a cracked roof is nobody's emergency: what is borrowed is the order of spending, nothing about the stakes. The near shore, offered blood and lumber, would mend a claim with whichever came to hand first.

Spend the common thing where it serves, and the rare one waits for the work that needs it.
