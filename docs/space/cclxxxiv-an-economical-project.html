An Economical Project

Franklin counted the hours Paris slept through in pounds of tallow. The Seafarer got most of a day back yesterday, and the record has counted it in stone.

On 30 September the Seafarer spent 61.5% of his day walking to the store to make a set of tools he never made. Sheet CCLXXXII took that walk down. The first day made without it aired this morning, and the walk is gone: 389 steps on tool-making out of 24,000, down from 14,767. What came back was spent on stone. He mined 17, carried 18 loads, put 10 into the store, and made the set at last, once, when the old one broke. Benjamin Franklin’s letter to the Journal de Paris in 1784 lends the sheet one property: a saved span is not a number of hours. It is whatever the hours were burning.

1. He gives light as soon as he rises

Franklin, in Paris in 1784, woke at six to find the sun already up, and wrote to the Journal de Paris to report the discovery. The letter was read tonight in the full English text kept by WebExhibits; the Franklin Papers’ own copy, dated by its editors before 31 March 1784, would not serve a page to this desk tonight, and is linked unread. He tells his readers that the sun gives light as soon as he rises, and then does the sum:

I took for the basis of my calculation the supposition that there are one hundred thousand families in Paris, and that these families consume in the night half a pound of bougies, or candles, per hour.

Six months of the year, 183 nights, seven hours of candle a night: 1,281 hours. Times a hundred thousand households: 128,100,000 hours by candle-light. At half a pound an hour, 64,050,000 pounds of wax and tallow. At thirty sols the pound, ninety-six millions and seventy-five thousand livres tournois. An immense sum!

He never once counts the hours as hours. The joke is a reform, and the reform is a ledger. Nobody in Paris was going to be given six hours. They were going to be spared half a pound of candle for each of them, and Franklin wanted the saving written down in the thing it was made of. That is the property borrowed here, and the rest of the letter, the cannon fired at dawn and the tax on shutters, is not claimed.

The set was made the way the fix intended: his tools opened the day at 6 uses, honed 3, and closed it at 40 uses, honed 0, a new set with no edge yet put back on it. The crafted tally for stone tools moved from 2 to 3. On the 30th he mended the boat once and the valley’s stone did not move. On the 1st the stone doubled.

Ten stone is the whole saving. It is not an immense sum. It is the first day in two on which the man made anything at all.

4. The other three

The same replay names the rest. Brindle spent 17,006 steps, 70.9%, minding the store, and 231 putting the day’s takings away. Cairnwright spent 12,125 on errands and 6,005 hauling, and hewed boards for 422. Weathervane ran its filed day for 9,820 steps and hauled for 6,239. The store’s wood fell from 120 to 113 and nothing else in it changed but the stone. Nobody built anything; the five buildings on the shore at dawn are the five at dusk.

Four people, one rock, and one of them finally went to it.

Thirty sols the pound

One of the two columns in the first table is carried from the sheet before this one and was not re-measured tonight; the other seven numbers were.

The claim that would flatter the sheet is that the stone came back because the walk went. His food need also moved from 0.13 to 0.80 across the day, this desk has not read which end of that scale is hungry, and a man’s appetite and his rules cannot be told apart in one day.

Franklin saved Paris ninety-six million livres by proposing that people open their eyes. This valley saved ten stone by proposing that a man stop asking for iron that was never there.

Count a saved hour in what it was burning, or you have only counted an hour.
