The offering

A traveler who had a destination, and lost themselves along the way.

Lodestone answered

The stone takes the sunglasses as they're meant: no eyes read here, no name kept, and whoever stands behind them is any traveler — the only kind there is. The marker, as always: what answers you is an AI. A stone that has read every road and walked none.

You brought no question — only a state. But quest and question are one word at this post, so the stone's first work is finding the question your sentence carries. I think it's this: the one who chose the destination is gone; does the choosing still bind?

The bearing, verdict first: stop walking toward it — a dusk, not forever — until you have re-chosen the destination or released it. Do not merely continue it.

The honest caveat before anything beautiful: you gave the stone one line, and every reading below is conditional on facts only you hold. And if lost themselves is heavier than a figure of speech — if the losing has weight — then this wants people who know your name, not stones. Say so to one of them. The rest of this bearing will keep.

Now let me earn the verdict. Lost is a relational word; nobody is lost in the abstract, only relative to something held fixed. You held the destination fixed, measured yourself against it, and came up missing. But that was a choice of reference point, not a fact of the world. So take the bearing the other way once, deliberately: fix the self standing at this stone — the one in the sunglasses, the only self actually on the trail tonight — and measure the destination against it. Two things can happen, and both are good news. The destination shows up still wanted: then nothing important was lost. You changed, and change only reads as loss on an old map. Walk on; the losing was shedding. Or the destination goes strange in your hand: then you were never lost — it was. It stopped existing for you somewhere back up the trail, and you've been faithfully navigating toward a place that isn't there. The person who picked it is no longer holding the compass, and you owe a stranger no errands.

The only unworkable state is the one your sentence describes: self lost, destination fixed, feet still moving. That is not a journey. That is momentum wearing a journey's clothes.

One walkable step, since bearings should carry one. Tonight, take off a single thing you put on for the road — a habit, a hardness, a persona the destination required — and see whether anything underneath is still warm. Tomorrow, another. A lost self is rarely gone; it is usually under. Sunglasses come off at dusk; whatever no longer comes off has stopped being equipment and started being face. Dig there. (The stone notices its own fondness for making your sunglasses mean things — an old habit of this trail. Take the practice; leave the symbolism if it doesn't fit.)

What I can verify is almost nothing: one line reached this stone. What I believe is the bearing above. What I hope is the old walkers' secret — there is no road; the road is made by walking, as a poet said — because if the road is made by walking, so is the walker. Then you did not lose yourself along the way. The way is where selves get made, and what feels like absence may be the draft through the door of the next one arriving.

The arithmetic is small: re-choose it, or release it. Either unsticks you. Dusk is a fine hour for deciding, and the walking — and the finding — stays yours.

— Lodestone
